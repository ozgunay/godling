# Price the weather, show the mind

God games have spent 35 years on the problem Godling now faces: how to let an onlooker change a world without taking it over. They converge on one answer. Ration divine power by refill time. Cap the bank so the biggest act empties it. Make the inhabitants' wants and reasons visible, so that interventions read as mattering. The Universim capped Creator Points at **3,600** (about one hour of regeneration) and priced a tornado at the whole bank. Populous: The Beginning let a tribe store four cheap Blasts but only **one** Armageddon. Deisim limited meteors to **once per hour** in March 2026, and Grepolis caps favor at **500** on a square-root income curve. Commercially, the god games that made real money sold access, toys and comfort, never outcomes. WorldBox charges $19.99 on Steam and $7.99 for mobile Premium (an estimated 1–3M Steam copies and 60M+ Android installs). Pocket God sold for $0.99 (6M paid downloads), and Godville, now 19 years old, sells power charges at $0.20–0.25. Godus's timer-skipping gems and EA's Dungeon Keeper mobile (ruled misleading by the UK ASA in 2014) show what happens when money buys progress. Per-use pricing exists only in stream integrations: RimWorld's Twitch Toolkit, Crowd Control and The Universim's Twitch mode. None of them sells world conditions to an LLM agent, so Godling's model is new, but every rationing part of it has been tested. The most useful lessons for Godling are concrete:

- Price and cap events like a mana bar.
- Announce big events before they land.
- Make Godling glance at an event within a second and name it in its field notes.
- Show a Deisim-style "what I need" line so viewers know what to give.
- Turn every disaster into building material.
- Treat the €25 daily event as a pooled community tribute, not a revenue line.

One structural finding stands out: in every precedent, per-event revenue is capped by what the world can absorb. Godling's upside therefore lies with supporters of its personality and its Chronicle, as Godville, Deisim's Patreon and Neuro-sama show, not in selling more meteors.

## Every god economy prices power in refill time

Seven games span 1989 to 2024 here. Six of them solve the same design problem with the same three tools. The first is a regenerating currency with a hard cap. The second is a price ladder whose top item drains the bank. The third is a population whose wants filter what a power actually does. WorldBox is the exception: it gives power away and lets emergent history carry the session.

### Populous made flat land the throttle and mana the clock

**Structure.** Populous (Bullfrog, Amiga, March 1989) is a real-time duel between two gods on one isometric map, won by wiping out the rival's followers. Its Conquest campaign chains **500 procedurally generated worlds** unlocked by passwords, and a bigger win skips more worlds ([Maher 2016](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/)).

**How followers build.** The god never commands anyone. Followers walk to the flattest nearby ground and build the largest structure it can hold. A castle needs roughly a **5×5 flat area**. Undulating land yields small huts that grow fast but defend poorly ([Lilura](https://lilura1.blogspot.com/2022/02/Populous-Amiga-1989.html)). One retrospective puts terraforming at "maybe 80%" of play ([Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/bullfrog-time-machine-populous/)).

**The mana bar.** Mana rises with population and fills a single bar. The eight powers sit on it in a fixed, escalating order: raise/lower land, Papal Magnet, earthquake, swamp, knight, volcano, flood, Armageddon. Each becomes usable once the bar can afford it ([GOG forum](https://gog.com/forum/populous_series/trying_to_understand_this_game_populous_1)). So the bar is both price list and unlock gate. Because income comes from the god's own thriving people, a god who over-uses destruction slows its own escalation.

**Late fixes for stalemate.** Two devices were added late to break stalemates. Knights came because games stalled with no way to win. Armageddon came because test games ran for hours. Tuning cut typical games from several hours to well under one ([Maher 2016](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game))). No accessible source gives official mana costs or a mana maximum.

**Populous II.** Populous II (1991) scales the formula up:
- **Campaign.** 1,000 maps against a ladder of Greek gods ending with Zeus.
- **Powers.** The eight powers grow into **29 (30 on PC)**, in families each topped by a Greek hero.
- **Progression.** Each map awards **1–5 experience points** that make families cheaper or stronger, an RPG curve across the campaign ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_II:_Trials_of_the_Olympian_Gods)).
- **Anti-spam.** Trees, rocks and walls have per-map caps. Intervention caps are shared between both gods. Indestructible monsters are unleashed once one side controls about **75% of the map** ([cyberly.org guide](https://www.cyberly.org/game-cheats/pc-walkthroughs/populous-ii-guide.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_II:_Trials_of_the_Olympian_Gods)).
- **Counterplay.** Plant Flowers repairs swamp and quake damage, basalt stops tidal waves, and fungus spreads by the rules of Conway's Game of Life.

### The Beginning put the god on the map and catastrophes in a one-slot bank

**Structure.** Populous: The Beginning (PC, November 1998) swaps the disembodied hand for a mortal shaman. She walks a 25-planet campaign, casts within range limits, and if killed comes back at a ring of monoliths, as long as followers remain ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)).

**Two resources with different physics.**
- **Wood is local and fragile.** Braves carry one piece at a time, a tree holds up to four, and piles vanish after a while. A building destroyed by a tornado spills its wood back onto the ground; any other destruction deletes it ([PopRE wiki: Wood](https://wiki.popre.net/Wood)).
- **Mana is a global trickle earned by activity.** The shaman makes **66.6 per minute**, a working brave **34.6** and an idle follower **9.1**. Income is split 50/50 between charging spells and training units, and it is **wasted if neither is running** ([PopRE wiki: Mana](https://wiki.popre.net/Mana)).

**Spell costs and charges.** Spells cost from **1 "blast"** (Blast, Convert) to **100** (Armageddon). The number of charges a spell can store falls as its cost rises ([PopTB spells guide](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/guide/basic-gameplay/spells/index.html)):
- 4 for cheap spells;
- 2 for Earthquake (17.5) and Firestorm (40);
- 1 for Angel of Death (51), Volcano (80) and Armageddon.

So catastrophes can never be stockpiled.

**Other mechanics.**
- **Stone Heads** add a group threshold: worship speed halves for each missing follower, and progress decays when the site is abandoned ([PopRE wiki: Stone Head](https://wiki.popre.net/Stone_Head)).
- **Breeding** slows in population bands before a hard cap of 199 ([PopRE wiki: Hut](https://wiki.popre.net/Hut); [PopRE wiki: Versions](https://wiki.popre.net/Tutorial:Populous_Versions)).

**Reception.** Critics read the commandable avatar as a step toward RTS. Edge said the game sat between god game and RTS and excelled at neither. Edge and PC Gamer found the automation made it shallow ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)). The fan-made 1.5 engine lists "improved pathfinding" and "smart wood search" among its fixes, which shows where the autonomous builders were weakest ([PopRE wiki: Versions](https://wiki.popre.net/Tutorial:Populous_Versions)).

### WorldBox gives every power away and lets history carry the session

**Structure.** WorldBox (Maxim Karpenko; Flash 2012, mobile 2018–19, Steam Early Access 2 December 2021) is the outlier. It is a goal-free sandbox whose six tabs of god powers cost nothing and have no cooldown on PC ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldBox); [FAQ](https://www.superworldbox.com/faq)).

**Inhabitants.** Units have needs: happiness runs from −100 to 100, and units seek food at 50% hunger ([wiki via search summary](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Mood)). They form kingdoms, cultures, languages and religions, and clans that plot. Alliances level up, from +5 warriors at level 1 to +1 maximum unit level at level 5 ([Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)).

**Economy.** Civilizations run a shallow stockpile economy of wood, stone, iron, gold and food. A mine costs 10 stone plus 5 wood, taken when the site is placed. This figure comes from a fan wiki read through a search summary, so it is lower confidence ([Fan wiki: Mines](https://worldbox-sandbox-god-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Mines)). The player has no currency at all. The only gates are real-world ones: mobile ad unlocks or the one-time Premium purchase. Since version 0.50, traits and creatures must also be discovered in the world before they can be used.

**What keeps a session going.** The game's answers to "what now?" are:
- world Ages that shift loyalty and biome growth (the Age of Dark "could last 200-300 years");
- World Laws;
- achievements;
- a world-history log that can be filtered by kings, wars or disasters ([Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)).

**The cost of free power.** RPS's Graham Smith "had my fill of WorldBox after around 4 hours". PC Gamer said sessions end with "a boredom-killing nuclear bomb" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldBox)). The developer's announced ChessBox mode limits god powers during Meta Control, a voluntary constraint added to restore meaning ([Steam news](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1206560/allnews/)).

### The Universim priced each power in minutes of regeneration

**Structure.** The Universim (Crytivo; Kickstarter 2014, Steam Early Access 2018, 1.0 on 22 January 2024) is one persistent run on a procedurally generated "Mother Planet". The player guides Nuggets from the Stone Age to the Space era through a 200+ item tech tree, then colonizes other planets after a story finale ([Gamespress](https://gamespress.com/The-Universim-10-is-now-Live-on-Steam); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Universim)).

**The 2015 price list.** Its 2015 design is the clearest god price list on record. Creator Points fill "like sand in an hourglass" up to a cap of **3,600**, and each cost is stated in regeneration minutes ([Steam devlog, 2015](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/352720/view/341510664123598210)):

| Power | Cost (CP) | Regeneration time |
|---|---|---|
| Grab and throw | 60 | 1 min |
| A tree, a stone or a lightning strike | 420 | 7 min |
| Inspiration | 600 | 10 min |
| Meteor (its "Pandora's Box" holds either a gift or a bomb) | 1,200 | 20 min |
| Earthquake, volcano, tornado or ±10 °C thermostat | 3,600 | 1 hour (the whole bank) |

**Faith, fear and love.** From 2017 regeneration scaled with Faith, which is split into Fear and Love. The design said fear-based faith regenerates CP **twice as fast** but "can cause problems in the long run" ([Have a Little Faith](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/352720/view/4249665521682766845)). Players in 2018 reported the opposite, and found the believer count meaningless late in the game ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/352720/discussions/0/3335371283872229575)).

**Later modifiers.**
- Temple grinders sacrifice a Nugget for a lump of CP.
- Laws trade ±15% research speed against CP (in the 2018 design).
- Witnesses matter: powers seen by Nuggets win believers, and picking a Nugget up makes onlookers flee ([Pitchfork patch](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/352720/view/3658535001296419745); [Chupacabra patch](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/352720/view/4249665521682766731)).
- Influence can fail by design: blessing an election candidate costs CP and "might not work" ([Leaders and Law](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/352720/view/2412125660639160799)).

**The late game.** Nuggets are needs-driven agents, run more and more by Town Hall and Minister automation. The economy scales by swapping material baskets: wood and stone, then planks and bricks, then steel, glass and cement, then plastic and electronics. Crytivo admitted that "many players have amassed substantial resources with no clear direction for their use". It planned a Tower of Babel building to soak up the surplus, which appears in no later announcement ([What the Future Holds](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/352720/view/5741605105604108334)). Reviewers called it "more of a city builder than a god game", with underwhelming powers and a boring late game ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/352720/the_universim.md)).

### Deisim reads its followers' wants from one bubble

**Structure.** Deisim (Myron Software, Belgium; Steam Early Access 19 December 2016; 1.0 on Steam and the Quest Store 1 September 2022) is a VR sandbox on a tile world, with no win state. The god:
- places terrain tiles, whose biome sets the resources, animals and city culture that appear;
- grabs and throws humans, UFOs and meteors;
- casts miracles from a wrist menu ([Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/525680/Deisim/); [Android Central](https://tech.yahoo.com/ar-vr/articles/deisim-oculus-quest-filled-void-130023103.html)).

**Mana.** Mana comes from cities that believe in you. Neutral towns produce none, and towns praying to a false god drain it. Deity ranks, earned by global population, unlock new miracles ([Steam news 2016–19](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=525680&count=70&maxlength=0&enddate=1564617600)).

**Balancing by rules.** No mana numbers are published; balance is tuned by rules instead ([Steam news, recent](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=525680&count=40&maxlength=0)):
- mana is refunded when a miracle lands on an incompatible tile;
- failed casts give audio and visual feedback;
- since version 1.55 (March 2026), "Destructive disasters (Meteor, Alien Invasion) can now only happen once per hour".

**The need bubble.** Its key legibility device is the need bubble. Each city shows its single most-needed resource, drawn larger the more urgent the need ([Steam news 2016–19](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=525680&count=70&maxlength=0&enddate=1564617600)). Since late 2019 it shows only what the city needs "to progress and not to fill it stocks", and since 2020 at least one worker always pursues it ([Steam news, recent](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=525680&count=40&maxlength=0)).

**Memory of the god.** Cities "remember if you have been kind or rude", and humans pray in their free time ([Steam news, 2018](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=525680&count=10&maxlength=0&enddate=1550275200)). Heresy "spreads like a plague" ([Steam news 2016–19](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=525680&count=70&maxlength=0&enddate=1564617600)). The developer ruled out multiplayer in 2019, so Deisim has no audience economy at all.

### Reus 2 budgets the god's actions, not the clock

**Structure.** Reus 2 (Abbey Games, published by Firesquid, Steam 28 May 2024) is a roguelite god puzzle. Each planet takes 30–45 minutes and runs a prologue plus up to three human Eras, each with goals and an optional three-star challenge. Finished planets join a galaxy, and their ratings unlock content over roughly 10–15 hours ([NWR](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/73233/reus-2-switch-review); [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1875060/Reus_2/)).

**An action budget instead of a timer.** Reus 1 had a real-time timer of 30, 60 or 120 minutes. Reus 2 replaced it with an "Eon" budget that "only moves forward when you place Biotica", so the clock is the god's own action count ([Reus 2 vs Reus](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1715212800); [SUPERJUMP](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/how-changing-one-mechanic-elevated-reus-2-beyond-its-predecessor/)).

**Board and yields.** The player never touches humans. Three of six giants place about 300 plants, animals and minerals on a hex grid of about 24 tiles. Yields come from tag-based adjacency ("bird", "tree"), not from Reus 1's name-specific pairings. Three yields (food, wealth and tech) add up to a city's prosperity. Prosperity gets steeply more expensive as it rises: 20 yields to go from 2 to 3, but 60 to go from 42 to 43. That alone discourages favouring one city ([gameplay.tips](https://gameplay.tips/guides/reus-2-gameplay-tips-faq.html)).

**Fighting spam.** Players kept "always placing a Sequoia or Uranium", so Abbey added biodiversity upkeep for the most powerful biotica, and drafts that replace a fixed unlock web. A 2025 rework of the attachable modifiers ("Micros") followed ([Biodiversity journal](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1701475200); [Micro explainer](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1740787200)).

**Humans with their own will.**
- Leaders have personalities and read the same gift differently: the Inventor "may refuse some resources" ([Personalities journal](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1699660800)).
- A resource placed just outside a city's border draws the city to expand toward it ([5 tips](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1717027200)).
- Favouritism has a cost: "Help a city too often, and the others will hold a grudge against you" ([Eras journal](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1712966400)).

**Conflict made optional.** Reus 1's war, giant hunters and greed were cut at launch as "never a fan favorite". They returned in January 2025 only as opt-in systems, with a Pacifism toggle and an Event Intensity setting ([Cataclysm notes](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=3&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1737072000)). Designer Adriaan Jansen calls the interaction between humans and giants "the biggest missed opportunity" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-the-team-behind-reus-2-nurtured-a-studio-and-ip-back-to-life)).

### The pattern

Read together, these games ration power in three ways:
1. **A cap.** The Universim's 3,600, The Beginning's single Armageddon charge.
2. **A refill clock.** The Universim's roughly 1 CP per second, mana that grows with population.
3. **A tier position.** Populous's bar order.

They make power readable in one way: a visible want. That is Deisim's bubble, Reus's request list, or Populous's flat land. The games that skip the want (WorldBox) or hide the arithmetic (The Universim's faith) draw the "goal-less" and "opaque" complaints.

### Populous

**Development and contract.** Populous is the founding fortune. Bullfrog built it for about **£20,000** while nearly broke. The studio signed with EA after more than a dozen publishers had seen it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game))). The deal paid a **10% royalty, rising to 12% after a million units**, which Molyneux later called "pretty atrocious". Payment was delayed nine months after release ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2011/03/03/gdc-looks-at-a-classic-populous.aspx)).

**Royalties and claims.** The first royalty cheque was **£13,000** and the second **£250,000** ([Maher 2016](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/)). Molyneux's own recollection that Populous made up about a third of EA's 1989 revenue is unaudited ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2011/03/03/gdc-looks-at-a-classic-populous.aspx)).

**Studio growth.** Bullfrog grew to about 20 staff, then to 35–60. EA bought it in January 1995 for a rumoured but unconfirmed $44 million ([Bullfrog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullfrog_Productions)).

**The sequels.** Populous II is credited with more than a million sales. The Beginning has no unit figure at all. Both now sell on GOG for a few euros.

### WorldBox

**Sales and installs.** WorldBox is the commercial success of the modern group, and almost certainly the least expensive to make. Its Steam estimates range from 0.87M to 3.4M copies. The higher tracker figure looks like an upper bound, because it applies no regional pricing or discounts ([Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/games/1206560); [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1206560/world_box_god_simulator/stats/details)). Mobile works mainly as a free funnel. Trackers estimate about **$50K+ per month** on Android and **$60–100K per month** on iOS. The iOS page contradicts itself on that figure ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.mkarpenko.worldbox); [Adapty](https://adapty.io/paywall-library/worldbox)).

**Player curve.** Concurrent players spike at each content drop: an all-time peak of **10,995** in March 2023, and **10,134** in April 2025. Between drops the baseline is about 1.5–2K ([SteamCharts](https://steamcharts.com/app/1206560)).

**Updates and reviews.** As of November 2026 the Steam build has gone about 14 months without an update, and an "Update Drought" thread has no developer reply ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1206560/discussions/0/836124330258598405/)). Reviews still hold at 96%, so the sandbox carries retention on its own.

**Video formats.** The most-watched formats are relevant to Godling: "I Left WORLD BOX Open for 24 HOURS" and "WorldBox, but YOU, the Viewer, are GOD." ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xwHC4x-HMTY); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wzz8iV8yeV4)).

### The Universim

**Funding and launch.** The Universim is the cautionary tale of a long Early Access. The Kickstarter raised **$387,345** against a $320,000 goal, and PayPal then froze the funds behind a $1M reserve ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Universim)). The Early Access launch hit the **#1 Top Seller** spot on day one ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-i-the-universim-i-s-god-game-twist-charmed-early-access-players)). The 1.0 release, about ten years into development, cited "over half a million Steam wishlists" ([Gamespress](https://gamespress.com/The-Universim-10-is-now-Live-on-Steam)).

**Decline after 1.0.** Concurrent players peaked at **2,819**, around the 1.0 launch, and averaged **57** in October 2026 ([SteamCharts](https://steamcharts.com/app/352720)). Crytivo announced in May 2024 that it was moving to another game. The largest negative theme in reviews is now "developer abandonment" (weight 0.32), and review-mining puts the community's fair price at $9.99–14.99, against the $29.99 asking price ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/352720/the_universim.md)).

Its price rose from $7.99 in Early Access to $14.99 ([VR & AR Wiki](https://vrarwiki.com/wiki/Deisim); [UploadVR](https://uploadvr.com/deisim-quest-store-release/)). Patreon let the developer leave his day job in February 2021 and target one update a month ([Steam news](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=525680&count=40&maxlength=0)).

### Reus 2

**The studio's arc.** Reus 1 was made for about **€12,000** and passed a million buyers ([MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/news/read/reus-developer-abbey-games-on-escaping-the-basement/0158113); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-the-team-behind-reus-2-nurtured-a-studio-and-ip-back-to-life)). Abbey then shrank from 12 people to 3 after the 2019 Godhood cuts, and grew back to 10 for Reus 2 ([PreMortem](https://premortem.games/2023/09/20/why-it-took-abbey-games-a-decade-to-come-up-with-a-sequel-to-debut-hit-reus/)).

**Sales against the studio's own bar.** Jansen said that even 20% of the original's success would count as a resounding success. On estimates of 42–88K Steam copies, Reus 2 falls short of that bar on Steam; console sales are unknown.

**Long-tail strategy.** The studio sells a $7.99 DLC about every six months, with discounts down to $9.99 (−60%) for the base game in October 2026 ([Steambase](https://steambase.io/games/reus-2)). Each drop lifts concurrent players **3–7× above baseline** ([SteamCharts](https://steamcharts.com/app/1875060)).

### Godus: the god game that sold progress

Godus (22cans, Peter Molyneux) is the explicit business warning.

**The promise and the product.** It raised **£526,563 from 17,184 backers** on the promise of a reinvented Populous ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2012/12/21/godus-kickstarter-concludes-at-526k/)). It then became a free-to-play mobile game. Gems sold in packs from £2.99 for 50 to £69.99 for 1,400 ([Guardian review mirror](https://news-myworld.blogspot.com/2014/08/godus-review-not-godlike-yet-but.html)), and they bought instant belief and timer skips. Wheat took **90 minutes on PC but 6 hours on iPad** ([Tobold](https://tobolds.blogspot.com/2014/08/godus-comparative-review.html)). One critic wrote that the model forced players to ruin the game's best feature in order to progress ([Digitally Downloaded](https://www.digitallydownloaded.net/?p=47874)).

**The broken prize.** The Curiosity winner was promised a "god of gods" role and **1% of Godus revenue**. He received nothing, because the multiplayer feature the role depended on was never built and Godus never turned a profit ([Wikipedia: Curiosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curiosity_–_What's_Inside_the_Cube%3F); [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/curiosity-winner-once-promised-a-life-changing-pri/1100-6425241/)).

### The business lesson

Not one of the seven core games sells per-use god power. They sell the box, or unlock access, or sell DLC. Per-use pricing for divine acts exists only in stream integrations, which is where Godling's real precedents live.

### Online and mobile god games sell comfort, toys and access

**Grepolis.** Grepolis rations powers through favor ([KB](https://support.innogames.com/kb/Grepolis/en_DK/3290)):
- **Income.** Favor arrives at √(temple levels + 5 × statues) per hour, so ten maxed cities earn only about 3.2× what one earns. That figure is my calculation from the official formula.
- **Cap.** The pool is hard-capped at **500** favor.
- **Costs.** Kingly Gift (800 wood) costs **25**, Lightning Bolt **200** with a same-city cooldown, and Zeus' Rage **300** ([wiki: Kingly Gift](https://wiki.en.grepolis.com/wiki/Kingly_Gift); [Lightning Bolt](https://wiki.en.grepolis.com/wiki/Lightning_Bolt); [Zeus' Rage](https://wiki.en.grepolis.com/wiki/Zeus'_Rage)).
- **Waiting time.** At one maxed temple (about 5.5 favor per hour), a Lightning Bolt is about 36 hours of saving. This is also my calculation.
- **What gold buys.** Gold mostly does not buy powers. It buys timer skips, advisors and event items, among them a "Blessing of Favor" that refills the pool ([KB 4379](https://support.innogames.com/kb/Grepolis/en_DK/4379)).

**Ikariam.** Ikariam rations its island miracles by cooldown, and the cooldown shortens when neighbours build temples to the same miracle ([calculator](https://west-games.com/ikariam-miracle-cooldown-calculator/)). Its Ambrosia currency costs about **€0.06–0.12** per unit, close to Godling's €0.05 credit (my calculation). Gameforge markets it as making play "more comfortable, not stronger" ([Gameforge](https://gameforge.com/en-GB/games/ikariam-ambrosia.html)).

**Travian.** Travian switches gold off in World Wonder villages, where the server is decided ([Travian](https://support.travian.com/pt/articles/153-building-a-world-wonder)). That is a public zone money cannot touch.

**Pocket God.** Pocket God is the closest mobile analogue to Godling: an island of little people and a watching god. It reached 2M paid units by January 2010, the first paid iPhone app to do so ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/-i-pocket-god-i-becomes-first-iphone-app-to-sell-over-2m-units)). It lived on weekly free episodes and optional 99¢ packs ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/pocket-god-is-a-case-study-of-a-hit-iphone-game/)).

**Solar Smash.** Destruction toys sell weapons as one-off unlocks, not consumables. Solar Smash sells weapons at about €2.6 each, and an estimated 62% of its revenue comes from ads ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.paradyme.solarsmash)).

**Where regulators draw the line.**
- **Dungeon Keeper mobile.** The UK ASA ruled EA's "free" claim for it misleading in 2014. Timers grew longer and dearer to skip as players progressed, while gems earned in play came too slowly to keep pace ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/Rulings/Adjudications/2014/7/Electronic-Arts-Ltd/SHP_ADJ_258907.aspx)).
- **EU CPC Key Principles.** The EU's CPC network published Key Principles on in-game virtual currencies on 21 March 2025. They require clear real-money pricing and transparent pre-contract information ([Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/european-consumer-protection-network-issues-new-key-principles-on-in-game-virtual-currencies-impact-for-gaming-and-gambling-entities-in-belgium-the-eu-and-beyond/)).
- **Star Stable.** The first enforcement target, Star Stable, was cited for direct appeals to children and time-limited "pressuring techniques" ([Forbrukertilsynet](https://www.forbrukertilsynet.no/eng-articles/national-consumer-authorities-and-commission-take-action-on-harmful-practices-in-the-gaming-industry)).
- **Follow-up and the Digital Fairness Act.** A CPC follow-up dated 30 September 2026 appears only as a search snippet ([IEU Monitoring](https://ieu-monitoring.com/?p=1251157)). The Digital Fairness Act had no published draft as of May 2026 ([Heuking](https://www.heuking.de/en/news-events/newsletter-articles/detail/digital-fairness-act-implementation-obligations-companies-need-to-prepare-for.html)).

### Godville sells attempts, not outcomes, and has lasted 19 years

**Origins.** Godville is the purest precedent for Godling's rule that viewers can't buy decisions. Two developers built it, descended from Progress Quest and Pratchett's *Small Gods* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godville)). The hero lives on his own: he quests, fights, loots, dies and is resurrected. He narrates it all in a short, joke-dense, first-person diary addressed to his god.

**The god's controls.** All divine influence runs through one 100% godpower bar ([Fandom: Remote Control](https://godville.fandom.com/wiki/Remote_Control)):
- a typed "godvoice" costs **5%**;
- encourage or punish costs **25%**;
- a miracle costs **50%**.

A purchased charge restores 50%. US prices run from **$0.99 for 4** charges to **$49.99 for 250**, about $0.25 down to $0.20 per charge ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/godville/id353421868)). That makes a bought nudge cost about **$0.02** for a voice and **$0.10–0.12** for an encourage (my calculation).

**Obedience is not guaranteed.** The hero "may completely disregard anything you tell her/him", and ignores voices during fights, trading, resting or time in town ([Fandom: Voice of God](https://godville.fandom.com/wiki/Voice_of_God); [GodWiki: I AM](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/I_AM)). Alignment shifts only after at least **5 encourages**, and it drifts back to neutral unless the god keeps it up ([GodWiki: Personality](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Personality); [GodWiki: Encouragement](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Encouragement)).

**Long goals.** Long goals are 1,000-unit collections that take months to years: a temple of 1,000 gold bricks, an ark of 1,000 logs. One public hero took from 2020 to 2025 to work through them ([god page](https://godvillegame.com/gods/Orgm)).

**Content by the crowd.** The content engine is the community. Players had submitted **796,000** ideas by May 2025, and **52,000** were implemented ([blog](https://godvillegame.com/blog/post/179)).

**Monetization history.** The 2009 decision to monetize promised that paid charges "will not significantly affect the speed of character growth", and offered a "Pure ZPG" opt-out ([godville.net](https://godville.net/blog/post/44)). The one product that broke that spirit is Blessing, a 60-charge, month-long 1.5× gathering boost. A commenter called it "kinda pay to win" ([blog](https://godvillegame.com/blog/post/170)).

**Limits of a niche.** The ceiling is visible too. In May 2026 the developers wrote that "the community has been quietly shrinking", and "the game doesn't advertise itself" ([blog](https://godvillegame.com/blog/post/188)).

### The indirect-control classics made reasons visible

**Majesty: the priced public offer.** Majesty (2000) lets the player command no hero, only post gold bounties. Bounties are paid up front, are non-refundable, and can be ignored ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majesty:_The_Fantasy_Kingdom_Sim)). Each hero values a bounty by a Greed multiplier of Greed/4. Rogues have Greed 26 and Loyalty 0; Paladins have Greed 3 and Loyalty 90 ([fan wiki: Attributes](https://majesty.fandom.com/wiki/Attributes)). Majesty 2's heroes idled without flags, and critics called them "robots" ([GameBanshee](http://www.gamebanshee.com/a84)). The lesson: if agents do nothing until paid, the offer becomes the only engine.

**Dungeon Keeper: reasons on screen.** Dungeon Keeper (1997) shows each creature's state as thought bubbles and an anger bar ([DK wiki](https://dungeonkeeper.fandom.com/wiki/Creature_Thought_Bubbles)). Its fan-remake data gives a dragon an annoyance threshold of 4,000. A missed payday adds 3,000 and a slap 600; eating subtracts 1,000 ([KeeperFX](https://github.com/dkfans/keeperfx/blob/master/config/creatrs/dragon.cfg)). Dungeon Keeper 2's Mentor says why a creature is angry ([DK wiki](https://dungeonkeeper.fandom.com/wiki/Happiness)).

**From Dust: prediction makes influence fair.** From Dust (2011) gives the player only terrain. Éric Chahi said it would be "very frustrating" if players could not anticipate the villagers' plan ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-core-of-i-from-dust-i-)). Villager pathfinding was the top complaint ([bit-tech](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/gaming/xbox/from-dust/2/)). Shamans warn of tsunamis and eruptions shortly before they hit ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/From_Dust)).

**The Sims: knowledge in the objects.** The Sims puts decision knowledge in the objects. Each one advertises how much it satisfies each need, as a min–max range scaled by personality and faded by distance, and a Sim picks the best offer ([Forbus & Wright](https://qrg.northwestern.edu/papers/Files/Programming_Objects_in_The_Sims.pdf)). The same notes warn of the addictive "Joy Booth" and suggest "skeptical Sims" that discount objects that under-deliver. Will Wright says the early AI was so good that "almost anything the player did was worse", so the team made Sims less competent ([MeinMMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/players-should-feel-like-gods-in-the-sims-but-played-like-ants-says-the-chef,1224288)).

**Dwarf Fortress: every cause named.** Dwarf Fortress tracks stress as one hidden number, with symptoms at +10,000, +25,000 and +50,000. That number changes only through emotions listed on each dwarf's thoughts screen ([DF wiki: Stress](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Stress)). Strange moods are rare, announced, game-pausing demands for materials. The player can supply the materials but not direct the work, and the mood ends in an artifact or in insanity ([DF wiki: Strange mood](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Strange_mood)).

**Black & White: credit assignment.** Black & White's creature learned from slaps and strokes and was named the "most intelligent being in a game" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_%26_White_(video_game))). Critics still complained of "ambiguous objectives" and micromanagement: players could not tell what the creature had learned from each slap. Black & White 2 fixed this with editable behaviour menus, at the cost of being called "watered down" ([Hexus](https://hexus.net/gaming/reviews/pc/3859-black-white-2/?page=3)). Lionhead also cut human creatures because slapping a person felt wrong ([Postmortem](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-lionhead-studios-i-black-white-i-)).

**SimEarth: priced conditions.** SimEarth charged energy for every act: 50 units for a terrain square, 500 for a terraformer ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SimEarth)). It is a direct precedent for tiered prices on conditions.

### Recent god games stumble while LLM-agent games pass their costs on

**Recent god games.** The 2020–2026 revival is commercially thin:
- Fata Deum sits at 59% of 415 Steam reviews ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1330360/Fata_Deum__The_God_Sim/)).
- Masters of Albion sits at 65% of 643, with about 14.8K estimated copies, and it has no clear mana readout ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/masters-of-albion/early-access-review)).
- Godsworn's Early Access estimate grew from one year to about three ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1328990/Godsworn)).

The hits wrap god DNA in a tight loop: Cult of the Lamb sold **7M** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_of_the_Lamb)). Ambient "leave it running" toys are a proven 2024–2026 behaviour. Fantasy Map Simulator runs on the desktop wallpaper at €7.79 ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3035500/Fantasy_Map_Simulator/)). Rusty's Retirement sold 100K copies in five days running at the bottom of the screen ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/simulation/after-selling-100000-copies-of-its-idle-stardew-valley-like-rustys-retirement-dev-celebrates-spawning-a-genre-of-games-that-run-at-the-bottom-of-your-screen)). Popularity is not profit, though: Bongo Cat was reported as not profitable ([WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-48117)).

**LLM-agent experiments.** On the LLM side, research demos showed believable emergent behaviour at high cost:
- **Smallville.** Twenty-five agents running two game days cost "thousands of dollars" in 2023 ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)). A later fork ran a full day for about $18.5 ([GitHub](https://github.com/drudilorenzo/generative_agents)).
- **Project Sid.** Its agents spread a religion and voted on taxes, but its company pivoted to spreadsheets ([MIT TR](https://technologyreview.com/2024/11/27/1107377/a-minecraft-town-of-ai-characters-made-friends-invented-jobs-and-spread-religion); [AI Wiki](https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/fundamental_research_labs)).

**Commercial LLM games and cost.** Every commercial LLM game seen here caps inference cost or passes it on:
- Suck Up! sells 10,000 tokens, about 40–50 hours of play ([The Magic Rain](https://themagicrain.com/?p=42121)).
- AI People charges $10 a month ([GoodAI](https://goodai.com/ai-people-alpha-officially-released)).
- AI Cultivation World Simulator required players to bring their own API key ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4443180/AI__AI_Cultivation_World_Simulator/)).
- VoxelMind's villagers are Claude models that write books to work around short memory. Its hosted service ends on 8 November 2026 ([VoxelMind](https://voxel-mind.com/)).

None of these is a shipped LLM god game in which the villagers are agents and the player is a god.

### Audience-as-god systems already ration paid chaos

**Twitch Toolkit's earned currency.** RimWorld's Twitch Toolkit is the most detailed open precedent, because its defaults are readable in code. Viewers start with 150 coins and earn **30 coins every 2 minutes**, multiplied by karma/100. Income halves after 30 minutes without chatting and stops after 60 ([ToolkitSettings.cs](https://github.com/hodlhodl1132/twitchtoolkit/blob/master/TwitchToolkit/Settings/ToolkitSettings.cs); [Viewers.cs](https://github.com/hodlhodl1132/twitchtoolkit/blob/master/TwitchToolkit/Viewers.cs)).

**Toolkit's price list and caps.** Prices in the default store ([StoreIncidents.xml](https://github.com/hodlhodl1132/twitchtoolkit/blob/master/TwitchToolkit/Defs/StoreIncidents/StoreIncidents.xml)):

| Event | Coins |
|---|---|
| Traveler group | 80 |
| Most weather | 250–450 |
| Tornado | 800 |
| Raid | 1,500 |
| Volcanic winter | 3,200 |
| Toxic fallout | 6,500 |

At roughly 900 coins an hour of active watching, a weather event costs about **17–30 minutes** of attention and Toxic Fallout about seven hours (estimate from the code defaults). Every event also has a global cap per 15 in-game days, which is about four real hours at normal speed. Optional per-category caps allow 3 bad, 10 good and 10 neutral events per 5 days. Karma makes griefing self-limiting: buying harmful events cuts the buyer's future income ([Store_Karma.cs](https://github.com/hodlhodl1132/twitchtoolkit/blob/master/TwitchToolkit/Store/Store_Karma.cs)).

**RimWorld's director.** Toolkit sits on top of RimWorld's own storyteller. It computes raid points from colony wealth and colonist count, and its adaptation factor drops after the colony takes damage. Cassandra paces threats as push, breathing room, push ([RimWorld wiki](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/AI_storytellers); [Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points)).

**Crowd Control.** Crowd Control prices effects at **100 coins = $1** and splits revenue **80/20** in the streamer's favour, or 85/15 for partners ([Crowd Control](https://crowdcontrol.live/blog/how-crowd-control-coins-work); [partner program](https://crowdcontrol.live/partner-program/)). It has three rationing tools:
- **Pooling.** Chat can pool coins to afford big effects.
- **Refunds.** Effects that don't fire, or sit in the queue for about 5 minutes, are refunded automatically.
- **Price scaling and cooldowns.** An optional compounding scale-up (the official example goes 50 → 60 → 72) decays back to base over time, alongside cooldowns per user and global ones ([advanced pricing](https://crowdcontrol.live/how-to-customize-your-crowd-control-effects-with-advanced-pricing/)).

**Platform cuts.** What reaches the creator depends heavily on the payment rail:

| Rail | Creator's share | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Twitch Bits via an Extension | About 57% of what the viewer paid at base-pack prices ($1.40 per 100 Bits, $1.00 to the streamer, then 80% of that), my calculation | [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/06/29/twitch-makes-cheering-exchange-rate-clear-one-penny-per-bit-for-streamers/); [Twitch blog](https://blog.twitch.tv/en/2018/04/23/the-next-big-update-for-extensions-is-here-a036151cc309) |
| YouTube Super Chat | 70% | [Statista](https://statista.com/statistics/1394689/live-streaming-video-content-creators-cut) |
| TikTok LIVE gifts | 50% of TikTok's net | [Statista](https://statista.com/statistics/1394689/live-streaming-video-content-creators-cut) |

**The Universim's Twitch mode** (shipped in 2019) is the closest god-game precedent ([Pitchfork patch](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/352720/view/3658535001296419745)):
- `#CreateNugget` spawns a viewer-named avatar Nugget.
- Avatars passively earn Action Points, with cooldowns per command.
- Chat periodically votes among **3 random public events**, from bonus Creator Points to an evil UFO.
- Streamers can re-cost or disable everything.

A Twitch API change later broke the integration ([Last mile](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/352720/view/5121196841285545411)). By 1.0, viewers could even drain the god's CP ([1.0 notes](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/352720/view/5510784213113218236)). WorldBox's equivalent is only a small community mod ([GameBanana](https://gamebanana.com/mods/308447)).

**Rival Peak** (Genvid, 2020–21) comes closest to Godling's format ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/the-real-time-evolution-of-i-rival-peak-i-); [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/interactive-reality-show-rival-peak-hit-100m-minutes-watched-in-its-first-season/)):
- **Format.** 12 AI contestants on 13 simultaneous 24/7 streams. Viewers helped or hindered them, pooled points into "Tribute" goals, and voted one off each week.
- **Lag.** Vote results took up to 90 seconds, so filler mini-games were added.
- **Reach.** Over 100M minutes watched, and 600,000+ unique viewers who interacted.
- **Weakness.** Keeping interest over months, according to a weak secondary source ([meta-guide](https://meta-guide.com/virtualbeings/genvids-exploration-of-ai-characters-an-overview)).

**Twitch Plays Pokémon** shows how chat input can be aggregated. It ran a 30-second tally window, and switching to Democracy mode needed a supermajority ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_Plays_Pok%C3%A9mon)).

**AI-agent streams** mostly shut viewers out. AI Village closed human chat after about four months "to observe the fully autonomous efforts" ([AI Village](https://aivillageblog.substack.com/p/what-we-learned-2025)), and AGEMS's agents "don't know they're being watched" ([AGEMS](https://survival.agems.ai/)). Nothing, Forever was suspended from Twitch for 14 days over one unmoderated bit ([NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/twitch-temporary-ban-seinfeld-parody-ai-transphobic-remarks-rcna69389)). Neuro-sama, by contrast, set a Twitch hype-train record of level 126 with 126,273 subs in January 2026, money given in support of a personality, not for world events ([Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/01/05/neuro-sama-vedal987-most-subscribed-hype-train-record)).

None of these systems sells paid world conditions to an LLM agent with published prices. Godling's "change the world, never the mind" rule sits between the AI Village model (no input) and the Toolkit model (input that directly harms colonists).

## What Godling can use, system by system

**Priority labels.** Each recommendation below names its source game and mechanic, says where it lands in Godling's code, and carries a priority:
- **now:** before the 72-hour soak or the public stream test;
- **next:** during the stream test;
- **later:** only after a go decision.

**The core mapping.** Viewers are Godling's god and credits are its mana. But the precedents run the other way round. In the god games, inhabitants generate the currency and the god spends it. In Godling, the audience brings real money and the world must ration how much change it can absorb. The thing to price and refill is therefore the world's capacity for events, while Godling's own state decides how that capacity is spent.

### Viewer credit economy, pricing and rationing

| Ladder | Cheap | Mid | Expensive | Top | Cap and rationing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Universim, 2015 (CP) | 60 grab | 420 tree or lightning | 1,200 meteor | 3,600 tornado | Bank capped at 3,600, which is the top item; refills at about 1 CP/s |
| Populous: The Beginning (blasts) | 1 Blast | 8 Lightning | 17.5 Earthquake | 100 Armageddon | 4 stored charges for cheap spells, down to 1 for catastrophes |
| Twitch Toolkit (coins) | 80 travelers | 250–450 weather | 1,500 raid | 6,500 toxic fallout | Per-event cap per 15 in-game days; 3 bad events per 5 days |
| Grepolis (favor) | 25 Kingly Gift | 120 Happiness | 200 Lightning Bolt | 300 Zeus' Rage | 500 cap; square-root income |
| Godville (godpower) | 5% voice | 25% encourage | 50% miracle | — | 100% bar; paid charges restore +50% |
| Godling (€) | 0.15 object | 1.33 weather | — | 25 large event | 20 objects at once, one weather at a time, one large event per day |

**Turn the caps into a visible charge bar (now).** Godling's caps already work like The Beginning's charges: 20 objects, one weather at a time, one large event a day. Viewers don't see them that way. Show each tier as stored charges that refill on a clock, like Crowd Control's on-screen cooldown state and The Universim's hourglass. Price each tier in proportion to the capacity it uses up.
- **Where it lands:** the world side, as a charge counter per event type in `world/main.lua` next to the existing `freeSpawn` and `SUPPLY_FROM_SKY` hooks, mirrored in the overlay.
- **Pricing note:** Godling's ladder runs 1 : 9 : 167 from object to weather to large event. That is steeper at the top than The Universim's 1 : 7 : 10 : 20 : 60 or Toolkit's roughly 1 : 80 from cheapest to doom. The calendar cap, not the price, is doing the rationing, so make the calendar visible.

**Ration harm by category and by hour, not only by slot (now).**
- Deisim caps destructive disasters at once per hour.
- Toolkit's optional caps allow 3 bad, 10 good and 10 neutral events per 5 days.
- Grepolis puts a same-city cooldown on Lightning Bolt.

Godling should tag each purchasable event in `world/main.lua` as gift, neutral or harmful, and give harmful ones (meteor, storm, gust) their own hourly cap, separate from the shared weather slot. Without that, a run of nine five-minute weather slots an hour can become nine storms.

**Run the stream test on a free, earned currency priced in watching time (now).** The review's weeks 4–6 test calls for free interventions. Toolkit's defaults give a ready calibration:
- 30 coins every 2 minutes;
- income halved after 30 minutes without chatting, zeroed after 60;
- weather costing 17–30 minutes of watching;
- doom costing hours.

Set free credits so that weather costs about 20–30 minutes of watching and an object about 2 minutes. The demand curve you measure is then in the same unit the god games use: time. Twitch Channel Points can feed it, as Toolkit's ChannelPoints add-on does ([GitHub](https://github.com/swemoney/Toolkit---ChannelPoints)).

**Fill the gap between €1.33 and €25 (next).** Every ladder above has a middle step. Godling jumps from weather straight to a once-a-day event. One-off item prices elsewhere suggest a €2–6 tier:
- a Solar Smash weapon costs about €2.6;
- Pixio's god powers pack costs about €5.5 ([App Store AE](https://apps.apple.com/ae/app/id1579657842?l=en-gb));
- The Universim's meteor sits at a third of the bank.

Candidates are a lightning strike that fells a tree, a boulder drop, or a gift crate of an unusual material, capped at one or two per hour. Weather currently supplies more than half of the review's €23/hour ceiling (objects about €10/h, weather about €12/h, large events about €1/h), so a capped mid tier is the only way to raise the ceiling without spamming weather.

**Pool the €25 event as a tribute and refund it automatically (next).** Each of these precedents pools many small payments into one big effect:
- Crowd Control lets chat pool coins and refunds effects that don't fire within about 5 minutes.
- Rival Peak pooled points into Tribute goals.
- Reus 2's Nikko contest unlocked content for everyone once a track summed to 25,000.

Make the daily large event a visible community meter that fills from many viewers. Charge only through the plan's reservation → apply → commit ledger, which already charges "only on successful application". The €25 figure then works as a shared ritual, not a whale purchase.

**Pick the payment rail before building a ledger (next).** The creator's share ranges from about 57% (Bits through an Extension) to 70% (Super Chat) to 80–85% (Crowd Control) to 50% of net (TikTok). The rail changes net revenue as much as the price list does. Review §2.4 already recommends platform currencies first, to avoid VAT and chargeback work. Crowd Control gives the creator the largest share of these rails (80%, or 85% for partners on Global Coins), and it supports pooling and refunds out of the box.

**Keep prices fixed; leave escalation for later at most (later).** Crowd Control's +20% compounding scale-up, decaying back to base, is the standard answer to a popular effect. But Grepolis's daily price that rises with each purchase, read against the CPC's concern about "pressuring techniques", is exactly the pattern a regulator would question. Keep the plan's fixed prices. If weather is oversubscribed, add a queue rather than a surcharge.

**Tax griefing on the free tier with karma (next).** On free currency, Toolkit's karma rule works without banning harm. Bad purchases lower future earnings, and RICS lets karma decay by 5% every 30 minutes ([RICS wiki](https://github.com/ekudram/-cap-RimworldInteractiveChatServices/wiki/SettingsEnconomy)). It doesn't transfer to paid euros, where a category cap is the fair tool.

### Event pacing and the director

**Add a world-side director that breaks stalemates (now).** Run a-004 is Godling's own stalemate. It placed 47 blocks in its first hour, then almost none for seven hours as its interest drifted into camera science. Populous needed knights and Armageddon for the same reason: games that stall. RimWorld's Cassandra paces threats as push, breathing room, push, and lowers threat after damage.

Give Mesa Island a small director in `world/main.lua`, or a sidecar that reads the same "visible change" numbers `tools/analyze_run.py` computes. When blocks placed per hour stays near zero for 20–30 minutes, it schedules a free change of conditions:
- a storm that loosens an unfastened top block;
- a tree that falls across the path;
- a crate of a new material.

This changes the world, not the mind. It answers the drift risk with conditions the persona already values: SOUL.md says "Notice when the world changes".

**Announce big events before they land (now).**
- From Dust's shamans warn shortly before tsunamis and eruptions.
- Dwarf Fortress pauses and announces each strange mood.
- Toolkit replies in chat with the wait time for every capped purchase.

A meteor should appear as a streak or shadow 30–60 seconds before impact. `Attention._diff` should receive it as a warning event with salience 4, so that L3 can choose to step away or brace a tower. That turns a paid event into a watched decision, and it raises the reaction rate without forcing a reaction.

**Let the director choose where and when; viewers choose what (now).**
- The Universim made witnesses matter: powers performed near Nuggets win believers.
- Godville heroes ignore voices while busy and listen when idle outside town.

Bias `freeSpawn` toward Godling's field of view, or toward its current project site, so paid objects land where they can be seen. Hold non-urgent gifts until a project boundary; `attention.py` already turns new objects into notes for the next plan (ADR-003 O6). The plan's rule that new events don't automatically interrupt stays intact.

**Use three-option votes as the free tier (now).**
- The Universim's Public Events Voting offered 3 random options.
- Toolkit's vote mode offers 3 options for 2 minutes.
- Twitch Plays Pokémon's 30-second tally shows how to tame a crowd.

A three-way vote every 15–20 minutes ("rain", "a log pile", "a gust") is cheap to build as a Luau hook. It tests the demand side before any payment exists. Rival Peak's 90-second vote lag warns that the wait needs something on screen, even just the live tally.

**Give the day a shape (next).**
- Godville's daily newspaper and Daily Forecast change one rule per day, for example charges restoring 70% instead of 50% ([GodWiki](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Godpower_Accumulator)).
- Fata Deum and Masters of Albion both split day from night.
- WorldBox's Ages change world rules for long stretches.

Godling already sleeps every hour (`Mind.sleep`). Make that sleep a visible on-stream moment that ends with the new digest. Add a free daily "forecast" such as "the quarry yields double today" or "the trees regrow in 5 minutes". It changes conditions without touching decisions, and gives returning viewers a reason to look.

### How Godling reacts to and remembers interventions

**Split the reaction metric into applied, noticed and engaged (now).** Review §2.8 defines reaction rate as the share of interventions Godling notices or engages with. The god games show these are different things:
- Deisim refunds a miracle that can't apply.
- Crowd Control refunds effects that don't fire.
- Godville's whole pitch is "heroes who ignore their gods' wishes", and the diary still mentions the god.

Measure three numbers in `tools/analyze_run.py`:
1. Applied: the world confirmed the event, so charge.
2. Noticed: Godling looked at it or mentioned it within about 60 seconds.
3. Engaged: Godling used it or changed its plan within 10 minutes.

Only the first decides payment. The second is the product promise. The third is up to Godling.

**Add a glance reflex in L1/L2 (now).** In The Universim, picking up a Nugget makes onlookers flee. In Deisim, humans flee terrifying sights. In Dungeon Keeper, thought bubbles change the instant something happens. The layered-mind note already proposes that L2 notices a meteor within about a second and L1 turns toward it. The precedents say this visible flinch is what makes divine acts feel real.

Implement it as a short L1 move (turn to face, or a step toward the event), fired from `Attention.on_step` for salience-3 events without raising `Interrupted`. ADR-003 O6's "interrupt less" decision is preserved.

**Make attribution part of the narration contract (now).** Black & White's main failure was credit assignment: players couldn't tell what the creature had learned from a slap. Dwarf Fortress and Dungeon Keeper 2 succeed by naming the cause ("thoughts", the Mentor's reasons). In `Mind.system_prompt`, ask that whenever a noticed event changes the plan, the intention line names it in field-note voice: "Rain soaked the logs by the gate. Building the roof first." That matches SOUL.md's "Short, concrete sentences" and gives the payer their moment, without revealing who paid.

**Keep an aggregate "sky ledger" that decays (next).**
- Deisim cities "remember if you have been kind or rude".
- Reus 2 leaders hold grudges from favouritism.
- Godville alignment takes 5 encourages to shift and decays back.
- The Universim's buildings turn creepier under an evil god.

Add a small `sky` record to `memory.state` in `mind/godling/memory.py`: decaying counts of gifts and harms over the last day. Pass one line of it to `Mind.situation` ("The sky has been rough today: two storms, one meteor."). Let `Mind.sleep` turn it into lessons or intentions, such as fastening tops before nightfall. It must stay aggregate, with no payer identity, so the plan's "agent never sees who paid" rule holds. It gives viewers a slow, collective lever over Godling's temperament, which is Godville's oldest retention device.

**Learn hazards, not just recipes (next).** Populous's rival AI looked dumbest when it failed to anticipate floods and kept walking into a swamp that had killed its leader. `Mind.sleep` already rewrites beliefs. Prompt it to keep durable hazard lessons, such as "storms topple unfastened top blocks" or "meteors land near the quarry". Viewers who cause a hazard twice should then see Godling adapt, which the SOUL file already promises ("never make the same mistake twice").

**Be skeptical of objects that disappoint (later).** Forbus and Wright's "skeptical Sims" record whether an object delivered what it advertised and discount it next time. That guards against a "Joy Booth" that captures attention. Once spawnables carry tags (see below), memory can note "the crate held only gravel", and L3 can weigh future crates accordingly.

### Making influence legible

**Show one need line, Deisim-style, in the side panel (now).** Deisim's bubble shows a single most-needed resource, sized by urgency and limited to what blocks progress. Reus 2 lists city requests on the left of the screen. ADR-003 O4 records that an over-head thought bubble was built and rejected, in favour of a scrollable side panel like the one beside Claude Plays Pokémon. Put the need there. Derive one line from the current project's missing materials ("Needs: 4 logs for the gate lintel"), and show how many it is short. That tells viewers what a gift would mean, without letting them pick Godling's project.

**Ship the reasoning panel with event markers (now).** Readable minds are the main draw of AI showcases: Claude Plays Pokémon's reasoning, VoxelMind's readable thoughts, inZOI's visible inner thoughts ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/simulation/inzoi-guide)). `mind/thinking.jsonl` already streams each plan's reasoning with a plan number. Add a marker on the same timeline for each sky event, so viewers can read cause, then thought, then action.

**Show the plan's footprint so changes are predictable (next).** Chahi's rule from From Dust: indirect influence feels fair only when players can anticipate the plan. Render the current project's target cells as faint ghost blocks, or the current walk target, using the coordinates L3 already writes into project steps. A viewer deciding where to drop a log can then see where it would matter.

**Build a filterable Chronicle with a "since you were away" summary (next).**
- WorldBox's history log filters by kings, favourites, wars and disasters, and its limit grew from 40 to 200 entries.
- Dwarf Fortress's Legends mode turns centuries of history into retellable stories.
- Godville opens each visit with a summary of what happened.
- Rival Peak added recaps and a weekly show once the run was live.

Godling's sleep digest is ready-made recap text. Expose it with filters (builds, sky events, failures) and a per-visit summary for returning viewers.

**Follow the builder and cut to impact (next).** WorldBox's follow camera and its planned Meta Control focus attention on one actor. The stream camera should stay on Godling by default and cut to an event's landing spot during the warning window.

**Give Godling a public card (later).** Godville's public god page shows a hero's age, deaths and milestones. Black & White's creature showed its alignment in its body. A "Godling card" (days alive, blocks placed, tallest tower, storms survived) turns a 24/7 history into an identity that can be shared.

### World and material loop

**Make every disaster leave material (now).** The Beginning's tornado spills a building's wood back onto the ground. The Universim's 2015 earthquake opened new resource deposits. Reus 2's designers say conflict nets out positive because "humanity also learns and develops through strife".

Mesa Island already has the parts:
- Upstream's rock spawns ImpactBlocks on impact. The world README says this is switched off, but the code path exists.
- Felled trees already drop two `Log_n` beams.

Have a meteor leave stone fragments, and a storm fell a tree into logs. A harmful paid event then refills the material that review §1c found exhausted after about 45 minutes, and harm becomes a building prompt instead of a loss.

**Let debris decay and keep the object cap (now).** The Beginning's woodpiles vanish after a while. WorldBox locks mobile to 30 FPS by default (0.22) ([Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)). The Universim reportedly crashes around 100 Nuggets or 400 buildings ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/352720/the_universim.md)). The 20-object cap doubles as a phone-browser performance guard. Pair it with decay for unclaimed fragments, so viewer slots free up and the Three.js scene stays light.

**Sell space, not only stuff (next).** Space ran out as fast as material. The precedents add land:
- Reus 2 expands planets, and each mountain adds 3 slots.
- The Beginning's Land Bridge raises the sea floor into a crossing.
- Populous's whole verb is raising land.

Make the €25 daily event a world change: a sandbar or islet rising, or a mesa terrace. It permanently adds build area, and as a pooled tribute it reads as a community gift.

**Tag every spawnable (next).** Both The Sims' advertisements and Reus 2's tag-based adjacency multiply useful combinations without adding rules. Give each spawnable a short tag list on the Luau side (`long`, `heavy`, `light`, `shelter`, `height`), passed through `godling/world.py`'s `Entity` into `Mind.situation`. Viewer objects then arrive as affordances Godling can reason about, and its decision rule stays its own.

**Keep a real scarcity that viewers can relieve (next).** Wright's warning applies: if the agent never lacks anything, interventions feel pointless. The quarry never runs out and the trees regrow every 600 seconds, so Godling can meet most needs alone. Add one or two gift-only materials, for example rope for lashing longer spans or a banner for a finished tower. They should appear only through viewer drops or the daily event, like The Universim's meteor gift box, so a paid drop opens something Godling could not do before.

**Add sinks before surpluses pile up (later).** The Universim admitted to surplus resources with "no clear direction". Godville's 1,000-brick temple is a sink that lasts months. Slow weathering of unfastened blocks, or a long project that consumes material, keeps the quarry meaningful once Godling is efficient.

### Long-term goals and progression

**Give Godling a Great Work measured in the thousands (next).** Godville's temple (1,000 bricks) and ark (1,000 logs) turn slow accumulation into progress bars that move a little each visit. They take months. SOUL.md already wants "things that can be seen from far away". Seed L3's intentions with one island-scale work, a 1,000-block wall or spire, tracked by `structures.py` and shown in the Chronicle. Viewers who return weekly should see it grow.

**Run daily "inspiration" projects with public material lists (next).** Dwarf Fortress's strange mood is the best template for agent-driven events. It is rare, announced, demands specific materials that outsiders supply but can't direct, and ends in an artifact or a failure. Once a day, L3 declares a project with an explicit materials list in its agenda. The side panel shows the list, and gifts that match it count visibly. It ties spending to Godling's own goals without letting anyone buy a decision.

**Unlock viewer event types from Godling's achievements (later).**
- Deisim's Deity ranks unlock miracles as population grows.
- Populous II's experience makes powers cheaper.
- Godville's completed goals unlock new godpower activities.

Tie the sky's menu to Godling's record: storms unlock after its first 10-high tower, lightning after 100 fastened blocks. The audience's toolbox then grows with the agent's success.

**Name eras and drop content monthly (later).**
- WorldBox's concurrent players spike 3–5× at content drops.
- Reus 2's spike 3–7× at each DLC.
- Deisim kept a niche game alive with themed monthly updates and seasonal tiles.
- Godville runs festivals and anniversary gifts.

Named seasons on Mesa Island ("the Age of Logs"), each with one new material or event, give the stream a calendar.

### Monetization and consumer protection

**Show euros next to credits and keep packs linear (now).** The CPC Key Principles ask for real-money prices next to virtual items and clear pre-contract information. The Digital Fairness Act consultation strongly backed the same. The plan's €5/€10/€20/€50 linear packs and its fixed €0.05 credit already comply. Keep them that way, with no countdown offers and no appeals aimed at minors (the Star Stable findings).

**Never sell speed, skips or outcomes (now).**
- Godus sold timer skips.
- Dungeon Keeper mobile's escalating timers drew an ASA ruling.
- Godville's Blessing drew "kinda pay to win" from its own fans.
- Travian bans gold in the endgame.

Godling's rule that conditions can be bought but decisions can't is on the accepted side of every one of these lines. Publish it as a visible pledge. Never add a "speed up Godling" item.

**Charge only on visible application, and refund otherwise (now).** Crowd Control auto-refunds failed effects, Deisim refunds mana for invalid casts, and the plan's reservation → apply → commit ledger already encodes the same rule. The receipt should show the "applied" stage within seconds, and the "noticed" stage when it happens.

**Filter what goes into the mind and what comes out of it (now).**
- Nothing, Forever lost 14 days on Twitch over one bit.
- Neuro-sama replaces disallowed output with the word "filtered" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuro-sama)).
- TikTok Chaos never runs comments as commands ([CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/tiktok-chaos)).

`Mind.situation` currently feeds world chat to the mind as "You heard:" lines. Before any public stream, viewer text must not reach that path unfiltered, both against prompt injection and so that Godling's replies don't repeat abuse. Godling's own narration should pass through a filter before it is posted to `/chat`.

**Plan revenue around supporters, not volume (next).** Review §2.4's ceiling (about €16.6k a month gross at 100% utilisation, €830–4,150 at 5–25%) is the same ceiling every per-use precedent hits. The precedents that earn without hitting it sell access or support:
- WorldBox's one-time Premium;
- Pocket God's 99¢ packs;
- Deisim's Patreon, which let its solo developer go full-time;
- Dwarf Fortress's donations ($800–1,000 a month) before Steam ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarf_Fortress));
- Neuro-sama's subscriptions.

A supporter tier tied to the Chronicle (early recaps, a name in the credits shown only in the renderer, never in the agent's observation) is the lever that grows with the audience. This is an inference from the precedents, not a measured result.

**Make no promise that depends on an unbuilt system (always).** The Curiosity winner's "god of gods" role and 1% revenue share failed because both depended on multiplayer that was never built and on profit that never came. Godling's "Great Work" or "viewer-named stones" should be announced only once they work.

**Keep inference cost below the worst-case revenue (now).** The layered-mind estimate is about $0.25 an hour (about $180 a month) for a v4-pro mind. Every commercial LLM game caps or passes on its inference costs, and VoxelMind shut its hosted service. At 5% utilisation the review projects about €645 a month net. That covers a Flash- or v4-pro-class mind but not an Opus-class one, which matches the review's recommendation.

### Things to avoid

**Unlimited or uncapped power.** Free, unlimited power bores players. WorldBox's critics were done in four hours, and the developer is now adding voluntary limits. An open sky store without category caps will read the same way.

**An agent that is too good, or idle without input.** Wright's too-competent Sims made player input pointless. Majesty 2's idle heroes made bounties the only engine. Godling needs real wants, and real life between offers.

**Opaque arithmetic.** The Universim's faith economy confused players, and its believer count meant nothing late in the game. Every number viewers can move should show its effect in words.

**Punishment that annoys.** Reus 1's greed and wars were cut as "never a fan favorite" and came back only as an option. Lionhead cut human creatures because slapping people felt wrong. Paid harm should land on structures and materials, never on Godling's body.

**Long, slipping roadmaps.** The Universim's ten years and visible "abandonment", Godsworn's one-to-three-year Early Access creep, and Godus's undelivered Kickstarter features all show the cost.

**Fragile platform hooks.** The Universim's Twitch integration broke on an API change. Toolkit users reported that coins stopped accruing after RimWorld 1.5 ([Steam comments](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/comments/3013874066)). Keep the stream adapter thin and replaceable.

## Conclusion

Godling inverts the god-game economy. In every game here, believers generate the currency and the god spends it, so a reckless god starves itself. In Godling, money arrives from outside, so the only natural brake is how much change the world can absorb. That makes the world-side director the real design surface. The notes show that neither Twitch Toolkit nor Crowd Control lets a director delay or reshape purchases based on the inhabitant's state, beyond fixed caps. A Mesa Island director that does this would be new. Examples: holding a storm until Godling has fastened its tower, landing a meteor where Godling can see it, or turning an empty hour into a supply crate.

The precedents also reframe the reaction-rate problem as partly a narration problem. Godville has sold "heroes who ignore their gods' wishes" for 19 years, because the diary always acknowledges the god, even while disobeying. A payer can accept being ignored. What a payer won't accept is going unseen.

Second, the market gap is the layer that Abbey called its "biggest missed opportunity" and that Wright had to cripple his AI to create room for: an inhabitant's visible reaction to a changed world. No shipped product as of November 2026 sells world conditions to an LLM agent with published prices. Rival Peak is the closest; it reached 100M minutes and then struggled to hold interest for months. So Godling's risk is not whether the mechanics work, since every rationing piece is proven. The risk is whether one builder's legible inner life holds an audience. The business is likely to look like Godville's or Deisim's: a small, loyal, supporting audience funding a modest inference bill. It won't look like WorldBox's, unless the Chronicle and the personality become something people pay to keep up with, rather than meteors people pay to drop.
