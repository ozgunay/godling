# Populous: The Beginning (Bullfrog / EA, 1998) — "Populous 3"

Source-quality note: the deepest mechanical numbers come from the fan community (wiki.popre.net, the archived PopTB.com guide hosted on ts.popre.net). Several of those numbers are stated to come from the game's `constant.dat` data file, and others were measured by players. They are not official. Items marked "(snippet)" were seen only in search-result summaries; the page itself was not fetched or could not be fetched (GameSpot, IGN, MobyGames and Rock Paper Shotgun all refused automated fetches). Treat those as lower confidence.

## 1. History and business: team, development, release, engine, sales, expansion, reception, re-releases, community

### Takeaway
Bullfrog built it after Molyneux left. It shipped on PC in November 1998, came to PlayStation in 1999–2000, and was the first Populous with a real-time 3D world: a torus-topology map drawn as a sphere. It reviewed well (GameRankings 80%) and won a 1999 Milia d'Or. No audited unit-sales figure exists. Since EA closed its official matchmaker on 8 January 2004, the fan-run Populous: Reincarnated has kept multiplayer alive and has released a community "1.5" engine version.

### Cited Findings
**Team and development**
- Developer: Bullfrog Productions; publisher: Electronic Arts. Producer: Stuart Whyte. Art lead: Phil McLaughlin. Character concepts: Mark Pitcher. Composer: Mark Knight (joined Bullfrog in 1997). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- Project/team leader: a Wikipedia-derived mirror names **Alan Wright** as project leader, saying the game set out to differ from earlier Populous titles and from games like Command & Conquer. A 1998 GameSpot preview names **"Andy Wright"** as team leader on *The Third Coming*. The first name is unresolved. — [LiquiSearch (Wikipedia mirror), snippet](https://www.liquisearch.com/populous_-_the_beginning/development); [GameSpot preview, snippet](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/populous-the-beginning-preview/1100-2560277/)
- Bullfrog representative Brian Allen argued that these departures set the game apart from other RTS games of the time. — [LiquiSearch, snippet](https://www.liquisearch.com/populous_-_the_beginning/development)
- Peter Molyneux had sold Bullfrog to EA in 1995 and left after *Dungeon Keeper* to found Lionhead, so he was not on this game. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- Working title *Populous: The Third Coming*, renamed before a fully playable beta in late 1998. Bullfrog waited more than four years after *Populous II* (1991) for hardware to support the new approach. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning); [PopTB.com history](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/populous-history/index.html)
- Announced at E3 1998 as one of 16 EA titles at the show. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- Cut feature: the series' "Plague" spell was dropped because it was too frustrating in practice. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)

**Engine and world**
- First series entry with fully 3D environments that can be scaled and rotated in real time. Characters stay 2D sprites. The camera rotates 360 degrees and can change height. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- The terrain's topology is a **torus**, projected onto a sphere so it looks like a planet: the map wraps on both axes. Textures are blended by altitude using masks. Some maps have no fog of war. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- Game-time clock: Angel of Death expires after "2500 turns (208.3 seconds at default speed)", which implies about 12 simulation turns per second. — [PopTB spells guide](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/guide/basic-gameplay/spells/index.html)

**Release dates and platforms**
- Windows, North America: 17 November 1998. PlayStation: North America 8 April 1999, Japan 10 June 1999, Europe 20 July 2000. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- GOG lists the release date as 30 November 1998. Wikipedia dates the official manual and reference card to the same day. This conflicts with Wikipedia's 17 November NA date and may be a different region or the street date. — [GOG](https://www.gog.com/en/game/populous_the_beginning); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)

**Price and sales (all claims, none audited)**

**Multiplayer and community**
- Up to 4 players over LAN, modem, or the Internet through an external matchmaking service. Bullfrog was closed by EA in 2001, and the official servers went offline in 2004. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- EA lobby era (1998–2004): EA leagues ranked by wins and game type, with free-for-alls worth more points than 2v2. Players could resync, change debug variables and speed up games, but "mana cheats were not possible". - Populous: Reincarnated (PopRE) grew out of TheGabber's custom-map website. Early maps were hex-edited until Hepple wrote a map editor. ALACN built the replacement Matchmaker: first a win32 app connected to EA, then a custom IRC backend. 1/2lb made the website. IncaWarrior later took over both. — [PopTB.com history](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/populous-history/index.html); [PopRE news 2008, snippet](https://popre.net/news.php?y=2008)
- Later additions include map packs, mods, skins and private messaging. An Android Matchmaker client (chat and PM) appeared on Google Play in 2014. A 2019 installer restored "Hardware mode" on new Windows. — [PopRE news, snippet](https://www.popre.net/news.php?y=2014); [PopRE Matchmaker wiki, snippet](https://wiki.popre.net/Matchmaker)
- Versions, per the PopRE wiki:
  - **1.03 "collective patch"**: builds on Bullfrog's official 1.01, fixes its bugs and a network-hosting bug, and needs no CD. Population cap 199, building cap 80, 800×600, 30 FPS, 4 tribes.
  - **1.5 (PopRE)**: unlimited population and buildings, up to 1080p, 60 FPS, 8 tribes, "improved pathfinding", "smart wood search", "no spell loss", Matchmaker multiplayer, runs on Linux via Wine.
  - **Multiverse (by "Toxicity")**: single-player only, modding support, campaign manager, auto-save.
  - The page gives no release dates.
  — [PopRE wiki: Populous Versions](https://wiki.popre.net/Tutorial:Populous_Versions)

### Inferences
- Wrapping the map on a torus drawn as a sphere removes map edges. Every point can be reached from every direction, and the "planet" look is a projection trick rather than true spherical geometry. That is a cheap way to make a small world feel like a globe.
- The community's 1.5 fixes for "smart wood search" and "improved pathfinding" suggest the original follower AI had real weaknesses in exactly the autonomous-builder behaviours that matter for Godling.

- GOG's exact launch date not found; 2011–2012 is plausible from the ©2012 line, but that is unconfirmed. Any EA Origin release or "On the House" giveaway is also unconfirmed.
- Full credits (lead designer, lead programmer, team size) not found: MobyGames returned 403. The Alan vs Andy Wright discrepancy remains open.
- No reachable 1998 developer interview, and no Retro Gamer, Eurogamer or Rock Paper Shotgun retrospective (RPS was unfetchable; Wikipedia cites RPS, Oliver Milne, 14 March 2017, for the community).
- PlayStation-specific review scores and differences not found.

## 2. Structure and loop: campaign, tribes, win conditions, the shaman as avatar, session length, multiplayer modes

### Takeaway
The single-player game is a linear 25-planet campaign. The player's tribe starts on the planet furthest from the sun and must eliminate the Dakini, Chumara and Matak on the way to the shaman's apotheosis. The god-game twist is that the "god" is now a mortal, killable, reincarnating unit on the map, the shaman, not a disembodied hand.

### Cited Findings
- 25 missions across a planetary system. The player starts on the planet furthest from the sun. The overall goal is to defeat every enemy in the system and ascend to godhood. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning); [GOG](https://www.gog.com/en/game/populous_the_beginning)
- Set before the first two Populous games. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- Tribes: blue (the player, unnamed), red Dakini, yellow Chumara, green Matak, plus neutral "wildmen" who can be converted. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- Win and lose: most missions require eliminating the enemy tribes, but some have other objectives. The player loses if the shaman dies with no followers left, dies with no reincarnation circle, or the timer runs out on timed levels. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- The shaman is weak in melee but has powerful magic. If killed, she returns to her Reincarnation Site (8 monoliths in a circle) after a fixed time, provided enough followers remain. — [PopTB followers guide](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/guide/getting-started/followers/index.html); [PopTB buildings guide](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/guide/getting-started/buildings-structures/index.html)
- The shaman is the single largest mana generator: 66.6 per minute, against 34.6 for a working brave. — [PopRE wiki: Mana](https://wiki.popre.net/Mana)
- Some campaign levels gate progress behind worship sites. Example: on "The Journey Begins" a Stone Head grants four shots of Lightning, and the level's Vault of Knowledge gives the Warrior Training Hut. — [PopRE wiki: The Journey Begins, snippet](https://wiki.popre.net/The_Journey_Begins)
- Multiplayer: up to 4 players. In the multiplayer opening phase called "forge world", Alt plus left or right click reshapes land at no mana cost. A known trick is to dig a hole in your reincarnation site and drop the 25 starting wildmen into it with 10 seconds of forge world left, so you keep all 25. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning); [PopTB miscellaneous](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/guide/basic-gameplay/miscellaneous/index.html)
- PC Gamer called the missions "mind-numbingly repetitive". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)

### Inferences
- Losing the shaman is a setback, not a defeat, as long as followers and the reincarnation site survive. The avatar is fragile but the tribe is the real "life bar", which keeps the avatar in play without making every death fatal.
- Because the shaman produces about twice a brave's mana, the avatar is also an economic asset. That gives the opponent a reason to hunt her and the player a reason to protect her.

### Gaps
- Typical mission length or session length: no published figure found.
- No list of all 25 level names with objectives was compiled. The wiki has per-level pages (for example "Solo" is level 22), but they were not fetched.
- The multiplayer mode list beyond FFA and 2v2 (seen in the EA league notes) was not found.

## 3. Mechanics: spells, how they are learned, charges, buildings, units, wildmen, reincarnation

### Takeaway
There are 26 spells. Each has a cost expressed in "blasts" of mana (Blast = 1, up to Armageddon = 100) and a maximum of 1–4 stored charges, which refill automatically from the mana pool. New spells and buildings come from worship sites: Stone Heads, Totems and Obelisks give single-use or limited-use rewards when enough followers pray, while Vaults of Knowledge, entered by the shaman, teach permanently. Buildings cost only wood; units are braves retrained with mana.

**Spell counts, starting spells and controls**
- Total is "26 spells", some one-use and some regenerating. The PopTB table above lists only 20, and I found no explanation for the difference. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- Blast and Convert are the default starting spells. When wildmen are present, the guide recommends charging only Convert at the start. — [cdplayer.popre.net strategy, snippet](https://cdplayer.popre.net/strategy/mana.htm); [PopTB spells guide](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/guide/basic-gameplay/spells/index.html)
- Charging controls: Ctrl+right-click toggles whether a spell charges, Shift+right-click toggles all, and Ctrl+click keeps a spell selected after casting. Casting locks the shaman in an animation for about 0.9 s. A cast made while she is dying loses the charge. — [PopTB spells guide](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/guide/basic-gameplay/spells/index.html)
- Community 1.5 advertises "no spell loss", which implies vanilla can lose spells or charges in some situations, such as dying mid-cast. — [PopRE wiki: Versions](https://wiki.popre.net/Tutorial:Populous_Versions)

**How spells and buildings are learned**
- Followers worshipping at totems unlock spells for a limited number of uses. The shaman learns spells permanently by entering vaults. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- Stone Heads, as described on the PopRE wiki:
  - Each head shows a required worshipper count (the example is 5). Speed is 100% when at least that many followers pray, and each follower short of the requirement halves it.
  - Progress decays when nobody prays and holds steady with at least one worshipper. Extra worshippers beyond the requirement add nothing.
  - Rewards are single-use but stack: a spell is castable once per reward, and a building can be built once. Followers keep praying until told to stop.
  - A drained head sinks into the ground. Worship items cannot be targeted by damaging spells or sunk.
  — [PopRE wiki: Stone Head](https://wiki.popre.net/Stone_Head)
- Obelisks have no fixed reward and can trigger anything from a land change to a high-level spell. On level 22 "Solo", worshipping the obelisk fully refills your spells. — [PopRE wiki: Obelisk, snippet](https://wiki.popre.net/Obelisk); [PopRE wiki: Solo, snippet](https://wiki.popre.net/Solo)
- Vault of Knowledge: the shaman enters and takes an enemy shaman's knowledge, which she keeps permanently. A symbol on top shows what it holds. Giant Bomb says most spells are unlocked this way. — [PopRE wiki: Vault of Knowledge, snippet](https://wiki.popre.net/Vault_of_Knowledge); [Giant Bomb, snippet](https://www.giantbomb.com/games/3030-16988/)
- Idol varieties are totems, vaults and stone heads. Some grant their wisdom only when the shaman herself worships. — [PopTB buildings guide](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/guide/getting-started/buildings-structures/index.html)

### Inferences
- Costs span two orders of magnitude, from 1 to 100 blasts, and charge caps shrink as cost rises: cheap spells store 4, mid spells 2–3, catastrophes 1. Disasters can never be stockpiled. Even a huge economy gets at most one Volcano or Armageddon "in the bank" at a time.
- The learning system has two tiers. Communal worship (Stone Heads) yields consumable rewards that need several followers present and decay when abandoned, while the avatar's personal act of entering a Vault grants permanent knowledge.

## 4. Mana and resource economy: generation, wood, costs, population, charge scaling

### Takeaway
There are two resources with separate jobs. **Wood** is physical: braves chop it one piece at a time, carry it, pile it and lose it to fire or water, and it pays only for buildings. **Mana** is abstract: it trickles from every living follower, with busy braves and the shaman producing most. It is split automatically 50/50 between spell charging and unit training, and is wasted if neither is active. Population grows from huts, with a soft brake that slows breeding as the tribe gets larger, and a hard cap of 199 in vanilla 1.03.

### Cited Findings
**Mana generation**
- Rates in mana per minute, measured by the community:

  | Source | Mana/min |
  |---|---|
  | Shaman | 66.6 |
  | Brave in a hut or working | 34.6 |
  | Warrior, Firewarrior, Preacher, Spy while moving | 11.3 |
  | Any follower idle | 9.1 |
  | Angels of Death, ghosts | 0 |

  Stone Heads can also reward mana. Worked example: shaman + 7 working braves + 2 + 2 other units ≈ 354 mana per minute. — [PopRE wiki: Mana](https://wiki.popre.net/Mana)
- "Braves generate the same mana whether they are in large huts, medium huts, small huts or when moving". The wiki warns never to keep braves idle. — [PopRE wiki: Brave](https://wiki.popre.net/Brave)
- A tutorial speech file is titled "When placed in huts braves generate more mana". — [PopRE wiki: Hut](https://wiki.popre.net/Hut)
- A unit that cannot reach its ordered destination raises its hands and loses health until it dies, yet shows maximum mana generation while "idle" like this. — [PopTB miscellaneous](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/guide/basic-gameplay/miscellaneous/index.html)
- Spell regeneration speed depends on the number of followers. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)

**Starting mana and allocation**
- Starting mana is **300**. A fan guide says this is enough for about 1.5 charges each of Blast and Convert, which implies 1 Blast ≈ 100 displayed mana. That is consistent with the internal 10,000 ÷ 100. — [PopRE wiki: Mana](https://wiki.popre.net/Mana); [cdplayer.popre.net, snippet](https://cdplayer.popre.net/strategy/mana.htm)
- How mana is allocated:
  - When spells and training are both active, mana is split 50/50.
  - If only one is active, it gets all of it. If neither is active, the mana is **wasted**.
  - The spell share is divided equally among the spells set to charge, and the training share equally among active training huts.
  - Charging only Hypnotise with training stopped makes it charge "six times as fast". Stopping all spell charging makes training "twice as fast".
  — [PopRE wiki: Mana](https://wiki.popre.net/Mana)
- Charging one spell at a time is "theoretically more effective". — [PopTB spells guide](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/guide/basic-gameplay/spells/index.html)

**Wood**
- Gathering and carrying:
  - Only braves carry wood, one pile at a time.
  - "Up to four wood can grow on Trees". Scenario-placed "super trees" can hold more.
  - Trees regrow quickly, but a fully chopped tree takes much longer to come back.
  — [PopRE wiki: Wood](https://wiki.popre.net/Wood); [PopTB buildings guide](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/guide/getting-started/buildings-structures/index.html)
- Woodpiles:
  - Wood can be piled anywhere except on a tree, and hovering shows the count.
  - Piles form when braves bring wood to huts for upgrades, and they vanish "after a while".
  - Bigger piles burn with bigger flames, and a glitch makes piles immune to lava.
  — [PopRE wiki: Wood](https://wiki.popre.net/Wood)
- Losing wood:
  - A building destroyed by a **tornado spills its wood onto the ground**.
  - Destruction by any other spell or unit, or sinking in water, removes the wood from the game.
  - Deleting a building plan after the first delivery, but before construction starts, also loses that wood.
  — [PopRE wiki: Wood](https://wiki.popre.net/Wood)
- Wood is "the only material needed for construction". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)

**Huts, breeding and population**
- Upgrades:
  - A hut can upgrade twice, from small to medium to large.
  - Upgrades are triggered by wood placed at the hut's entrance.
  - Manually ordering braves to drop wood at the door is the fastest route for small huts. A tree right next to the door blocks manual upgrade.
  — [PopRE wiki: Hut](https://wiki.popre.net/Hut); [PopTB buildings guide](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/guide/getting-started/buildings-structures/index.html)
- A guide recommends 3 braves inside plus 3 wood at the entrance as the fastest upgrade. Sources conflict on how much is consumed: one says only 2 of the 3 pieces are used. The wiki says piling more wood at the door does not speed the red bar; it mainly saves search time. — [popms.net advanced building, snippet](https://ts.popre.net/websites/popms.net/en/tips/advanced-tips/53-advanced-building.html); [PopRE wiki: Upgrading, snippet](https://wiki.popre.net/Upgrading)
- Breeding (from `constant.dat`, via the wiki):
  - Base breeding time: L1 4000, L2 3000, L3 2000, so a level-3 hut breeds twice as fast as level 1.
  - Occupant multiplier: 0 → 0.5, 1 → 1.0, 2 → 1.5, 3 → 2.0. Empty huts still breed, and the shaman or a trained unit counts as an occupant.
  - "Breeding units" needed per new follower rise in population bands: pop 0–4 → 30, 5–9 → 35, 10–14 → 40, and so on. Every hut slows as the tribe grows, separately from the hard cap.
  — [PopRE wiki: Hut](https://wiki.popre.net/Hut)
- Dismantle trick: dismantling and rebuilding a hut produces a brave immediately but resets the hut to the start of its current upgrade stage. — [PopRE wiki: Dismantle, snippet](https://wiki.popre.net/Dismantle)
- Hard caps in vanilla 1.03: population **199**, buildings **80**. Community 1.5 makes both unlimited. — [PopRE wiki: Versions](https://wiki.popre.net/Tutorial:Populous_Versions)
- Huts are "needed for population growth, mana generation, and expanding buildable territory". — [PopTB buildings guide](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/guide/getting-started/buildings-structures/index.html)

### Inferences
- Charge scaling, assuming the community rates are in displayed mana (1 Blast ≈ 100):
  - The 354/min example tribe, with half its mana going to spells, earns about 1.8 Blasts' worth per minute.
  - At that rate, one Firestorm (40 blasts) takes about 22 minutes and Armageddon (100) about 56 minutes, unless training is paused and other spells are toggled off.
  - Big spells therefore effectively need large populations or long saving. This is an inference with unverified units, so treat the minute figures as orders of magnitude.
- "Wasted if neither is active" means mana is use-it-or-lose-it, not a bank. Full spell slots plus idle training huts throw income away, which pushes players to always queue something.
- Wood stays local and fragile: a piece in transit, a pile at a door, or a building that burns. Mana is global and safe. The terrain and disaster spells therefore attack the wood side directly (burn, sink, spill), while the mana side can only be hurt by killing followers.
- The population-band rule is a built-in soft cap, giving diminishing returns on growth before the 199 hard cap is reached.

### Gaps
- Official manual figures were not obtained. The archive.org and GameFAQs manual could not be checked within budget, so all numbers here are community-derived.
- Whether the mana-per-minute values are displayed or internal units is not stated explicitly.
- How mana scales with hut level beyond "same whether large, medium or small" is unresolved. It conflicts slightly with the tutorial line "in huts braves generate more mana"; the likely reading is that huts merely count as "working".
- Breeding-unit bands above a population of 14, and how breeding time units convert to seconds, were not found.
- Tree regrowth timing in seconds, wood per chop, and how long woodpiles last were not found.

## 5. Follower AI: autonomous gathering and building, player micromanagement, opponent AI

### Takeaway
Followers handle routine life themselves: they breed at huts, fetch wood, build whatever plans the player places, upgrade and repair huts, and occupy them. The player decides what is built and where, all combat, spell use and training. Reviewers found the automation convenient but hard to steer in chaotic fights, and some felt it made the game shallow. Competitive players still micromanage wood placement to speed things up.

### Cited Findings
- Followers are created automatically at houses. The player directs them by ordering construction or attacks. Automation handles daily tasks, but combat orders are the player's. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- GOG: you "order individuals or groups to gather resources, build, or attack". — [GOG](https://www.gog.com/en/game/populous_the_beginning)
- Braves build, repair and upgrade structures, collect wood and generate the most mana. — [PopRE wiki: Brave](https://wiki.popre.net/Brave)
- Building is plan-based: the player places a building plan, braves deliver wood to it, and construction then starts. Deleting a plan after the first delivery wastes that wood. — [PopRE wiki: Wood](https://wiki.popre.net/Wood)
- For fast building, players "must carefully micro manage" follower behaviour. — [PopTB followers guide](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/guide/getting-started/followers/index.html)
- Hut upgrades happen automatically when wood arrives at the door, but manual wood ferrying is faster. — [PopRE wiki: Hut](https://wiki.popre.net/Hut); [PopRE wiki: Upgrading, snippet](https://wiki.popre.net/Upgrading)
- Pathing failure mode: a follower sent to an unreachable spot raises its hands and loses health until it dies unless moved. — [PopTB miscellaneous](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/guide/basic-gameplay/miscellaneous/index.html)
- Community 1.5 adds "improved pathfinding" and "smart wood search", which implies these were weaknesses of the original. — [PopRE wiki: Versions](https://wiki.popre.net/Tutorial:Populous_Versions)
- Wildmen are not followers. They eat, drink and "scamper about" until converted. — [PopTB followers guide](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/guide/getting-started/followers/index.html)
- Followers keep praying at a Stone Head after its reward is earned until told to stop. — [PopRE wiki: Stone Head](https://wiki.popre.net/Stone_Head)
- Reviews on control: GameSpot (Ron Dulin) found automatic construction convenient but a problem when directing units in chaotic battles. Reviewers generally found followers hard to control in intense fights. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- Reviews on depth: Edge and PC Gamer felt automation made the game too simple, with no upgrades or meaningful resource management. IGN said automated follower behaviour reduced replay value even with multiplayer. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)

### Inferences
- The split of authority is clear. The god (player) chooses plans, placement, targets and spells. The tribe executes logistics: fetching wood, construction, breeding, worship. That maps closely onto "viewers set conditions, the agent does the work", except that in PopTB the player also issues plans, which Godling viewers would not.
- Naive autonomous behaviours, such as wood search and pathing to unreachable goals, were the visible failure points that both critics and the community later addressed. That is the area where an autonomous builder most needs robustness.

### Gaps
- Opponent AI behaviour (how enemy shamans attack, build or prioritise; difficulty levels; scripted vs adaptive) was not found in any fetched source.
- Exactly when braves self-assign versus waiting for orders (do idle braves auto-gather or auto-enter huts?) is not documented in the fetched pages. Manual and in-game guide text would be needed.

## 6. Reception: praise, criticism, and comparison with Populous I/II indirect control

### Takeaway
Critics praised the 3D world and the bold reinvention. The common criticism was that it fell between a god game and an RTS: the hands-off, terrain-sculpting indirect control of Populous I/II was replaced by a commandable avatar plus semi-automated units, and that felt simpler and more repetitive.

### Cited Findings
- Praise:
  - CVG called it one of the year's best titles.
  - IGN (Ward Trent) was impressed by the 3D graphics.
  - GameSpot (Ron Dulin) valued it as a bold revamp rather than a safe sequel.
  — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- Edge argued that graphics now came ahead of gameplay, and that the game sat between god game and RTS and excelled at neither. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- GamePro (Peter Olafson) called it good but different, "without a quintessential quality that defined Populous". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- PC Gamer called the missions "mind-numbingly repetitive". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- In the old games' terms: Wikipedia sums up the economy as "no formal resource management": mana only for units and wood only for buildings. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning)
- Bullfrog's stated aim (project leader, via the mirror) was to depart from earlier Populous titles and from C&C-style RTS. — [LiquiSearch, snippet](https://www.liquisearch.com/populous_-_the_beginning/development)

### Inferences
- In Populous I/II the god acted only by shaping land and casting from above, while followers settled flat ground on their own. PopTB keeps the terrain spells (Flatten, Land Bridge, Earthquake, Volcano) but routes all power through a mortal avatar on the map. Divine power becomes positional: range limits of 3072–8192 units, towers that extend casting radius. It also becomes vulnerable to assassination. This is the design shift critics read as "more RTS".

### Gaps
- Original 1998 review texts (GameSpot, IGN, PC Gamer) could not be fetched directly; the statements above are as summarised by Wikipedia.
- No retrospective found (Retro Gamer, Eurogamer, RPS 2017).

### Notable for Godling
- **Avatar plus autonomous tribe.** The shaman is the only "hand" and can die. The tribe survives her, and she returns after a fixed delay at a reincarnation site if followers remain. A resilient in-world agent with a respawn rule that depends on world state is a proven model.
- **Two resources with different physics.** Wood is spatial and fragile: carried one at a time, piled at doors, burned or sunk, and spilled back onto the ground by tornadoes. Mana is a global trickle earned by activity: busy braves earn about 3.8× idle ones. Viewer-triggered disasters can then act on the physical resource without touching "decisions".
- **Charges with caps scaled to cost.** Spells have 1–4 stored charges, cheap ones store 4, catastrophes store only 1, and recharge comes automatically from a shared pool split equally among the spells set to charge. Income is wasted when nothing is charging. This is a ready template for pacing viewer-bought events, such as a meteor capped at 1 charge.
- **Terrain spells change what can be built.** Flatten makes buildable ground, Land Bridge opens new land, Earthquake and Volcano reshape it and damage buildings, and huts extend buildable territory. In PopTB, terrain is the lever between god and builders.
- **Communal worship sites** (Stone Heads) need N participants. Speed halves per missing worshipper, progress decays when abandoned, rewards are single-use but stack, and the head sinks when drained. This is a well-tuned group-threshold mechanic.
- **Diminishing population growth.** Breeding cost rises in population bands and occupied, upgraded huts breed much faster. My arithmetic from the wiki's constants: base time 2000 vs 4000 and multiplier 2.0 vs 0.5 make an L3 hut with 3 occupants about 8× faster than an empty L1 hut. The wiki marks the multiplier as "still to confirm". A hard cap of 199 sits on top.
- **Known autonomy failure modes to avoid.** Units stuck on unreachable goals drained health and died; wood search and pathfinding were weak enough that the community patched them in 1.5; and critics found automated followers hard to steer in chaos and repetitive over time.
- **Pre-game free sculpting** ("forge world": free land edits for a set period at match start) is a precedent for a no-cost setup phase before the economy switches on.
