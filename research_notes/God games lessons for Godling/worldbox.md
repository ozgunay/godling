## 1. History and business: team, timeline, platforms, pricing, monetization, sales, reviews, community, modding, video popularity

### Takeaway
WorldBox started as a solo project by Russian developer Maxim Karpenko: a Flash prototype in 2011 and a Newgrounds release in 2012. It grew into a Unity game with mobile, PC, Mac and Linux versions. Mobile is free-to-play, with ads that unlock powers for a time and a one-time $7.99 Premium unlock, and has passed 60M Android installs. On PC it costs $19.99 on Steam, has been in Early Access since 2 Dec 2021 (still there in Nov 2026, almost five years later), and estimates put it at roughly 1–3M copies. Reviews are 96% positive. As of 2026-11-01 the Steam build has had no update for about 14 months. The next big update ("Meta Control and ChessBox", reported as about 85% done in June 2026) has not shipped.

### Inferences
- Steam is probably the main revenue source. About 1.2–3M copies at a $19.99 list price implies roughly $14M–$60M lifetime gross (third-party estimates). Mobile tracker estimates of about $50–100K/month per store come to roughly $1–2M/year across both stores. The huge mobile install base (60M+ Android) works mainly as a free funnel and awareness channel.
- The concurrent-player spikes (Mar 2023, Apr 2025, Sep 2025) line up with major content drops. Between drops the baseline is steady at about 1.3K–2K concurrent players. The long-tail audience stays without new content, but content drives the spikes.
- The update cadence is lumpy: 0.21 to 0.50 took about 2 years, and as of 2026-11-01 Steam has gone about 14 months without a patch. The "Update Drought of '26" thread shows community frustration, yet review scores stayed at 96%, so the sandbox itself carries retention.
- The mobile "watch an ad to unlock a power" model ties monetization to the powers themselves, not to resources. There is never an in-world currency for the player.

## 2. Structure and core loop: goals, modes, session shape, world generation and size, time controls

### Takeaway
WorldBox has no win condition. It is a pure sandbox: you generate or paint a world, seed life, watch civilizations self-organize, and intervene. Sessions usually build up and then end in spectacular destruction. Long runs are driven by watching history unfold, helped by time controls, world "Ages", World Laws, achievements and (from 0.50) unit possession.

### Cited Findings
- Rock Paper Shotgun headline: "WorldBox is a god sim too smart to give you goals" (Graham Smith, 8 Jan 2022). — [Wikipedia citing RPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldBox)
- RPS's Graham Smith said he had "had my fill of WorldBox after around 4 hours, but it was a happy four hours." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldBox)
- PC Gamer (Joseph Knoop, 25 Aug 2021) said sessions often end with "a boredom-killing nuclear bomb". Its headline: "This new god game has an actual Thanos snap button." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldBox); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/super-worldbox-impressions/)
- Steam features: god powers, living creatures with needs, four civilized races (humans, orcs, elves, dwarves), kingdoms, wars, diplomacy, procedural world generation, a level editor, and Workshop map sharing. — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1206560/WorldBox__God_Simulator/)
- 0.21 added more world template options at generation. — [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)
- Main control tools include pause, an hourglass (time speed), world statistics, Eye of Insight, and World Laws. — (via search summary) [Official wiki: Powers](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Powers)
- World "Ages" (0.21): Hope (default), Sun, Dark, Tears, Moon, Chaos, Wonders, Ice, Ash, Despair. Each changes loyalty and opinions and boosts specific biome growth. The Age of Dark "could last 200-300 years". Clicking time opens an eras window. — [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)
- World Laws act as global rule toggles, e.g. Mutant Box (1–4 random birth traits per subspecies), Gene Spaghetti, Glitched Noosphere and Grin Reaper. Some sit under a "forbidden knowledge" sub-menu. — [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog); (via search summary) [Steam achievement guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3520774032)
- Achievements were added in 0.50. In 0.50 most traits, genes and creatures must be unlocked by finding them naturally generated in the world, a light progression layer. — [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog); (via search summary) [TV Tropes YMMV](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/YMMV/WorldBox)
- Unit possession (0.50) lets the player directly control a unit's movement and attacks. — [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog); (via search summary) [Official wiki: Units](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Units)
- Upcoming "ChessBox Mode" is a World Law that restricts god powers while you control a meta-group, described as adding a roleplay element. — [Steam news](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1206560/allnews/)
- PC supports larger world sizes than mobile. — (via search summary) [Steam discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1206560/discussions/)
- Single-player only. — [FAQ](https://www.superworldbox.com/faq)

### Inferences
- The typical arc is creation, then emergence, then catastrophe. Because nothing is at stake, the player invents their own goals (an "evil god", a "balanced world", "1000-year observation"). The YouTube formats in Section 1 show this.
- Ages, World Laws, achievements, and unlock-by-discovery are the game's only answers to "what now?". They add soft structure without a win state.

## 3. God powers: catalogue, costs, cooldowns, unlocks, limits

### Takeaway
The powers are a large set of free, unlimited brushes and buttons in six tab groups: world creation, civilizations, creatures, nature/disasters, destruction, and other. The player has no mana, currency or cooldown on PC. The only gates are mobile ad/Premium locks and the 0.50 "discover it first" unlocks for traits, genes and creatures.

### Cited Findings
- Six power groups: World Creation, Civilizations, Creatures, Nature and Disasters, Destruction Powers, Other Powers. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldBox)
- Main controls: pause, hourglass, world statistics, Eye of Insight, world laws. World shaping: shovels, vortex, demolish, Life Eraser, paint, fuse. A separate "Other various powers" tab also exists. — (via search summary) [Official wiki: Powers](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Powers)
- Other powers include Divine Light, The Plague, Zombie Infection, Gray Goo, Tumor, Crabzilla, Flame Tower, and Ice Tower. — (via search summary) [Fan wiki: Other various powers](https://worldbox-sandbox-god-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Other_various_powers)
- Destruction tools include explosives (bombs up to nukes), earthquakes, volcanoes, and acid rain. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldBox); [PC Gamer via Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldBox)
- PC Gamer's headline highlighted "an actual Thanos snap button". The article body could not be retrieved, so what it does exactly (presumably wiping out a share of life) is unconfirmed. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/super-worldbox-impressions/)
- Diplomacy and kingdom powers (0.21): "spite" starts a total war, and "friendship" removes a kingdom from a war. 0.50.4 added a border brush. Earlier versions (0.14) added kingdom banner and trait control. — [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldBox)
- Creature and race spawning grew with each update: four civilized races at Steam launch; 0.50 added monoliths that "evolve" animals into humanoid civilizations (e.g. turtle-people); 0.51 added Navy Seals, Punkcorns, ghosts and aliens/UFOs; 0.50.5 added planes, bees, dunglords and the hypercube. — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1206560/WorldBox__God_Simulator/); (via search summary) [Steam discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1206560/discussions/); [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)
- Traits, genetics and possession: an editable Genetics tab per subspecies; 204 subspecies traits (wiki count); creature traits can be applied; unit possession. — (via search summary) [Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3520774032); [Official wiki: Subspecies Traits](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Subspecies_Traits); [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)
- UI and tools: 0.51 added new power tabs, multi-unit selection, meta-object selection and Ctrl+0–9 hotkey binding. — [Steam news](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1206560/allnews/)
- Costs and limits: mobile powers are partly locked behind ad views or the $7.99 Premium unlock. On PC all powers are unlocked by default. In 0.50, most traits, genes and creatures must first be found in the world before they can be used. — [FAQ](https://www.superworldbox.com/faq); (via search summary) [TV Tropes YMMV](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/YMMV/WorldBox)
- ChessBox Mode (upcoming) would restrict some powers during Meta Control, the first opt-in self-limitation mode. — [Steam news](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1206560/allnews/)

### Inferences
- With no cost or cooldown, the player's limiting factor is attention and taste, not resources. That pushes play toward experimentation and escalation, which may be why sessions end in nukes.
- The developer's move toward ChessBox (voluntary restriction) suggests that unlimited power eventually flattens long-session interest. Constraint adds meaning.

## 4. Inhabitant simulation: autonomous units, kingdoms, culture, genetics, and reactions to interventions

### Takeaway
Units have needs (hunger, happiness), jobs, traits, families and genes. They form villages, kingdoms, clans, cultures, languages and religions, and they plot, ally, war and rebel on their own. The player mostly acts indirectly, by changing terrain, biomes, Ages, World Laws and traits, or by injecting disasters and creatures. The simulation absorbs these and shows the results through history logs and statistics.

### Cited Findings
- Steam pitch: "living creatures with needs"; humans, orcs, elves and dwarves build kingdoms, wage wars, and run diplomacy. Civilizations can grow, war, ally and rebel. — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1206560/WorldBox__God_Simulator/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldBox)
- Happiness runs from -100 to 100 and determines whether units are happy, whether they can find a best friend, and which tasks they perform. It replaced the older mood system in 0.50. — (via search summary) [Official wiki: Mood](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Mood)
- Hunger: units look for food at 50% hunger or below. At 0% they starve and lose 5% of max health every 3 (in-game) months. Civilized units have a favourite food that affects mood. — (via search summary) [Official wiki: Food](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Food)
- Reproduction needs empty housing in the city, and gives +10 happiness afterwards. — (via search summary) [Official wiki: Reproduction](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Reproduction)
- Jobs include cleaner, woodcutter and food gatherer. Food-gatherer slots scale with the village's food stock and fill quickly when there is no food. — (via search summary) [Official wiki: Jobs](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Jobs)
- Clans (0.21) are bloodlines started by kings or leaders. They have levels, small member bonuses and a motto, and they feed into diplomacy and loyalty. Succession goes to the oldest clan member in the kingdom. — [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)
- Plots and plans (0.21): clan members start plots before wars or rebellions, such as starting wars, poisoning kings or forming alliances. A plot breaks if its planners die. — [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)
- Alliances level up with age: L1 +5 max warriors, L2 +1 tower limit, L3 +10% armor, L4 +10% damage, L5 +1 max unit level. — [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)
- City loyalty factors include being recently conquered, sharing a clan with the king, the kingdom's world rank, and how long the king has ruled. Low loyalty drives rebellion. — [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldBox)
- Wars (0.21) track sides, duration and deaths. Captured cities go to the main side if borders touch. 0.50.4 added war renown. — [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)
- Cultures can be sorted by age, followers, level and villages, and have custom logo and colour (0.21). 0.50 added languages, religions and families. 0.50.4 added culture, language and religion traits. 0.51 added name-change history and made armies a meta object. — [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog); [Steam news](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1206560/allnews/)
- Subspecies and genetics (0.50): subspecies traits apply to the whole subspecies, and genes set base stats such as damage and lifespan. Trait examples: Unstable Genome periodically shuffles a subspecies' genes; Genetic Mirror makes children inherit all biological traits. 0.50.5 added multiple phenotypes per subspecies. Monoliths can evolve animals into sapient races. — (via search summary) [Reddit thread](https://reddit.sudovanilla.org/r/Worldbox/comments/1jr3fdz/news_box); [Official wiki: Subspecies Traits](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Subspecies_Traits); [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)
- Lifespan modifiers: e.g. the Long-liver trait adds +30 years. New statuses include rage, enchanted, invincible, ash fever and cough (0.21). — [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)
- Reacting to the environment: Ages change loyalty and opinions and biome growth. Cold foods (snow cucumbers, pine cones) can freeze units or give freeze resistance. Disasters and plagues spread on their own (plague, zombie infection, tumor, gray goo). — [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog); (via search summary) [Fan wiki: Other various powers](https://worldbox-sandbox-god-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Other_various_powers)
- Upcoming: fog of war will gate diplomacy, so kingdoms must meet before they can war. Corpses will rot and can be resurrected. Farms will be reworked. — [Steam news](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1206560/allnews/)
- Balance complaint (0.50, player report): human reproduction is slow enough that deaths outpace births and kingdoms collapse. — [BugBox](https://bugbox.featureupvote.com/suggestions/625879/severe-population-decline)
- Players say the 0.50 genetics numbers are hard to interpret without research. — (via search summary) [Reddit thread](https://reddit.sudovanilla.org/r/Worldbox/comments/1jr3fdz/news_box)

### Inferences
- Interventions feel meaningful mostly through second-order effects. A disaster does not just kill: it lowers loyalty, triggers rebellion, opens a power vacuum for a rival clan, or changes succession. The plot, loyalty and clan layer turns god actions into stories.
- WorldBox has many agents and little individual depth. Each unit is a stat bundle, and the story comes from aggregation (kingdom histories) rather than from one character's thoughts. That is the reverse of Godling's single-agent setup.

### Gaps
- No detailed per-unit decision model (task priority order) was found. Fear or reaction behaviours toward specific god powers (e.g. fleeing meteors) were not documented in accessible sources.
- Exact birth rate, age-of-maturity and population cap formulas were not found.

## 5. In-game economy: civilisation resources, production, building costs, growth; player currency

### Takeaway
Civilizations run a simple stockpile economy. They gather wood, stone, iron, gold and various foods into village storage and spend them on buildings, upgrades and gear; food and housing gate population growth. The player has no currency at all. Confirmed: powers cost nothing in-game. The only "costs" are real-world ones on mobile (ads/Premium).

### Cited Findings
- Resources include wood (mostly from cutting trees, "one of the most needed resources"), stone, iron and gold (mined), and foods (including worms, snow cucumbers and pine cones, added in 0.21). — (via search summary) [Official wiki: Economy](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Economy); [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)
- A building's cost is taken from village storage as soon as its construction site is placed, not on completion. — (via search summary) [Official wiki: Economy / Category: Buildings](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Buildings)
- Mine: 10 stone + 5 wood; produces stone, iron and gold. — (via search summary) [Fan wiki: Mines](https://worldbox-sandbox-god-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Mines)
- House tiers: tier 1 free; higher tiers need combinations of wood, stone, iron and gold. Sources conflict, e.g. a tier-2 house at 4 wood vs. 10 wood + 10 stone. Town hall tiers need wood and stone, plus iron at tier 3. — (via search summary) [Fan wiki: Humans](https://worldbox-sandbox-god-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Humans); [Official wiki: Town Hall](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Town_Hall)
- Gold was called the only "currency"-type resource for civilizations, capped at 999, with its main use being building upgrades (as of v0.5.144, an old version). — (via search summary) [Fan wiki: Civilizations, Kingdoms, and Villages](https://worldbox-sandbox-god-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Civilizations,_Kingdoms,_and_Villages)
- Population growth needs empty housing and food (hunger mechanics above). Food-gatherer jobs scale with food scarcity. — (via search summary) [Official wiki: Reproduction](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Reproduction); [Official wiki: Jobs](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Jobs)
- Alliance level bonuses raise military caps (max warriors, tower limit). — [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)
- Player currency: none. Powers are free to use. On mobile, access to some powers is bought with ad views or the one-time $7.99 Premium; on PC everything is included in the $19.99 price. — [FAQ](https://www.superworldbox.com/faq)
- A farm rework is planned for the Meta Control update. — [Steam news](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1206560/allnews/)

### Inferences
- The economy is legible and shallow. Its job is to make growth and collapse visible (cities expand, upgrade, starve), not to be optimized. The player can indirectly "tax" or "boost" it through terrain, biomes, Ages and disasters, but never trades with it.

### Gaps
- Current (0.51) exact building cost tables, storage caps, trade between cities/kingdoms, taxation, and city-size scaling formulas were not retrievable (wiki blocked, and sources conflict).
- Whether gold still caps at 999 in current versions is not confirmed.

## 6. Reception, long-session appeal, emergent stories, and how events are surfaced to the watcher

### Takeaway
Players love the miniature living world, the emergent drama (wars, rebellions, plagues, dynasties) and the cathartic destruction. Critics note that without goals, interest can fade after a few hours. Players complain about slow, lumpy update gaps and some 0.50 balance and complexity issues. WorldBox surfaces events through a filterable world-history log, favourite units, a follow camera, important-event notices, statistics and graphs, and (coming) group-level Meta Control observation.

### Cited Findings
- Steam reception: 96% positive across 30,120 English reviews ("Overwhelmingly Positive"). — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1206560/WorldBox__God_Simulator/)
- Press: RPS ("happy four hours", "too smart to give you goals") and PC Gamer ("boredom-killing nuclear bomb", "Thanos snap button"). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldBox); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/super-worldbox-impressions/)
- Update-drought frustration: a Steam thread titled "The Update Drought of 26'" (10–27 May 2026) asks for more communication and has no developer reply. Commenters noted the last big update took about two years. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1206560/discussions/0/836124330258598405/)
- The 0.50 population-collapse complaint and the complexity of genetics numbers (see Section 4). — [BugBox](https://bugbox.featureupvote.com/suggestions/625879/severe-population-decline)
- World History log: events are logged automatically for real-time or later viewing, and the log can be filtered by Kings, Favorite units, Cities, Kingdoms, Wars, Clans, and Disasters. Favourited units get perks, and their deaths are logged with different wording for natural death vs. being killed. The PC log limit rose from 40 to 200 entries (0.8.2). — (via search summary) [Official wiki: World History](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/World_History); [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)
- Follow camera: the Unit tab's follow option (F key) locks the camera on a unit. An "Important Events" toggle shows major events as they happen. — (via search summary) [Official wiki: Powers](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Powers)
- Statistics: a world statistics window and an eras window (0.21). Expanded graphs and statistics in 0.50.5, graph settings in 0.50.6, and name-change history in 0.51. — [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)
- Nameplates over cultures, alliances, cities, capitals and clans (0.22) make political geography readable at a glance. — [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)
- Procedural music (0.21) changes with what is on screen, an ambient cue for what is happening. — [Changelog](https://www.superworldbox.com/changelog)
- Upcoming Meta Control lets the player lock onto a species, kingdom or city and observe it, with fog of war lifted around it; meta windows gain population breakdowns by subspecies, language and culture. — [Steam news](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1206560/allnews/); (via search summary) [Steam announcements](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1206560/allnews/)
- Emergent-story video formats: hell-world builds (about 2.67M views), "100 kingdoms for 1,000 years", "left open for 24 hours", and "the viewer is god". — [superworldbox.com/community](https://www.superworldbox.com/community); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f4EIP_n-6Ng); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xwHC4x-HMTY); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wzz8iV8yeV4)

### Inferences
- WorldBox is watchable because it reads at a glance. Pixel-art top-down maps, coloured kingdom borders and nameplates show the whole state at once. Destruction gives immediate payoff, and long simulation gives slow-burn narrative. Video creators supply the missing goal, e.g. "simulate hell" or "1,000 years".
- "Leave it running for 24 hours" videos show that the simulation alone can generate a watchable story without player input. That is the scenario closest to Godling.
- The history log, favourites and follow camera are WorldBox's tools for compressing an overwhelming amount of simulation into stories a human can follow. The 0.22–0.51 updates kept adding naming, history and statistics features, which signals that the developer sees event-surfacing as a core need.

### Notable for Godling
- WorldBox gives the player no currency. Powers are free, and monetization gates access to powers (mobile ads or Premium, or the PC price), not each use. Godling's per-use viewer credits are a different model, closer to Twitch-integration mods (a small GameBanana mod exists) than to WorldBox itself.
- Indirect influence feels meaningful because of second-order social and political effects (loyalty, plots, succession, rebellion) and lasting world changes (Ages, terrain, biomes), not because of the immediate damage.
- Unlimited, free power leads to boredom and escalation ("boredom-killing nuclear bomb"; RPS done after about 4 hours). The developer is now adding voluntary constraint (ChessBox) and discovery-based unlocks (0.50), a signal that pacing or limits on viewer interventions help long sessions.
- Long-horizon formats (24-hour runs, 1,000-year runs) are a proven audience draw. A persistent, legible chronicle (history log filtered by actor type, favourite-unit death notices, name-change history, era windows) is what makes long runs followable.
- Players stay for the sandbox even without updates: the average stays around 1.5–2K concurrent and reviews at 96% through a 14-month gap. Major content drops still spike attention 3–5x.
- A "follow/lock onto an actor" camera, with fog of war lifted around the chosen entity, is how WorldBox focuses a viewer's attention. That maps directly onto a single-builder stream camera.

### Gaps
- No reliable data on average session length or median playtime (SteamSpy playtime is restricted).
- No developer interview explaining design intent (e.g. why no goals, philosophy on player influence) was found in accessible sources.
- Full critic review texts (RPS, PC Gamer) could not be retrieved, so only quoted fragments via Wikipedia and headlines are used.
