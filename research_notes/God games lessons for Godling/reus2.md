## 1. History and business: studio, publisher, release, platforms, engine, price, DLC/updates, sales, reviews, Reus 1 comparison, funding, talks

### Takeaway
Abbey Games is a small Utrecht indie studio: four founders, about 12 staff at its peak, 3 people after the 2019 Godhood crisis, and about 10 on Reus 2 as of 2023-09. Its 2013 debut Reus cost about €12k and sold more than a million copies. Reus 2 (Unity per secondary sources; published by Firesquid; $24.99) launched on Steam on 2024-05-28, reached Switch and Xbox on 2025-10-23, and got 12+ free updates and 4 paid biome DLCs through 2026-09. It is critically well liked: 94% positive on Steam across about 2.3k reviews, and an OpenCritic top-critic average of 79. Commercially it is far smaller than Reus 1: third-party estimates range from about 42k to about 88k Steam units and about $0.63M to $1.56M gross.

### Cited Findings

**Studio history and team**
- Abbey Games was founded by four Utrecht University students: Adriaan Jansen, Bas Zalmstra, Maarten Wiedenhof and Manuel Kerssemakers. Reus began in a university basement, and the team then moved to the Dutch Game Garden business centre in Utrecht — [MCV, 2015-10-28](https://www.mcvuk.com/news/read/reus-developer-abbey-games-on-escaping-the-basement/0158113)
- Adriaan and Bas met in the second half of 2011 through DGDARC at Utrecht University and first made jam games (Momo and Mia, Llamapalooza). The studio moved into a Dutch Game Garden office in April 2012. Reus was made with "virtually no money", funded by government student loans and the founders' savings, with unpaid helpers. Jansen originally planned 6 months with 2 people, which he later called unrealistic — [Abbey Games blog, "The Road to Reus", 2013-03-11](https://abbeygames.com/blog/2013/03/11/the-road-to-reus/)
- Kerssemakers said Reus was made with "the incredibly low up-front investment of €12,000". Reus profits were reinvested in Renowned Explorers, so the studio stayed independent with no publisher — [MCV, 2015-10-28](https://www.mcvuk.com/news/read/reus-developer-abbey-games-on-escaping-the-basement/0158113). The GDC 2014 session listing by Bas Zalmstra ("Reus: From Zero to Indie Hit", Production track) cites development costs of $16,000 over 18 months — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020383/Reus-From-Zero-to-Indie). The two figures are consistent within the EUR/USD conversion.
- Dutch support: Abbey Games was supported by the Dutch Game Garden, described as an EU-funded studio incubation programme — [Wikipedia: Reus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reus_(video_game)). I found no evidence of Stimuleringsfonds Creatieve Industrie or Nederlands Filmfonds game grants for Reus or Reus 2 (see Gaps).
- Renowned Explorers: International Society (released 2015-09-02) had a budget of about $600K plus about $200K for the in-house engine "AbbeyCore". It sold about 10K units in month 1 and had about 145K owners after 12 months; a TotalBiscuit video roughly doubled sales. The studio reached break-even and a small profit. After that release it shrank to a 10-person studio and then cut more staff. In the same post Reus is called "a huge financial success from launch" with about 100K sales — [Game Developer post-mortem by Adriaan Jansen, 2016-09-05](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/post-mortem-renowned-explorers)
- Godhood entered Early Access on 2019-07-10 — [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2019/05/abbey-games-to-launch-godhood-in-early-access-on-july-10th-looking-real-good/page=1). On 2019-11-05 Abbey announced it would end all staff contracts by 2019-12-31 to "scope down" and bring Godhood to 1.0 in the first half of 2020. The 4 founders stayed and 7 staff were let go; the founders sought to avoid bankruptcy — [Game Developer, 2019-11-06](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/abbey-games-scoping-down-and-terminating-all-employee-contracts-by-2020)
- Jansen: "We went from working with 12 people, down to 3, and now we're back up to 10 working on Reus 2." "We created 3 games in 10 years." He said reaching even 20% of the original's success would be a "resounding success" — [PreMortem Games, 2023-09-20](https://premortem.games/2023/09/20/why-it-took-abbey-games-a-decade-to-come-up-with-a-sequel-to-debut-hit-reus/)
- Jansen said that when Reus 2 pre-production began the studio was "probably at its lowest point ever", having lost 75% of the team. Leaning on the Reus IP was partly a way to rebuild confidence. He is co-founder and the person mainly responsible for game design — [Game Developer, 2024-07-30](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-the-team-behind-reus-2-nurtured-a-studio-and-ip-back-to-life)
- As of 2026-04-03 the studio said it was busy with a new, unannounced project and "can't divide ourselves between two major projects". It said "the main game has reached the content and additions we wanted to do" after 12 free updates and 3 DLCs, and that work on one more DLC (themed on Atlas, the Rock Giant) had begun in early 2026. Posts are signed "Adriaan and the Monks at the Abbey" — [Steam news, "What's planned for the Giants in 2026?", 2026-04-03](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1775260800)

## 2. Structure and loop: sessions, eras, planets, goals/win conditions, challenges, progression between sessions, sandbox

### Takeaway
A Reus 2 session is one planet of about 30–45 minutes. It runs a prologue plus up to 3 human Eras chosen from options, each with goals, and an optional 3-star "Challenger Quest"/Turning Point. There is no clock: an "Eon" action budget advances only when you place things. The finished planet stays in your galaxy, and its rating feeds profile-level meta-progression that unlocks biotica, giants, leaders and eras over roughly 10–15 hours. There are also Free Play (sandbox), daily/weekly challenges, seeded replays and highscores.

### Cited Findings
- Reus 1 was real-time with a fixed session timer of 30, 60 or 120 minutes. Reus 2 replaced this with a finite "eon" budget spent per placement. Completing objectives in each of three eras grants more eon and advances you; if you fail, progress stalls and you move to another planet — [SUPERJUMP, 2026-01-02](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/how-changing-one-mechanic-elevated-reus-2-beyond-its-predecessor/)
- Dev framing: "Eon (representing time) only moves forward when you place Biotica." Cities raise the Eon limit and add actions, and replacing a bioticum costs 1/5 of a new placement. The aim is that players can take breaks and the game adapts to their speed — [Steam news, "Reus 2 vs Reus", 2024-05-08](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1715212800). Jansen calls it a "faux-turn-based system" that is "much more relaxed" while still feeling like a living simulation — [PreMortem, 2023-09-20](https://premortem.games/2023/09/20/why-it-took-abbey-games-a-decade-to-come-up-with-a-sequel-to-debut-hit-reus/)
- Eras: "As time progresses with any of your actions, so does humanity… If you develop enough, you will enter a new Era." You choose one Ancient, one Medieval and one Discovery era. Options depend on game progress and on the planet's appearance. Each era has a unique twist and an optional Challenger Quest; completing it gives unlocks, resources and a short cinematic. Example: in the Castle Age, three cities grow in different directions, and "Help a city too often, and the others will hold a grudge against you" — [Steam news, "Dev Journal: The Eras", 2024-04-12](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1712966400)
- "Up to 3 Eras per planet, each with its own goals" (e.g. getting humans to space, a revolution). Completing goals triggers "a little happening". Each era has a 3-star goal tied to achievements — [Steam news, "Reus 2 vs Reus"](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1715212800). Each planet has 3 Turning Points that grow in size as the game progresses. Planet milestones give bonus Eon and mountain charges — [Demo Conclusions](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1709683200)
- Each era has three overall goals. Meeting them, or ending an era early, grants energy for placements and unlocks upgrades or alternate bonuses — [Turn Based Lovers, 2025-02-04](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/reus-2-impressions)

### Inferences
- The macro-structure is a roguelite: a short run (planet), drafted options, an end rating, and permanent unlocks. The god-game fantasy sits inside a puzzle-score loop. Eras turn a 30–45 minute run into three visible "chapters", each with a choice point (which era next) and a stretch goal.
- Because Eon spending is the only clock, pacing is fully player-controlled. Reus 2 never simulates while the player is idle. This is a deliberate departure from Reus 1's live world.

### Gaps

- Detailed Free Play rules (e.g. infinite Eon?) were not found.

## 3. Mechanics: giants, biomes, resources and placement, symbiosis/adjacency, aspects→micros, upgrades, luxuries, projects, hunters/heroes, harming and helping humans, greed and danger

### Takeaway
You never touch humans. You pick 3 of 6 giants; each terraforms its biome(s) and places "biotica" (about 300+ plants, animals and minerals) on a small hex grid. Yields depend on tag-based adjacency (e.g. "bird", "tree", "herbivore"), biome-wide rules, biodiversity (a count of distinct biotica that powerful "Apex" biotica consume as upkeep), boosted nodes, and Micros (attachable modifiers). Greed, war and giant hunting were removed at launch as unpopular "nuisances". They returned in 2025 as optional relationship systems (likes and dislikes, wars, temples, hunters) with per-giant disaster abilities and a Pacifism toggle.

## 4. Human simulation and indirect control

### Takeaway
Humans in Reus 2 are led by personality-driven leaders who pick their own projects and requests from their character plus surroundings. They settle from nomad groups, expand their borders toward attractive biotica, invent, trade, form likes and dislikes, and since 2025 go to war, build temples or hunt giants. The player's only levers are where land and biotica go (inside the borders gives yield immediately; just outside pulls expansion), which leaders and Ambition to back at setup, which era to enter, whom to help (helping one city too often breeds grudges), and optional disasters. The player never issues orders.

### Cited Findings
- "Every nomad or city will carry a personality", shown as a leader portrait. Options for projects come from "a combination of their character and surroundings". Turning Points depend on which personalities you support: "the favored will paint the future". At planet creation you choose one personality to help most (the Ambition), which sets the planet's success goal — [Dev Journal: Personalities, 2023-11-10](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1699660800)
- Leader examples. Goddess: loves wealth and splendour, wants the most glorious city, and her hubris must be checked. Inventor: shares ideas between cities, producing curious projects and eras, and may refuse some resources. ### Inferences
- The indirect control works through a few levers:
  1. **Placement as attraction.** The border "lure" makes people move toward what you give.
  2. **Placement as yield.** Gifts inside the borders feed requests.

  4. **Reputation.** Request fulfilment and favouritism shape relationships.

## 5. In-game economy: food, wealth, tech, how values combine, scaling across an era, balance

### Takeaway
There are three base yields: Food, Wealth/Gold ("valuables") and Science/Technology. Prosperity aggregates population, tech and wealth per city, and is the score. Yields combine additively with flat adjacency and tag bonuses, percentage boosts, per-count scalers ("per adjacent empty space", "per Undergrowth in biome, max 4") and caps. Biodiversity acts as a support currency and as upkeep for Apex biotica. Eon is the action currency, and Technology also buys Micros. Rising prosperity costs, era-gated Micros and frequent numeric patches keep the game balanced.

### Inferences
- Reus 2's economy is a score economy, not a survival economy. Yields only matter for hitting request, era and prosperity thresholds. - Reus 1 punished oversupply (greed). Reus 2 instead limits the god's own actions (Eon) and requires variety (biodiversity upkeep). The constraint moved from the humans' psychology to the god's budget.

## 7. What is notable for Godling (24/7 autonomous agent; viewers change conditions but cannot buy decisions)

### Takeaway
Reus is a working template for "influence without command". The god shapes the land, the people read it through their own personality and decide, and the game makes that readable through visible requests, a border "lure", and reputation. Abbey's own post-mortem view is that the human-reaction layer was the under-built part. That layer is exactly where Godling's value lies.

### Cited Findings
- Placing a resource just outside the borders makes humans expand toward it; inside the borders it yields immediately — [5 tips](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1717027200)
- Leaders interpret identical inputs differently (mystery conversion per leader); the Inventor may refuse resources; projects come from "character and surroundings" — [gameplay.tips](https://gameplay.tips/guides/reus-2-gameplay-tips-faq.html); [Dev Journal: Personalities](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1699660800)
- Tag-based symbiosis across about 300 elements on a tiny grid — [Reus 2 vs Reus](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1715212800)
- Biodiversity upkeep and Micro or draft reworks were used to break dominant spam combos — [Dev Journal: Biodiversity](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1701475200); [Micro explainer](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1740787200)
- Favouritism breeds grudges; giant reputation depends on request fulfilment; temples versus giant hunters; disasters are optional, with Pacifism and Event Intensity settings — [Dev Journal: The Eras](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1712966400); [Cataclysm notes](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=3&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1737072000)
- Reus 1's war and giant-hunting "were never a fan favorite" and Reus 1 humans were "very annoying" — [Reus 2 vs Reus](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1715212800)
- Human–giant interaction was the "biggest missed opportunity"; reviewers say the game is "more puzzle than god game" — [Game Developer 2024](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-the-team-behind-reus-2-nurtured-a-studio-and-ip-back-to-life); [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1875060/reus_2.md)
- Action budget (Eon) instead of a clock; eras as 3 chapters per 30–45 minute run, with choice points and stretch goals — [SUPERJUMP](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/how-changing-one-mechanic-elevated-reus-2-beyond-its-predecessor/); [NWR](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/73233/reus-2-switch-review)
- CCU jumps about 3–7× baseline at each content drop — [SteamCharts](https://steamcharts.com/app/1875060)
- Community-wide shared goal (summed prosperity unlocking content for all) — [Nikko post](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=1875060&count=1&maxlength=0&format=json&enddate=1763683200)

### Inferences (short "notable for Godling" list; not a design proposal)
- **Lure, don't command.** Reus's cleanest indirect verb is "place something attractive near the edge of their world and they choose to go there". Viewer-spawned objects work best as attractors and affordances that the agent may or may not adopt. The agent's own personality decides the uptake, like the Inventor refusing gifts.
- **Make the inhabitant's wants visible.** Reus cities post a request list. A builder agent that publicly states needs or plans gives viewers something meaningful to answer with conditions, without buying decisions.
- **Tags over recipes.** Godling's spawnables and building parts could carry a small tag vocabulary that the agent reasons over (shelter, food, height, wet, fire). This mirrors Reus 2's shift from name-based to tag-based adjacency, which multiplied emergent combos.
- **Anti-spam via variety and upkeep.** Biodiversity (reward distinctness; powerful items need upkeep) and per-era charge resets are tested ways to stop a crowd repeating one dominant intervention.
- **Reputation memory toward the "god".** Reus 2's likes and dislikes, grudges from favouritism, and temples versus hunters let consequences accrue from the history of interventions, not single acts. For a viewer-driven god, the agent could hold attitudes toward kinds of events (gifts versus meteors) as its own response.
- **Keep hostility optional and non-punitive.** Reus 1's greed and war were disliked. Reus 2 brought conflict back as opt-in, small-area disasters with intensity settings. Large viewer events (meteors) work better as dramatic but recoverable perturbations than as fail states. Reus's one hard fail is that a dead giant means withdrawing from the planet.
- **Invest in the reaction layer.** Abbey's own admitted weakness (thin human–giant interaction, "more puzzle than god game") is Godling's core feature: the watched character's interpretation of and response to changed conditions.
- **Chapters and drops for a 24/7 stream.** Reus 2 paces play with era chapters (goal → choice → stretch goal → cinematic) and paces the business with content drops that cause CCU spikes. A continuous stream may still benefit from named eras or milestones and periodic new spawnables. Community-wide summed goals (Nikko contest) are a proven crowd mechanic.
- **Budget the god, not the clock.** Reus 2's Eon budget limits divine actions instead of time. This maps naturally to viewer credits, though Godling's world, unlike Reus 2's, keeps living between interventions.

### Gaps
- None specific beyond those listed in sections 1–6.
