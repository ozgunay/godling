## 1. History and business: team, launches, platforms, players, revenue model, prices, ratings, survival

### Takeaway
Godville is a two-person side project that has lasted 19 years: Russian browser game in 2007, English version 2010, mobile from 2010/2011. Its only revenue is optional "godpower charge" packs, about $0.20 to $0.25 per charge. There are no real ads and no subscription, though a charge-priced monthly "Blessing" works much like one. Over 1.6M heroes had been created by May 2025, but the active base is small: about 3K iOS MAU (estimate). The developers said in May 2026 that the community "has been quietly shrinking."

### Inferences
- Revenue (estimate): AppGoblin's iOS-only "<$10K/month" plus Android (at least 1M EN and 500K+ RU installs) and web sales suggests a modest business. Plausibly low six figures USD a year in total, but this is not confirmed by any source. The developers have never published revenue in what I could reach.
- Effective price per charge (my calculation from US prices): $0.248 at 4-pack, falling to $0.200 at 250-pack. One charge restores 50% godpower, which pays for 2 encourages or 10 godvoices. So a bought godvoice costs about $0.02 and a bought encourage about $0.10–0.12. A month of Blessing (60 charges) costs about $12–15 at pack prices: a disguised optional subscription.
- How it survived 19 years: a tiny team, text-only content that is cheap to serve, community-written content (52K accepted ideas), no ad dependence, a steady trickle of updates, and goals spanning years. The 2026 "shrinking" post shows the limit of no marketing.

## 2. Structure and loop: what the hero does alone, the diary, what the god sees per visit, check-in rhythm

### Takeaway
The hero runs a fixed autonomous loop: quests, fighting monsters, looting, selling in towns, levelling, praying, dying and being resurrected. He narrates it in a short, joke-dense first-person diary addressed to his god. Most of the phrases are written by players through the Ideabox. Each visit the god reads what happened since last time and decides whether to spend scarce godpower. The game is pitched as a "couple of clicks an hour" companion rather than a session game.

### Cited Findings
- The hero is an NPC and "religious fanatic" who keeps a diary to talk to his god. He wanders, fights monsters, finds treasure, levels up, learns skills, develops a personality, and can have a pet. — [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godville)
- The game summarizes the key events since the player's last visit. — [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godville)
- Some items "only work with the god's involvement". Otherwise the hero sells them anyway. — [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godville)
- Official pitch: "a self-playing comedy game about gods, heroes, and heroes who ignore their gods' wishes". You can watch the hero "write a story on their own, or try to control the hero, usually without success". — [godvillegame.com](https://godvillegame.com/)
- Google Play pitch: "Take a look on your hero for a couple days". The world "continuously evolved based on ideas and suggestions from the community of players". — [Google Play EN](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.godvillegame.android&hl=en_US)
- The Russian listing says a huge part of the game "was created by the players themselves". — [Google Play RU](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ru.godville.android&hl=en_US)
- There are no graphics. The text is dense with film, book, game and meme references and "cynical and peculiar" humour, and players share diary entries online. — [iPhones.ru](https://www.iphones.ru/iNotes/48892); [Google Play RU](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ru.godville.android&hl=en_US)
- Diary rules (GodWiki Ideabox guidance): entries are "first person and past tense" and "must be appropriate for the hero's intelligence". — [GodWiki: Ideabox](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Ideabox) (search excerpt)
- The diary shows the latest 10 entries, rising to 20 after the "Book of Creation" goal **[Grokipedia, unverified]**. — [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/godville)
- Ideabox numbers (May 2025): 796,000 ideas submitted and 52,000 implemented, about 6.5% accepted (my calculation). — [blog/post/179](https://godvillegame.com/blog/post/179)
- Ideabox access: ideas from hero level 3, more categories at level 10, voting at level 15, full access at level 20. — [GodWiki: Ideabox](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Ideabox) (search excerpt)
- Ideabox rules (2012): ideas are voted on for 24 hours. Diary and "earthly news" submissions need 50% up-votes, other types 40%. — [Godville announcement 62](http://godvillegame.com/announcements/post/62) (search excerpt)
- Corrections to existing text (the "Enhancement Room") are gated by "IQ points": full access at 70 or more. — [GodWiki: Ideabox/IQ points](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Ideabox/IQ_points) (search excerpt)
- Wikipedia: players at level 10 can suggest updates, which the community votes on, and most in-game phrases are community-made or selected. — [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godville)
- Nov 2025: Ideabox voting became a swipe card deck (right to approve, left to dismiss). A quick button above the diary in the app opens it, and each idea's approval % is shown to its author. — [blog/post/184](https://godvillegame.com/blog/post/184)
- Content keeps flowing from the Ideabox:
  - Dec 2025: "Hundreds of new diary phrases and artifacts". — [Godville blog](https://godvillegame.com/blog)
  - Jul 2026: "several hundreds of new quests and monsters". — [Godville blog](https://godvillegame.com/blog)
  - Spring 2026: a call for reports of problematic in-game text. — [Godville blog](https://godvillegame.com/blog)
- Daily newspaper, the "Godville Times": pantheon and bingo updates refresh at midnight **[Grokipedia, unverified]**. — [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/godville)
- The newspaper's daily activatable ad has odd side-effects, e.g. remove "tribbles" but lose all loot, or cancel a side job and get a wallet. It costs no godpower. — [blog/post/167](https://godvillegame.com/blog/post/167)
- Daily variety and check-in hooks:
  - A "Daily Forecast" can change rules for the day, e.g. charges restore 70% instead of 50%. — [GodWiki: Godpower Accumulator](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Godpower_Accumulator) (search excerpt)
  - Quiz seasons last 2–3 months, and crosswords exist. — [Godville blog](https://godvillegame.com/blog)
  - Experimental push notifications for boss battles (Dec 2025). — [Godville blog](https://godvillegame.com/blog)
  - Optional one-time alerts (Jun 2026). — [App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/godville/id353421868)
  - The iPhone client added notifications for important events, which the browser lacks. — [iPhones.ru](https://www.iphones.ru/iNotes/48892)
- Player descriptions of the rhythm:
  - "Best game, if u wouldn't like to do more then one click per hour". — [Google Play RU review, 2021](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ru.godville.android&hl=en_US)
  - Another review says it "doesn't take too much attention", but checking more often helps the hero progress faster, and uninstalling and returning picks up where you left off. — [Google Play EN review, 2020](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.godvillegame.android&hl=en_US)
- Death: a hero dies when fleeing fails at 19 HP or below. Resurrection is free (0% godpower), or happens on its own after about 23–24 hours **[Grokipedia, unverified]**. The 0% resurrect cost is confirmed by the Fandom mirror. — [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/godville); [Fandom: Remote Control](https://godville.fandom.com/wiki/Remote_Control)

### Inferences
- The diary is the product: the simulation is thin (Progress-Quest-like), and the entertainment comes from tens of thousands of crowd-written, voted lines delivered in the hero's voice. Curation (votes, approval thresholds, IQ gating, problematic-text reports) is how quality is kept up without a writing staff.
- The check-in loop has several layers. There is a "since you were away" summary and a small godpower budget to spend. There is a daily newspaper with a daily ad, forecast, bingo and crossword. Notifications cover rare events (bosses), and long goals give you something to check on.

### Gaps
- I found no data on how many diary entries the hero writes per hour, how phrases are picked, or how the visit summary is built.
- I found no published session-length or sessions-per-day figures for Godville.

## 3. God powers: encourage, punish, voice of god, miracles, godpower, accumulator, alignment, limits

### Takeaway
All influence goes through one "Remote Control" that runs on a 100% godpower bar: godvoice 5%, encourage or punish 25%, miracle 50% (needs a temple), and arena, dungeon or sail sends about 50%. Charges (+50% each) are the only way to stretch it, and they come from levelling, wins, gifts and purchase. Godvoice commands are keyword-matched suggestions that the hero often ignores, especially while fighting, trading, resting or in town. Repeated encourages or punishes slowly shift the hero's personality, which drifts back to neutral if not maintained. The god can always be ignored, and a "pure ZPG" mode turns influence off.

### Cited Findings
**Costs (Remote Control)**
- Voice of God 5%, Encourage 25%, Punish 25%. Send to Arena is 75% on the old Fandom page; Resurrect 0% (only when dead); Miracle 50% (only after the temple). The Remote Control can be disabled in options to make Godville "a complete ZPG". — [Fandom: Remote Control](https://godville.fandom.com/wiki/Remote_Control)
- The current GodWiki pages agree: encourage/punish 25%, voice 5%. — [GodWiki: Remote Control](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Remote_Control); [GodWiki: Voice of god](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Voice_of_god) (search excerpts)
- Arena: hero level 10 or above and at least 50% godpower. — [GodWiki: Arena](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Arena) (search excerpt)
- Dungeon: temple owners only, costs 50% godpower. — [GodWiki: Dungeons](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Dungeons) (search excerpt)
- Sail: costs 50% plus 5% per navigation step. — [GodWiki: Sailing](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Sailing) (search excerpt)
- Datamine: sending a boss costs 50% godpower; each datamine action costs 100 "Thrust" plus 15% godpower. — [GodWiki: Datamine](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Datamine) (search excerpt)
- 2009 (Russian): the god could give gifts, heal, or strike with lightning, and speak via the "voice of god". — [Wikipedia RU](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Годвилль)

**Godpower and the accumulator**
- Godpower caps at 100%, or 200% for gods who own a shop. — [GodWiki: Godpower](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Godpower) (search excerpt)
- The accumulator unlocks at hero level 5. A charge restores 50% (70% on a favourable Daily Forecast). — [Fandom: Godpower Accumulator](https://godville.fandom.com/wiki/Godpower_Accumulator); [GodWiki: Godpower Accumulator](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Godpower_Accumulator) (search excerpt)
- Charge sources:
  - 1 charge per level.
  - Converting 100% godpower into a charge, but only while you hold fewer than 3 charges.
  - 1 charge per arena duel win.
  - A completed temple "generates several charges per day" (old Fandom page).
  - Purchase.
  - Artifacts. — [Fandom: Godpower Accumulator](https://godville.fandom.com/wiki/Godpower_Accumulator); [GodWiki: Godpower](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Godpower) (search excerpt)
- Overflow is wasted: using a charge at 75% loses the charge but gains only 25%. — [Fandom: Godpower Accumulator](https://godville.fandom.com/wiki/Godpower_Accumulator)
- Conflict on free regeneration: Grokipedia (citing GodWiki Godpower) says godpower regenerates 1% per minute, but an older GodWiki user page says it does not accumulate over time. Several sources say the hero restores godpower through **prayer and sacrifice**, including a prayer phase before leaving town. — [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/godville); [GodWiki: User:Carmentis](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/User:Carmentis); [GodWiki: Godpower](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Godpower) (search excerpts)
- If no charges are bought, sailing and dungeon matchmaking that fails within 15–20 min makes the hero pray back the spent godpower, then wait a 1-hour cooldown. — [GodWiki: Sailing](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Sailing) (search excerpt)

**Voice of god (godvoice)**
- A typed message costs 5% and can be at most 120 characters, with a 20-second penalty outside duels **[Grokipedia, unverified]**. — [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/godville)
- Keywords the hero responds to (Fandom list, older): pray/praise/worship; heal/rest/drink; strike/hit/attack/smite; die; buy/shop; dig/gold (treasure, sometimes a gold brick); sacrifice; examine "item"; town; learn (extra XP); join/create "X" guild; quest; drunk; stop/cancel quest. An empty voice confuses the hero, finds a random artifact, or scares off a monster. — [Fandom: Voice of God](https://godville.fandom.com/wiki/Voice_of_God)
- Arena keywords: strike, heal, pray (restores godpower), defend. — [Fandom: Voice of God](https://godville.fandom.com/wiki/Voice_of_God)
- Obedience is not guaranteed. Orders "are not always followed"; the hero "may completely disregard anything you tell her/him… You gave her/him free will after all." Repeated insults anger the hero and can cost godpower. — [Fandom: Voice of God](https://godville.fandom.com/wiki/Voice_of_God)
- Voices are ignored during fights, trading, resting or in town. Heroes are most likely to listen when outside town and idle. — [GodWiki: I AM](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/I_AM); [GodWiki: Extend pet registration](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Extend_pet_registration) (search excerpts)
- Cancel-quest is rate-limited: a third cancel in a row draws a complaint. — [GodWiki: Epic Quests](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Epic_Quests) (search excerpt)
- Return-to-town orders are obeyed only a limited number of times per quest (older community note). — [GodWiki: Jimbob64](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Jimbob64) (search excerpt)
- A community FAQ says voices "never backfire" and cost only 5%, but "have a higher miss rate" than encourage/punish. — [GodWiki: FAQ](http://wiki.godvillegame.com/Help_at_godville) (search excerpt)
- Success rates: arena voice commands succeed about 30% of the time; encourage/punish about 50–70% **[Grokipedia, unverified, citing GodWiki Arena/Probability]**. — [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/godville)
- 2009: a Russian commenter claimed prana fails to heal in about 50% of cases. — [godville.net blog/post/44](https://godville.net/blog/post/44)

**Encourage, punish and alignment drift**
- It takes at least 5 encourages to move a hero from Neutral to Gentle. Each punish cancels one earlier encourage, and about 5–6 punishes make a hero spiteful. — [GodWiki: Personality](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Personality) (search excerpt)
- Alignment fades: constant encouraging is needed to stay good, and constant punishment to stay evil, "otherwise a hero will eventually drop back down to neutral". — [GodWiki: Encouragement](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Encouragement); [GodWiki: Punishment](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Punishment) (search excerpts)
- Effects of alignment: good heroes heal faster, while evil heroes deal slightly more damage but also take more. Encourage and punish can backfire depending on where they are used. — [GodWiki: Personality](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Personality); [GodWiki: Encouragement](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Encouragement) (search excerpts)
- Players mainly use encourage to heal a hero below 25% HP. — [icspicy blog, 2014](http://icspicy.blogspot.com/2014/11/godville-encouragement.html) (search excerpt)
- The god has no control over pet taming or the pet's name. In duels, the god's help sometimes aids the opponent instead. — [Wikipedia RU](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Годвилль); [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godville)
- Relic "Flattery" makes the hero address the god in flowery language: the voice of the narration is itself a modifiable cosmetic. — [blog/post/181](https://godvillegame.com/blog/post/181)

### Inferences
- Autonomy is protected four ways:
  - The costs leave room for only a few actions per bar: 20 voices or 4 encourages.
  - Obedience is probabilistic and depends on context.
  - Some commands are rate-limited.
  - Personality drift slowly decays back to neutral, so influence must be ongoing rather than a one-off purchase.
- Paid charges buy **more attempts**, never guaranteed outcomes. A bought charge still runs into miss rates and "free will", which is why the 2009 claim that extra prana does not much speed growth is believable.

### Gaps
- No current official figures exist for godvoice success probability, encourage/punish failure rates, or the free godpower regeneration rate; sources conflict on the last.
- The current full keyword list is on the CAPTCHA-blocked GodWiki; the Fandom list may be outdated.

## 4. Social and long-term systems: temple, ark, pantheons, guilds, arena, dungeons, sail, datamine, bosses, savings, pets, relics, events

### Takeaway
Long-term structure is a chain of "1,000-unit" collection goals, each taking months to years. In order: temple (1,000 gold bricks), ark (1,000 logs), 1,000 creature pairs, lab and boss, Book of Creation, souls, savings (30M gold, which buys a shop), then relics. Each goal unlocks a new godpower-costing group activity (dungeons, sailing, datamine), and the goals are ranked in about 16 "pantheon" leaderboards. Social play is lightweight and asynchronous: duels, 3–4-hero dungeon and sea parties, guilds, and seasonal festivals.

### Cited Findings
**The goal chain**
- Temple: 1,000 gold bricks, each worth 0.1% of completion. A temple unlocks miracles (50%), dungeons, and (older Fandom) daily charges. — [GodWiki: Temple](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Temple) (search excerpt); [Fandom: Remote Control](https://godville.fandom.com/wiki/Remote_Control)
- Ark: 1,000 gopher-wood logs, found mainly in dungeons and rarer than bricks, so it takes longer than the temple. Completing it unlocks sailing and creature collecting, and lets the ark store up to 3 pets that can be renamed. — [GodWiki: Ark](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Ark) (search excerpt)
  - The ark can be extended to 500% (5,000 logs) for 7 stables **[Grokipedia, unverified]**. — [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/godville)
- After the ark:
  - Collect 1,000 creature pairs to build a Lab. — [GodWiki: Ark](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Ark) (search excerpt)
  - "Post-Lab": collect boss parts and creatures to build a boss monster. — [GodWiki: Walkthrough](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Walkthrough) (search excerpt)
  - Bosses grown in the lab fight other players' bosses for "bits". — [chasingdings 2020](https://chasingdings.com/2020/05/11/godville-is-ten-years-old/)
- Souls ("Soulfulness"): 5,000 souls from Soul Monsters, the dungeon basement (3 every 24 h), side jobs and Wanted monsters. ## 5. In-game economy: gold, loot, bricks, wood, savings, sinks, and how paid charges interact

### Takeaway
The hero earns gold by killing monsters and selling loot, and spends it on equipment, skills, drinking and other hero whims. Gold bricks and logs come mostly as drops, from digging, duels and dungeons, not from purchases. Paid charges convert money into more godpower actions: digging, healing, and entering dungeons, sails and arenas. Those actions raise the drop rate of bricks, logs and souls indirectly. Blessing (60 charges/month, 1.5x gathering) is the most direct paid accelerator and the main pay-to-win complaint. Charges never buy gold, items or completion outright.

### Cited Findings
- Base loop: the character fights monsters, sells loot, buys equipment. Godville inherited this from Progress Quest. — [Wikipedia: Progress Quest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_Quest); [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godville)
- Gold sinks: the godvoices "buy/shop" and "drunk" (wastes money on a drink). — [Fandom: Voice of God](https://godville.fandom.com/wiki/Voice_of_God)
- Skill upgrades cost about 500 gold × target level **[Grokipedia, unverified]**. — [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/godville)
- Digging (godvoice, 5%) can turn up gold, artifacts, or occasionally a gold brick. — [Fandom: Voice of God](https://godville.fandom.com/wiki/Voice_of_God)
- Brick value conflict: a GodWiki excerpt says a temple's 1,000 bricks have "a total value of 3,000,000 coins". Grokipedia says surplus bricks sell for about 1,000 coins each. — [GodWiki search excerpt](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Temple); [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/godville)
- Logs come mainly from dungeons, which cost 50% godpower and require a temple. This ties ark progress to spending godpower. — [GodWiki: Ark](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Ark); [GodWiki: Dungeons](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Dungeons) (search excerpts)
- Savings: 30M gold, then a shop and a 200% godpower cap. The shop then trades junk for relics on its own. — [GodWiki: Gold fund](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Gold_fund) (search excerpt); [blog/post/181](https://godvillegame.com/blog/post/181)
- Event economy: Halloween "pumpkinized" trophies sell at 700% of usual price. — [Godville blog](https://godvillegame.com/blog)
- Developer stance (2009): extra (paid) prana "will not significantly affect the speed of character growth", and the "Pure ZPG" option exists. — [godville.net blog/post/44](https://godville.net/blog/post/44)
- Player stance:
  - 2015: buying "influence" is not required for comfortable play. — [AppleVis forum](https://www.applevis.com/comment/73394) (search excerpt)
  - 2024 commenter: Blessings are "kinda pay to win". — [blog/post/170](https://godvillegame.com/blog/post/170)
- Ian Bogost disliked the monetization and the god interference, though he conceded some interaction is needed for it to count as a game. — [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godville)

### Inferences
- Money buys tempo, not outcomes. Every paid path still goes through the hero's autonomous behaviour and random drops. Pantheons rank cumulative achievement, so paying moves you up the leaderboard faster, which is where the pay-to-win criticism lands.
- The economy has two layers: gold stays inside the simulation, while godpower is the god's scarce currency. Only godpower is sold. This split lets the hero's life stay "his" while still allowing monetization.

### Gaps
- I found no figures on gold income per day, prices of equipment, or the drop rates of bricks, logs or souls; GodWiki is blocked.
- I found no data on what share of players pay, or on average spend.

## 6. Progress Quest and other zero-player / idle games: lessons about watching an autonomous character; retention data

### Takeaway
Progress Quest (2002) showed that people will watch a character do nothing meaningful, out of attachment and satire. Godville added the god's small, rationed interventions and crowd-written humour. Academic work frames idle games as "played by not playing", along a spectrum from clicker to zero-player. I found no published retention or session data for Godville or Progress Quest, and only generic mobile benchmarks.

### Cited Findings
- Progress Quest: by Eric Fredricksen, released 2002 for Windows and Linux, a parody of EverQuest and MMORPGs. After rolling stats there is no further interaction. The character fights, sells loot at market, and buys gear; quest and act bars fill on fixed timers. — [Wikipedia: Progress Quest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_Quest)
- Progress Quest's online realms run 2002–2021: Pemptus launched 8 Feb 2007; Alpaquil launched Dec 2021 with a browser version. Source code was released May 2011. Its guilds (Good, Neutral, Evil) give no benefit. — [Wikipedia: Progress Quest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_Quest)
- CNET (Nick Hide, 14 Mar 2007) tied Progress Quest's appeal to players' emotional attachment to their characters. Todd Howard (Bethesda) named it an influence on *Fallout Shelter*. It is called arguably the first "idle RPG / incremental game". — [Wikipedia: Progress Quest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_Quest)
- Edge called Godville a "direct descendant" of Progress Quest. — [Wikipedia RU](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Годвилль)
- Platov called it "a clear progeny of Progress Quest". — [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godville)
- Zero-player game definition: a game that evolves from its initial state with no further human input. Godville "can progress with no interaction from the player". Incremental games "may become zero-player at higher levels". — [Wikipedia: Zero-player game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-player_game)
- "Playing to Wait: A Taxonomy of Idle Games" (Alharthi, Alsaedi, Toups, Tanenbaum, Hammer; CHI 2018): grounded-theory analysis of 66 idle games plus 10 non-idle controls. It argues idle games are "primarily played by not playing" and proposes an interactivity spectrum from clicker to zero-player. — [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10061230); [Monash Research](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/playing-to-wait-a-taxonomy-of-idle-games/)
- Generic mobile retention, from a summary of the GameAnalytics 2026 benchmarks (16,000+ games): median D1 about 22%, D7 just under 4%, D30 about 0.7–0.8%; top 1% reach 64–68% D1 and 13–15% D30. — [GameAnalytics 2026 report page](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2026-mobile-pc-gaming-benchmarks); [InvestGame summary PDF](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-01-20-Mobile_retention_benchmarks_2026.pdf)
- Idle/AFK-specific figures appear only in an unverified blog table: 35–50% D1, 20–30% D7, 10–15% D30, with no underlying data shown. — [Playio blog](https://blog.playio.co/retention-by-game-genre)
- Godville's own pitch says it is for people who don't want to play "24x7x365". 148Apps called it "the ultimate game for people who don't have a lot of time". — [Google Play EN](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.godvillegame.android&hl=en_US)

### Inferences
- Progress Quest alone proves attachment to an autonomous character, but it has no reason to come back. Godville's additions supply the reason: a voice, a diary, a scarce budget of influence, daily news, and long goals. That combination has kept a paying niche for 19 years.

### Gaps
- I found no published retention, session-frequency or churn data for Godville, Progress Quest or other autonomous-hero ZPGs.
- I did not research other autonomous-character games (e.g. Neko Atsume, Tamagotchi-likes, LLM-agent Twitch streams) because the search budget ran out.

## 7. What players love and criticise; why "watch a character live its own life" works

### Takeaway
Players love the writing: absurd, reference-heavy, crowd-sourced humour in the hero's voice. They love the low time demand and the attachment to a hero who has lived for years. Criticisms are thin interaction ("give us more godly options"), monetization of tempo (Bogost; Blessing "pay to win"), non-native English typos in the early years, and a shrinking community.

## 8. Notable for Godling (short list, not a design)

### Takeaway
Godville is direct evidence that a single autonomous character plus a voiced diary can sustain a paying audience for 19 years without selling control. It also shows the ceiling of a text-only, no-marketing niche: about 1.6M heroes ever, a few thousand actively monetized, and shrinking in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- See sections 1–7 for sources. Key anchors:
  - Costs 5% / 25% / 50% and charges at +50%. — [Fandom: Remote Control](https://godville.fandom.com/wiki/Remote_Control)
  - Charge price about $0.20–0.25. — [App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/godville/id353421868)
  - The hero ignores commands while busy. — [GodWiki: I AM](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/I_AM)
  - Alignment decays. — [GodWiki: Encouragement](https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Encouragement)
  - 52K crowd-written ideas implemented. — [blog/post/179](https://godvillegame.com/blog/post/179)
  - "Pure ZPG" opt-out and the no-pay-to-grow promise (2009). — [godville.net blog/post/44](https://godville.net/blog/post/44)

### Inferences
- **Sell attempts, not outcomes.** Godville's charges refill a meter; every use still passes through the hero's free will (miss rates, ignored while busy, rate limits). This parallels Godling credits changing conditions but never decisions.
- **Price anchor.** One Godville charge (about €0.20–0.25) buys 2 encourages or 10 voices, so about €0.02 per "nudge" and €0.10 per strong nudge. Compare with Godling's €0.05 credit.
- **Influence that decays.** Personality drifts back to neutral unless the god keeps reinforcing it. This is a model for viewer-driven conditions whose effect on the agent's mood or habits fades.
- **Context-gated influence.** Heroes ignore the god during fights, trading, resting or in town. Godling could likewise let events "land" only when the builder is between tasks, without breaking its current plan.
- **Diary = content engine.** First-person, past-tense, intelligence-appropriate lines, curated by vote, are the core entertainment; field notes in a consistent voice are the Godling analog. The Relic "Flattery" shows the narration voice itself can be a reward.
- **Daily cadence objects.** A newspaper, a Daily Forecast that tweaks rules, a daily ad with odd trade-offs, festivals and anniversaries give reasons to check in that do not touch the hero's autonomy.
- **1,000-unit long goals.** Temple, ark, pairs and savings are slow visible bars lasting months to years. They map naturally to Godling's towers and quarry goals as progress viewers can follow over weeks.
- **Free charges for rituals.** Free charges given for social rituals (birthday godvoice, invites, three-day streaks) and sales (Black Friday 2x) are Godville's main growth and engagement levers. In 2026, though, the developers admit "the game doesn't advertise itself" and the community is shrinking.
- **Opt-out purity.** "Pure ZPG" and a disable-the-remote option let purists watch without any influence. This is a credibility signal that influence is optional.
- **Watch-out.** A monthly paid accelerator (Blessing, 60 charges, 1.5x gathering) drew "pay to win" remarks. Anything that speeds Godling's progress for credits risks the same perception.

### Gaps
- No Godville data on paying conversion, ARPPU, or how often gods actually intervene per day, so the value of "rationed nudges" cannot be quantified from Godville alone.
