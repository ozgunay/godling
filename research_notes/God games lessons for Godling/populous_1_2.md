# Populous (1989), Populous II (1991) and their expansions, plus Godus (2012–2015) as a cautionary tale

Source-quality note for the report writer: Wikipedia, Jimmy Maher's *Digital Antiquarian* (filfre.net), contemporary magazine reviews (Commodore User 1989), a 2006 Sega-16 review, a GOG forum thread, a 1996 German fan-magazine tips file, and a fan-made Populous II guide (cyberly.org) were read in full. Items marked "(search snippet)" come from search-engine summaries of pages I could not open, so treat them as lower confidence. The original 1989 manual PDF (ts.popre.net) was too large to fetch (over 10 MB), and the Mega Drive manual mirror needed a login. So I found **no numeric mana costs or mana maximum from an official source**. See the Gaps sections.

---

## 1. History and business: team, development, publisher, dates, platforms, price, sales, Bullfrog's rescue, expansions, awards, re-releases

### Takeaway
Populous was made for about £20,000 by a tiny, nearly broke Guildford studio. Its core was Molyneux, Corpes and Donkin, with Edgar handling business. EA published it in March 1989 on a 10–12% royalty that Molyneux later called "pretty atrocious". It still turned Bullfrog into Britain's leading studio. Sales claims rise over time from 1 million (by 1990) to 3 million (1997) to 4 million (2001 and 2012). None of these figures is audited.

### Cited Findings
**Company and team**
- Peter Molyneux and Les Edgar founded Bullfrog in 1987. It began as a brand of their earlier firm Taurus Impact Systems, which wrote Commodore 64 business software. Commodore had reportedly mistaken Taurus for Torus, a networking company, which is how the separate Bullfrog entity came about — [Wikipedia: Bullfrog Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullfrog_Productions)
- Before Populous, Molyneux and Edgar were "running out of money" and Edgar suggested closing the company. Molyneux then came up with the Populous idea (Wikipedia, citing Retro Gamer) — [Wikipedia: Bullfrog Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullfrog_Productions)
- Molyneux said that before Populous, Bullfrog was not earning enough to pay Glenn Corpes, let alone himself. He describes his earlier life as heavy debt and obscurity, and says his life changed over about six weeks — [Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/features/how-peter-molyneux-hooked-japan-with-populous)
- Molyneux's first game, *Entrepreneur* (1983), sold exactly two copies — [Maher, "Bullfrog after Populous" (2020)](https://www.filfre.net/?p=4362)
- Core team and roles per Maher:
  - Molyneux: design and programming.
  - Glenn Corpes: artist, and author of the isometric block-landscape routine.
  - Kevin Donkin: design and programming.
  - Les Edgar: office manager, who pitched the game to about a dozen publishers.
  - Shaun Cooper (tester) and Andy Jones (artist/tester) appear in the team photo.
  - Source: [Maher, "Peter Molyneux's Kingdom in a Box" (30 Jun 2016)](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/)
- Wikipedia credits Molyneux, Corpes, Adrian Moore, Kevin Donkin and Les Edgar as designers, and David Hanlon and Rob Hubbard as composers. It gives a **budget of £20,000** — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game))
- Corpes has repeatedly said the core landscape code was in place before Molyneux got involved, so authorship is disputed (search snippet citing Wikipedia, NowGamer and Retro Gamer #160, Oct 2016) — [Wikipedia: Glenn Corpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Corpes)

**Development story**
- Corpes's isometric block art was inspired by David Braben's *Virus*. Molyneux first built a landscape with wandering "peeps". Raising terrain was added to help them move, and house-building followed — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game))
- When Corpes showed the raise/lower landscape routine, Molyneux proposed putting a thousand people running around on it. Raising and lowering land was partly a workaround: Molyneux says he could not write a proper pathfinding algorithm — [Maher 2016](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/)
- Molyneux and Corpes played a match every day and tweaked the game the next. Typical game length fell from several hours to well under one — [Maher 2016](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/)
- Bullfrog promised EA a finished game for Christmas 1988. The game was multiplayer-only for many months (1200-baud modem or cable), and the single-player Conquest mode was added afterwards because multiplayer-only was not commercially viable — [Maher 2016](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/)
- Development took about nine months. The game shipped after only two days of testing — [Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/features/how-peter-molyneux-hooked-japan-with-populous)
- **Knights** were added to stop games stalling, because otherwise there was no way to win — [Maher 2016](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/). A final battle (Armageddon) was added because test games ran for hours with no firm end — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game))
- Testers asked for a cheat to skip to the end of the 500 levels, and Bullfrog realised there was no ending. An interstitial screen was repurposed as the final screen — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game)). Molyneux says he finished the ending about 12 hours before EA needed to bug-test it (2025 Edge interview, as reported by GamesRadar) — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/strategy/years-before-fable-peter-molyneux-finished-making-proto-city-builder-populous-in-12-hours-because-he-forgot-to-do-it-sooner-there-was-no-end-of-the-game)
- Bullfrog prototyped a Lego board-game version. Lego came to look at it, but it was never produced — [Game Informer, GDC 2011 recap](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2011/03/03/gdc-looks-at-a-classic-populous.aspx). Molyneux said it did not help balance the game but gave a useful media angle. A water test failed because Lego is not watertight — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game))
- During development Molyneux had a broken monitor, so he wrote code lines under 17 characters long (GDC 2011) — [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2011/03/03/gdc-looks-at-a-classic-populous.aspx)
- Molyneux gave a GDC "Classic Game Postmortem" on Populous in 2011, and it is free on GDC's YouTube channel (search snippet) — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/audio/video-peter-molyneux-shares-a-game-dev-postmortem-of-i-populous-i-)

**Publisher deal and money**
- Bullfrog demoed to more than a dozen publishers. EA signed it because it had a gap in its spring schedule — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game)). Lego was among those that turned it down — [Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/features/how-peter-molyneux-hooked-japan-with-populous)
- Contract terms:
  - **10% royalty, rising to 12% after one million units**, with a small or almost no upfront payment.
  - Molyneux later called it "pretty atrocious".
  - Sources: [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game)); [The Daily Gamepad, GDC 2011 write-up (search snippet)](https://thedailygamepad.net/?p=16503)
- Payment timing:
  - The contract delayed payment to the developer until nine months after release, and Molyneux paid his mortgage on credit cards in the meantime — [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2011/03/03/gdc-looks-at-a-classic-populous.aspx)
  - The first royalty cheque was **£13,000** and the second **£250,000** — [Maher 2016](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/)
  - Les Edgar recalled that royalties were paid one quarter in arrears and that they did not expect any (search snippet) — [Where Were They Now: Bullfrog](https://whereweretheynow.blogspot.com/2023/11/bullfrog.html)
- **Claim (Molyneux, GDC 2011): Populous made up about a third of EA's revenue in 1989** — [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2011/03/03/gdc-looks-at-a-classic-populous.aspx); Wikipedia calls it "nearly a third", Molyneux's estimate — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game)). This is unaudited.
- Japanese licensing went through Imagineer (Super Famicom and Mega Drive), at royalties of "around ten or eleven percent" per Molyneux. Imagineer later published Powermonger and Populous II in Japan — [Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/features/how-peter-molyneux-hooked-japan-with-populous)

**Release dates and platforms**
- Release dates:
  - Amiga: **March 1989**
  - Atari ST: April 1989
  - PC Engine (Japan): 5 April 1990
  - Mega Drive/Genesis: 1990–91
  - Also released on MS-DOS, Acorn Archimedes, Master System, Game Boy, PC-98, X68000, SNES and Mac.
  - Source: [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game))
- Commodore User reviewed the Amiga 500 version in issue #67, dated 1 March 1989. It mentions two-player play by modem, or Amiga-to-ST link — [Commodore User via everygamegoing](https://everygamegoing.com/larticle/populous/54589)
- The Super Famicom launched in Japan at the end of 1990, with Populous among its few launch-window titles. Imagineer's version added a "Nintendo World", mouse and pig races, and exclusive levels such as "Bit World" — [Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/features/how-peter-molyneux-hooked-japan-with-populous)
- Molyneux says Populous topped the charts in Europe, the UK and the US within about two or three weeks of release. He also says Bullfrog did not know how many copies were selling worldwide — [Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/features/how-peter-molyneux-hooked-japan-with-populous)

**Sales claims (each labelled with source and date)**

**How it saved and grew Bullfrog**
- Within months, Bullfrog went from a cramped office with no toilet to Britain's most successful and respected games studio — [Maher 2020](https://www.filfre.net/?p=4362)
- After Populous, Bullfrog moved to Surrey Research Park, Guildford, with about 20 employees (NowGamer 2012) — [Wikipedia: Bullfrog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullfrog_Productions)
- Bullfrog had about 40 staff by 1993 — [Maher 2020](https://www.filfre.net/?p=4362)
- Staff grew from 35 to 60 before EA's acquisition and reached about 150 within months after it (Retro Gamer 1995) — [Wikipedia: Bullfrog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullfrog_Productions)
- EA bought Bullfrog in early January 1995. The rumoured price was **$44 million (£29 million)** (The Observer, 5 Mar 1995), and it is unconfirmed — [Wikipedia: Bullfrog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullfrog_Productions)
- *Powermonger* (1990) was made under EA pressure for a follow-up — [Wikipedia: Bullfrog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullfrog_Productions). Maher dates it to late 1990, about 18 months after Populous — [Maher 2020](https://www.filfre.net/?p=4362)

- The "4 million" figure is often repeated but traces back to press claims (Wired/BBC 2001; NowGamer 2012), not audited sales data. "A third of EA's revenue" is Molyneux's own recollection.

## 2. Structure and loop: 500 / 1,000 worlds, world codes, Conquest vs custom, win conditions, two-god opposition, session length, difficulty

### Takeaway
Each Populous match is a real-time duel between two gods on one isometric map. You win by wiping out the rival's followers, usually finished with Armageddon. The campaign ("Conquest") is a chain of 500 procedurally generated worlds unlocked by passwords, and bigger wins skip more worlds. Populous II scales this to 1,000 worlds against a ladder of Greek gods ending with Zeus, and adds RPG-style experience between maps.

### Cited Findings
- Each level is a land area containing the player's and the enemy's followers. The goal is to grow your followers until they can wipe out the enemy — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game)). You control the Blue people and the rival god controls the Red. You win by exterminating every Red follower — [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/bullfrog-time-machine-populous/)
- Conquest mode has **500 procedurally generated worlds**. Every world except the first is unlocked by a procedurally generated password, and a bigger victory skips more levels — [Maher 2016](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/). The console version is described as "over 500 passworded levels" — [Sega-16](https://www.sega-16.com/?p=7591). Wikipedia says both "more than 500" and "500" — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game))
- Map rules vary per world:
  - On the first map ("Genesis") the player can use all spells and the CPU can cast none. On later maps the restrictions differ, and the CPU may be able to cast spells.
  - Depending on map settings, you can alter land only where one of your settlements is on screen, or also where one of your followers is visible. This stops players flooding enemy houses at the start.
  - Source: [GOG forum thread](https://gog.com/forum/populous_series/trying_to_understand_this_game_populous_1)
- Some spells are switched off at the start of some levels — [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/bullfrog-time-machine-populous/)
- Difficulty: the AI gets extra powers, extra speed and handicaps at higher levels. The reviewer says it "cheats" at higher levels — [Sega-16](https://www.sega-16.com/?p=7591)
- Session length: tuning cut typical games from several hours to well under one — [Maher 2016](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/)
- Repetition: one retrospective reviewer played five levels and found them identical apart from topography — [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/bullfrog-time-machine-populous/). Maher names procedurally generated levels, grinding, and being most fun in the first hour as recurring Bullfrog flaws — [Maher 2020](https://www.filfre.net/?p=4362)
- Landscapes include desert, rock and lava, snow and ice, and grass ("green ones which look like Kent"). Terrain type affects how followers develop — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game)); [Commodore User](https://everygamegoing.com/larticle/populous/54589)
- The interface is a "tabletop" with command icons, a world map drawn as a book (the "Book of Worlds") and a mana slider — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game))
- Two-player play over serial or modem was a core feature — [Lilura blog](https://lilura1.blogspot.com/2022/02/Populous-Amiga-1989.html); [Commodore User](https://everygamegoing.com/larticle/populous/54589). The Genesis/Mega Drive port lacks two-player — [Sega-16](https://www.sega-16.com/?p=7591)
- **Populous II structure**:
  - The player is a demigod, a child of Zeus, who must defeat Greek deities one at a time before facing Zeus for a place on Olympus. There are **1,000 maps**. Performance determines how many maps are skipped — [Wikipedia: Populous II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_II:_Trials_of_the_Olympian_Gods)
  - Maher says the player faces **35 Greek gods** ending with Zeus, across more than 1,000 procedurally generated levels — [Maher 2020](https://www.filfre.net/?p=4362)
  - Completing a map awards **1–5 experience points** that boost the six power categories — [Wikipedia: Populous II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_II:_Trials_of_the_Olympian_Gods)
  - Fan guide detail: you start with 5 "experience bolts" to distribute, then earn 1–5 per map, win or lose, based only on score. Score depends mostly on how many interventions you use, at roughly 13–15k points per bolt — [cyberly.org Populous II guide](https://www.cyberly.org/game-cheats/pc-walkthroughs/populous-ii-guide.html)
  - Each conquest map shows the AI's **speed** (e.g. "Very Slow", "Sleepy", "Very Fast") and **aggression** (e.g. "Pacifist", "Killer") — [cyberly.org](https://www.cyberly.org/game-cheats/pc-walkthroughs/populous-ii-guide.html)
  - Minor gods with signature behaviours: the Fire god casts many fire columns; the Earth god causes quake-like damage; the Plant god grows trees; the Wind god drops whirlwinds; the Water god sinks 2x2 squares — [cyberly.org](https://www.cyberly.org/game-cheats/pc-walkthroughs/populous-ii-guide.html)
  - Late-game escalator: when one side controls about **75% of the map**, indestructible Greek monsters such as the Colossus or Medusa are unleashed — [Wikipedia: Populous II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_II:_Trials_of_the_Olympian_Gods)

### Inferences
- The structure is two nested loops: a 10–60 minute real-time match, and a long password-gated ladder in which winning big skips content. The skip mechanic shortens the 500/1,000-world campaign for strong players without the designers authoring a difficulty curve, because difficulty comes from map rule-sets and AI parameters.
- The two "end-forcing" devices, knights and Armageddon, were both added late to fix stalemates. This is a recurring problem for systemic sandbox games.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the exact Conquest password/world-code format, or how many worlds a given score skips (no manual access).
- I could not confirm the custom-game options list (I recall "water is fatal" or "swamps bottomless" style toggles, but did not verify it). The original manual would settle this.
- Populous II opponent roster: I did not verify Maher's "35 gods" against the manual, or the full list of deities and their order.

## 3. Mechanics in Populous (1989): land, flat ground, settlements, Papal Magnet, leaders, knights, behaviour modes, interventions, Armageddon

### Takeaway
The god never commands individuals. You shape terrain, and followers decide for themselves where to settle. The amount of contiguous flat land around a site sets how big and advanced their building becomes, up to a castle on a roughly 5x5 flat area. You steer people with one attractor (the Papal Magnet/ankh) and four global behaviour modes. Eight powers sit on the mana bar in a fixed escalating order, from raise/lower land up to Armageddon.

### Cited Findings
**Land and building**
- Raising and lowering land creates flat ground for building, and land can also be removed around enemy followers — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game)). One retrospective estimates terraforming takes "maybe 80%" of play time — [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/bullfrog-time-machine-populous/)
- Followers look for nearby flat land and build the largest structure it can fit. The bigger the available land, the bigger the building, which yields more mana but develops more slowly — [Sega-16](https://www.sega-16.com/?p=7591)
- Settlers' buildings scale in size and technology with the surrounding land. Mountains, ditches, water and rocks hinder the community — [Commodore User, Mar 1989](https://everygamegoing.com/larticle/populous/54589)
- The 1989 manual reportedly says small huts can be supported by one or two areas of land, while a castle needs large areas (search snippet of manual) — [Populous 1989 Manual (PDF)](https://ts.popre.net/archive/Downloads/Docs/Populous_1989_Manual.pdf)
- Castle footprint:
  - A castle needs a **5x5 contiguous flat area** — [Lilura blog](https://lilura1.blogspot.com/2022/02/Populous-Amiga-1989.html)
  - Another source puts it as "something like four tiles of flat land in each direction" — [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/bullfrog-time-machine-populous/)
- Flatter land produces larger, more advanced fortresses that grow slowly but are harder to conquer. Undulating land produces small, primitive settlements that expand fast but defend poorly — [Lilura blog](https://lilura1.blogspot.com/2022/02/Populous-Amiga-1989.html)
- Better settlements grow population faster and produce stronger followers. Settlements periodically spawn walkers that go off to found new settlements — [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/bullfrog-time-machine-populous/). Each walker's strength and health depend on the building that produced him — [Sega-16](https://www.sega-16.com/?p=7591)
- Castles build up mana quickly but take a while to fill, so a 1996 tips file advises not building too many — [Fanclub tips, 1996](https://discmaster.textfiles.com/view/10989/FANCLUB1.ISO/losung/populous.txt)
- The world has seven levels of verticality and eight-way auto-scrolling — [Lilura blog](https://lilura1.blogspot.com/2022/02/Populous-Amiga-1989.html)

**Behaviour modes, Papal Magnet, leaders, knights**
- Four follower modes:
  - fight
  - "buddy up" (gather / join forces)
  - settle
  - go to the Papal Magnet
  - Settle is the usual choice — [GOG forum](https://gog.com/forum/populous_series/trying_to_understand_this_game_populous_1)
- Walkers that cross paths combine into stronger walkers — [Lilura blog](https://lilura1.blogspot.com/2022/02/Populous-Amiga-1989.html). In a fight between walkers, the winner depends on strength and on the tech level of the settlement each came from — [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/bullfrog-time-machine-populous/)
- Papal Magnet (ankh):
  - The first follower to touch it becomes leader — [Maher 2016](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/)
  - You can move the magnet only if you have a leader. If the leader dies, the magnet stays at that spot, and in magnet mode followers head there until one becomes the new leader — [GOG forum](https://gog.com/forum/populous_series/trying_to_understand_this_game_populous_1)
  - Pulling followers into the leader strengthens him but reduces total population — [Sega-16](https://www.sega-16.com/?p=7591)
  - Commodore User called the magnet the symbol of the people's faith — [Commodore User](https://everygamegoing.com/larticle/populous/54589)
- **Knight**:
  - Turns the leader into a knight who roams independently, killing enemies and burning settlements and crops.
  - A knight dies to a swamp, or when the ground under him is sunk below sea level.
  - Sources: [Sega-16](https://www.sega-16.com/?p=7591); [GOG forum](https://gog.com/forum/populous_series/trying_to_understand_this_game_populous_1)
  - Knights can merge into "juggernauts", a behaviour the coders imposed because of sprite-count limits — [Lilura blog](https://lilura1.blogspot.com/2022/02/Populous-Amiga-1989.html)
  - The 1996 tips file advises knighting repeatedly until there are "three or four knights", then moving the magnet into the cleared area so your people resettle it — [Fanclub tips](https://discmaster.textfiles.com/view/10989/FANCLUB1.ISO/losung/populous.txt)

**Divine interventions (eight powers in total)**
- **Order on the mana bar**: raise/lower land, then Papal Magnet, Earthquake, Swamp, Knight, Volcano, Flood, Armageddon. A power can be used only once you can afford it — [GOG forum](https://gog.com/forum/populous_series/trying_to_understand_this_game_populous_1). There are eight powers — [Lilura blog](https://lilura1.blogspot.com/2022/02/Populous-Amiga-1989.html); [Wikipedia: Populous II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_II:_Trials_of_the_Olympian_Gods) ("eight in Populous")
- **Earthquake**: scrambles terrain, usually dropping it toward sea level, and can knock castles back to tents — [GOG forum](https://gog.com/forum/populous_series/trying_to_understand_this_game_populous_1). It destroys buildings and reshapes land — [Commodore User](https://everygamegoing.com/larticle/populous/54589)
- **Swamp**: anyone who walks on it drowns — [Commodore User](https://everygamegoing.com/larticle/populous/54589). It is effective against enemy leaders and knights — [GOG forum](https://gog.com/forum/populous_series/trying_to_understand_this_game_populous_1)
- **Volcano**: raises the land in an area by **1 to 10 levels** into a rugged mountain, and spews rocks — [Commodore User](https://everygamegoing.com/larticle/populous/54589); [Fanclub tips](https://discmaster.textfiles.com/view/10989/FANCLUB1.ISO/losung/populous.txt)
- **Flood**: raises the sea level by one step across the whole world, which is equivalent to lowering all terrain one level, so flat land just above sea level goes under — [Fanclub tips](https://discmaster.textfiles.com/view/10989/FANCLUB1.ISO/losung/populous.txt); [GOG forum](https://gog.com/forum/populous_series/trying_to_understand_this_game_populous_1)
- **Armageddon**: both Papal Magnets are placed at the centre of the world, and both populations march there and fight to the death — [Commodore User](https://everygamegoing.com/larticle/populous/54589); [GOG forum](https://gog.com/forum/populous_series/trying_to_understand_this_game_populous_1)
- Wikipedia lists earthquakes, floods, swamps, volcanoes and knights as powers unlocked by rising mana — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game))

### Inferences
- Flat land acts as the throttle on everything. Terraforming is cheap, constant and low-drama, while the scarce, dramatic powers sit at the top of the bar. The trade-off of many small fast-growing huts versus a few slow, strong castles is the main economic decision, and the player makes it purely through terrain.
- Both flood and earthquake are effectively "terrain as weapon", turning the same verb (change land height) into offence.

### Gaps
- Exact building tier names and counts: one search summary mentioned "tent through huts and towers to a castle", but I did not verify the names or the full ladder against the manual.
- Exact rules for when a settlement spills out a walker, such as a population threshold per building type, were not found.
- The effect of "fight" vs "gather" mode in detail was not found beyond the mode names.

## 4. Mechanics in Populous II: categories of power, effects, heroes, experience and progression

### Takeaway
Populous II grows the eight powers into about 29–35 effects in six elemental families. Each family is capped by a Greek hero that the leader can be turned into: Perseus (people), Adonis (vegetation), Hercules (earth), Achilles (fire), Odysseus (air) and Helen of Troy (water). Between worlds, experience points earned from score make each family cheaper, stronger or longer-lasting, which gives the god a persistent RPG-like growth curve.

### Cited Findings
- Power count:
  - **29 divine powers (30 in the PC version)**, versus eight in Populous. The development section quotes Molyneux giving **35** effects in total.
  - Categories are given as earth, water, wind/air, fire, plants and people. The development section also says the game was later split into five (earth, air, fire, water, people), so the sources disagree.
  - Source: [Wikipedia: Populous II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_II:_Trials_of_the_Olympian_Gods)
- Each category has a hero that transforms the leader into a legendary Greek figure — [Wikipedia: Populous II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_II:_Trials_of_the_Olympian_Gods)
- **Fungus** spreads by the rules of Conway's Game of Life and kills walkers and settlements — [Wikipedia: Populous II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_II:_Trials_of_the_Olympian_Gods); [cyberly.org](https://www.cyberly.org/game-cheats/pc-walkthroughs/populous-ii-guide.html)

**Effect list by family** (all from the fan-made guide at [cyberly.org](https://www.cyberly.org/game-cheats/pc-walkthroughs/populous-ii-guide.html); unofficial, and names may vary by version)
- **People**:
  - Raise/Lower Land; Place Papal Magnet.
  - **Perseus** (tier-3 hero): same strength as the source leader; avoids obstacles, so he is effectively immune to swamp, earthquake and baptismal fonts; weak to lava.
  - **Plague**: an infected settlement or walker contributes little mana.
  - **Armageddon**: turns all settlements and walkers into fighters who battle until one side remains; plagued units disintegrate.
- **Vegetation**:
  - Plant Trees.
  - Plant Flowers: overwrites swamp, fungus, quake and volcano damage, so it acts as a repair tool.
  - Swamps; "shallow" swamps vanish after use.
  - Fungus.
  - **Adonis** (tier-5 hero): splits in two after each victory; immune to fungus and swamp.
- **Earth**:
  - Build Road: walkers prefer roads and move faster on them.
  - Build City Wall: limited count; must extend an existing wall; blocks most walkers.
  - Earthquake.
  - Batholith: raises land and creates rocks, which are capped.
  - **Hercules** (tier-5 hero): twice the source leader's strength; immune to earthquake.
- **Fire**:
  - Fire Column: random, tends uphill.
  - Rain of Fire: can destroy settlements outright.
  - **Achilles** (tier-3 hero): immune to all fire.
  - Volcano: creates magma and spawns rain of fire, fire columns and lava; lava turns to basalt on water.
  - Divine Fire: eight pillars of fire radiate from your leader.
- **Air**:
  - Lightning Bolt: key for killing heroes and new leaders at the magnet.
  - Whirlwind: knocks units' "technology" down to fist level.
  - Storm.
  - **Odysseus** (tier-4 hero): twice as fast; immune to lightning and whirlwind.
  - Hurricane: blows walkers, settlements and heroes past a line.
- **Water**:
  - Basalt: impassable shoreline that stops tidal waves.
  - Whirlpool: turns land to water.
  - Baptismal Fonts: walkers or heroes that fall in switch sides, which often creates back-and-forth loops.
  - **Helen of Troy** (tier-4 hero): charms walkers and settlements into following her; not immune to interventions.
  - Tidal Wave: four waves that destroy land.

**Other details**
- A separate fan review calls Helen the most overpowered hero (search snippet) — [bestretrogames blog](https://bestretrogames.blogspot.com/2016/03)
- Experience bolts change specific effects. For example, they make Plague, Swamp, Fungus and Batholith cheaper, lengthen Earthquake and Storm, and make heroes faster. The effect on raise/lower land is "so subtle" it may not matter — [cyberly.org](https://www.cyberly.org/game-cheats/pc-walkthroughs/populous-ii-guide.html)
- Caps and limits:
  - Trees, rocks and walls each have map caps.
  - Intervention caps are shared between the two gods, so AI spam can lock you out of an effect.
  - Population above about 50% of the "stadium" stops "sprogging" (reproduction) and stops Adonis splitting.
  - The optional "Computer Assistant" will right-click and destroy your own settlements on maps where sprogging is disabled.
  - Source: [cyberly.org](https://www.cyberly.org/game-cheats/pc-walkthroughs/populous-ii-guide.html)
- Other player tools include building roads and walls, evicting a resident with a right-click, and an avatar that earns experience "like in a role-playing game" — [Dazeland](https://dazeland.com/en/Amiga/Populous2.html)
- Population and its consequences: manna is generated by population over time, and repeated use of devastating powers drains the reservoir. Monsters (Colossus, Medusa) appear once a side controls about 75% of the map — [Wikipedia: Populous II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_II:_Trials_of_the_Olympian_Gods)

### Inferences
- The hero "tiers" (3, 4, 5) put each family's ultimate power at a different mana threshold. Each family gets one agent-like unit (a hero) that acts independently once created, a direct descendant of the Populous knight.
- Several effects create counterplay loops: basalt blocks tidal waves, flowers repair damage, and baptismal fonts flip units back and forth. Caps on trees, rocks, walls and shared interventions are explicit anti-spam rate limits.

### Gaps
- I could not access an official manual for exact hero gating, so tier numbers come from one fan guide.
- I found no confirmed list of god rank titles. The demigod becomes an Olympian at the end, but I found no named intermediate ranks.
- Unresolved: whether "people, vegetation, earth, air, fire, water" exactly matches the shipped UI. Wikipedia's development section mentions a five-family version.

## 5. Mana economy: generation, maximum, costs and unlock thresholds, and the strategic loop

### Takeaway
In both games mana is produced passively by your population: more people and bigger settlements give more mana. It fills a single bar, and powers become usable as the bar crosses their position: cheap terraforming first, then the magnet, then disasters, then Armageddon. This creates the loop of shaping land, people growing, more mana, and bigger miracles. Each disaster spends the bar down, so destructive powers are paced by refill time. I found **no official numeric costs or maximum**.

### Cited Findings
- Houses create followers, more followers raise mana, and rising mana unlocks earthquakes, floods, swamps, volcanoes and knights — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game))
- Mana grows as walkers are created and as buildings enlarge. Mana lets you raise or lower land and place the Papal Magnet — [Commodore User, Mar 1989](https://everygamegoing.com/larticle/populous/54589)
- Building size and mana timing:
  - Larger buildings (from more flat land) yield more mana but develop more slowly — [Sega-16](https://www.sega-16.com/?p=7591)
  - Low-level huts are the key early mana source, and castles pay off in the long run — [Sega-16](https://www.sega-16.com/?p=7591)
  - Castles build up mana quickly once established — [Fanclub tips](https://discmaster.textfiles.com/view/10989/FANCLUB1.ISO/losung/populous.txt)
- Spending:
  - Walkers generate mana once they have built, and the god spends it on terraforming, knighting and interventions. Interventions cost much more than terraforming — [Lilura blog](https://lilura1.blogspot.com/2022/02/Populous-Amiga-1989.html)
  - Raising and lowering land itself costs mana — [Maher 2016](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/)
- The bar order is raise/lower, magnet, earthquake, swamp, knight, volcano, flood, Armageddon, and each power is usable only when affordable — [GOG forum](https://gog.com/forum/populous_series/trying_to_understand_this_game_populous_1)
- The Papal Magnet route trades population for power: merging followers into a strong leader lowers total population, and so lowers mana income — [Sega-16](https://www.sega-16.com/?p=7591)
- **Populous II**: the categories link to a global "manna meter" that must be filled to a certain level to enable specific powers. Manna comes from population existing over time, and a larger population generates more. Using a power spends manna, and repeatedly using the devastating ones empties the reservoir — [Wikipedia: Populous II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_II:_Trials_of_the_Olympian_Gods)
- Mana-denial tools in Populous II:
  - Plague-infected settlements and walkers contribute little mana — [cyberly.org](https://www.cyberly.org/game-cheats/pc-walkthroughs/populous-ii-guide.html)
  - Lightning a new leader at the enemy's magnet suppresses their population and mana — [cyberly.org](https://www.cyberly.org/game-cheats/pc-walkthroughs/populous-ii-guide.html)
  - Experience bolts make many effects cheaper, so the god's mana efficiency grows across the campaign — [cyberly.org](https://www.cyberly.org/game-cheats/pc-walkthroughs/populous-ii-guide.html)

### Inferences
- The mana bar works as both currency and a tier gate. A power's position on the bar is both its unlock threshold and roughly its price. Because income comes from the god's own thriving population, the "budget" for catastrophe is earned through stewardship rather than bought.
- Since disasters drain the bar and refill depends on population, a god who over-uses destruction slows their own escalation. This is a built-in pacing governor.

### Gaps
- **No numeric mana costs, generation rates or mana maximum were found** for Populous or Populous II in any accessible source. The original manual (too large to fetch) or a disassembly or fan wiki would be needed.
- Whether mana in Populous 1 scales with population count, with building tier, or with "tilled" land area: sources mention all three loosely (followers, building size, "castles build mana quickly"). I found no exact formula.

## 6. Follower AI and rival-god AI

### Takeaway
Followers are simple autonomous agents. They wander, look for the flattest nearby land, build the biggest structure it allows, breed, emit new walkers, merge on contact and fight on contact. The god influences them only through terrain, one magnet and four global modes. The rival god AI was written from scratch late in development and is widely described as predictable and exploitable. It makes up for this with speed, extra powers and handicaps on harder worlds.

### Cited Findings
- Raising and lowering land was partly a substitute for pathfinding: Molyneux says he could not write a proper pathfinding algorithm, so the player clears the way instead — [Maher 2016](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/); [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game)) (terrain raising added "to help them move")
- In Settle mode, followers look for nearby flat land and build the largest structure that fits — [Sega-16](https://www.sega-16.com/?p=7591). Settlements periodically spawn walkers that found new settlements — [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/bullfrog-time-machine-populous/)
- Walkers merge when they cross. Combat is decided by strength and by the source settlement's tech level — [Lilura blog](https://lilura1.blogspot.com/2022/02/Populous-Amiga-1989.html); [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/bullfrog-time-machine-populous/)
- A retrospective criticises the population as "uninteractive" — [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/bullfrog-time-machine-populous/)
- In Populous II, walkers prefer roads except under the magnet's pull — [cyberly.org](https://www.cyberly.org/game-cheats/pc-walkthroughs/populous-ii-guide.html)
- The rival AI had to be created from scratch during development, after the game had been multiplayer-only — [Maher 2016](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/)
- Observed AI behaviour and weaknesses:
  - It is predictable, uses disasters poorly, and tends to throw followers one after another at your knight — [Sega-16](https://www.sega-16.com/?p=7591)
  - It has exploitable patterns and gets extra powers, speed and handicaps on harder levels — [Sega-16](https://www.sega-16.com/?p=7591)
  - It does not anticipate floods, so a player who builds high can drown most enemy people while staying safe — [Fanclub tips, 1996](https://discmaster.textfiles.com/view/10989/FANCLUB1.ISO/losung/populous.txt)
  - It tries to grow and conquer at the same time, so well-placed disasters slow it — [Fanclub tips](https://discmaster.textfiles.com/view/10989/FANCLUB1.ISO/losung/populous.txt)
  - After a swamp kills its leader, it does not clear the swamp, and its followers keep walking into it — [GOG forum](https://gog.com/forum/populous_series/trying_to_understand_this_game_populous_1)
  - The CPU casts spells only on some maps, and one player believes it never casts Armageddon — [GOG forum](https://gog.com/forum/populous_series/trying_to_understand_this_game_populous_1)
- Populous II AI:
  - Speed and aggression are set per map.
  - The AI and the helper "Computer Assistant" mostly ignore the top edge of the map.
  - Against Very Fast AI, avoid slow interventions such as roads and fungus.
  - Source: [cyberly.org](https://www.cyberly.org/game-cheats/pc-walkthroughs/populous-ii-guide.html)

### Inferences
- The follower model is a cheap emergent-behaviour design: a few local rules (seek flat land, build to fit, spawn, merge, fight) plus global player levers (terrain, magnet, mode). It produces readable mass behaviour without per-unit commands.
- The rival AI's documented failures are failures to *remember and react to hazards*: it does not anticipate floods and does not remove a swamp that killed its leader. Hazard memory and anticipation are what a scripted opponent most visibly lacks.

### Gaps
- I found no technical description of the rival god's decision logic, such as how it chooses where to flatten or when to cast.
- No source described how a walker chooses a direction when no flat land is visible (random walk vs gradient).

## 7. Influence ("the first god game") and what reviewers praised or criticised

### Takeaway
Populous is widely called the first or original god game. The term was coined by ACE reviewer Bob Wade, and the genre was defined as direct control over terrain but only indirect control over people. Critics in 1989–91 praised its originality, tone and addictiveness, with scores in the 90s. The recurring criticisms were repetitive levels, a clumsy icon interface, weak AI, and limited depth after the first hour.

### Cited Findings
- Bob Wade of *ACE* coined "god game" in his review, calling Populous the best game he had ever played — [Maher 2016](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/)
- The media grouped Populous with SimCity as "god games", which give direct control over terrain but only indirect control over people — [Maher 2020](https://www.filfre.net/?p=4362). The Amiga versions of Populous and SimCity came out at nearly the same time — [Maher 2016](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/)
- Other "first" labels:
  - Widely regarded as "the first PC God game" — [Game Informer 2011](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2011/03/03/gdc-looks-at-a-classic-populous.aspx)
  - GOG markets it as "the original god-game" — [GOG](https://www.gog.com/en/game/populous)
  - CGW (1996) called it the "father of real-time strategy games" — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game))
- A fan critic credits Populous with formalising the god-game genre and influencing X-COM and Civilization. This is opinion — [Lilura blog](https://lilura1.blogspot.com/2022/02/Populous-Amiga-1989.html)
- Its independent, living systems run through Bullfrog's later games: Syndicate, Magic Carpet and Dungeon Keeper — [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/bullfrog-time-machine-populous/). The engine was reused with 3D graphics in Powermonger (1990) — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game))
- **Praise** (scores and comments):
  - CVG (Amiga) 96%; Your Amiga 93%; Zero 92%; ST/Amiga Format 92%; MegaTech (MD) 91%; Raze (MD) 89%; Dragon (DOS) 5/5; Famitsu (SNES) 31/40 — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game))
  - Commodore User gave it 94% (Playability 95%, Lastability 93%), calling it the best game the reviewer had yet played on the Amiga — [Commodore User](https://everygamegoing.com/larticle/populous/54589)
  - CGW's Biff Kritzen (1989) praised its light-hearted tone and graphics, and PC Gamer US (1994) called it "unbelievably addictive fun" — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game))
- **Criticism**:
  - Games International gave it 2/5 and called it "repetitive" (John Harrington). Orson Scott Card (*Compute!*) criticised the interface but praised the world-creation concept. CGW's 1993 retrospective gave it 3/5 — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game))
  - Sega-16 (2006, 8/10) cites predictable AI that cheats at higher levels, slowdown and jumpy controls when busy, and a soundtrack that is mostly a heartbeat — [Sega-16](https://www.sega-16.com/?p=7591)
  - Modern retrospectives cite a wasteful interface, awkward controls, identical levels and an "uninteractive" population — [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/bullfrog-time-machine-populous/). One critic argues it should have been turn-based, because indirect, icon-driven control ages poorly — [Lilura blog](https://lilura1.blogspot.com/2022/02/Populous-Amiga-1989.html)
- Curiosity: the *Daily Mail* warned that the game's good-versus-evil theme could draw fatwās, in the Satanic Verses era. Nothing came of it — [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game))
- Populous II reception:
  - The One for Amiga Games 96%; Mean Machines Sega 89%; MegaZone 90%; Super Gamer (SNES) 82% — [Wikipedia: Populous II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_II:_Trials_of_the_Olympian_Gods)
  - CGW (1992) called it "instantly absorbing and addictive" — [Wikipedia: Populous II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_II:_Trials_of_the_Olympian_Gods)
  - Maher: "reasonable but unimaginative" — [Maher 2020](https://www.filfre.net/?p=4362)
  - A modern retrospective calls it "pretty much the same game" in nicer packaging — [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/bullfrog-time-machine-populous/)

### Inferences
- The defining design idea was *indirect control*: the player shapes the conditions and the people do the rest. That is what made it feel new in 1989, and it is also the source of its main complaints (lack of agency over individuals, readability problems).

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve Bob Wade's original *ACE* review text, issue number or date.

## 8. Godus (22cans, 2012–2015) as a business cautionary tale: Kickstarter, free-to-play, broken promises, Curiosity

### Takeaway
Godus raised about £526.5k from roughly 17,180 Kickstarter backers in December 2012 on the promise of a reinvented Populous. It shipped first as a Steam Early Access game, then became a DeNA-published free-to-play mobile game. That version was built on gem-purchased skips of long timers, and reviewers said this ruined its best feature, land sculpting. Promised features never arrived. The *Curiosity* winner's "god of gods" role and 1% revenue share were never delivered, because the multiplayer feature the role depended on was never built and Godus never turned a profit.

### Cited Findings
**Studio and Kickstarter**
- 22cans was founded by Molyneux after he left Microsoft in March 2012 and had about 20 staff — [Wikipedia: Godus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godus)
- **Kickstarter**: goal **£450,000**, met on 20 Dec 2012 — [Wikipedia: Godus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godus)
- **Final total and backers** (sources differ slightly):
  - **£526,563 from 17,184 backers** — [Engadget, 21 Dec 2012](https://www.engadget.com/2012/12/21/godus-kickstarter-concludes-at-526k/)
  - £526,543 from 17,183 backers — [Gamewatcher](https://www.gamewatcher.com/news/2012-21-12-hours-remain-for-project-godus-and-survival-rpg-forsaken-fortress-update)
  - Wikipedia gives £526,563 (US$857,139) — [Wikipedia: Godus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godus)
  - One later blog says the goal was £460,000 and the campaign closed on 31 Dec 2012 — [GameQoL](https://www.gameqol.org/failure-to-launch-peter-molyneux-22cans-and-godus). This conflicts with contemporary reports and is probably wrong.
- **Stretch-goal conflict**: Wikipedia says 5 of 6 stretch goals were reached, with Linux and Ouya missed — [Wikipedia: Godus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godus). Shacknews says the total just passed the £525k Linux stretch goal — [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/77170/project-godus-kickstarter-ends-with-117-of-goal)
- The campaign looked likely to fail for much of its run and surged in the final days — [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/77170/project-godus-kickstarter-ends-with-117-of-goal)

**Releases**
- Steam Early Access (Windows/OS X) on 13 Sep 2013; freemium iOS on 7 Aug 2014; Android on 27 Nov 2014 — [Wikipedia: Godus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godus)
- DeNA (Mobage) was announced as mobile publisher in May 2013 for western territories, Japan and Korea (search snippet) — [Engadget, 21 May 2013](https://www.engadget.com/2013-05-21-molyneuxs-godus-goes-mobile-mobage-with-publisher-dena.html)

**Monetisation**
- Gems:
  - Gems were the premium currency, buying instant belief boosts, wheat, ore and "gifts from god" power-ups.
  - **Gem packs ranged from £2.99 for 50 to £69.99 for 1,400**; the reviewer bought a £6.99 pack of 120 to get past the slow early game.
  - Source: an unattributed mirror of what appears to be The Guardian's 18 Aug 2014 review, titled "not godlike yet, but a creative cut above the freemium herd" — [mirror](https://news-myworld.blogspot.com/2014/08/godus-review-not-godlike-yet-but.html); the Guardian date and title are listed at [GameQoL](https://www.gameqol.org/failure-to-launch-peter-molyneux-22cans-and-godus)
- Core loop: shape land into flat areas for houses; group houses into settlements; harvest belief, wheat and ore; spend belief on reshaping terrain; light hillside beacons to unlock territory. Powers, follower attributes and buildings unlock through cards, which need "stickers" from chests or timed Lemmings-style "voyages" — [Guardian review mirror](https://news-myworld.blogspot.com/2014/08/godus-review-not-godlike-yet-but.html)

  - The PC version had no real-money purchases after buying the game; gems came from sacrificing followers at a temple.

  - Source: [Tobold's comparative review](https://tobolds.blogspot.com/2014/08/godus-comparative-review.html)

### Inferences
- The core Populous verb (sculpt land so people can build) survived into Godus. The free-to-play layer then put time gates between that verb and its reward (timers on belief, wheat and expansion) and sold gems to bypass them. The 4x difference in wheat timers between PC and iPad shows that pacing was set by monetisation, not by design.
- Both Godus and Curiosity promised payouts or status (1% revenue, god of gods) tied to systems that were not built (multiplayer) and to a product that did not turn a profit. The promises were therefore structurally impossible to keep.

### Gaps
- *Curiosity* in-app chisel prices (the widely reported top-tier "diamond chisel" price) were not confirmed. Wikipedia lists pick types but no prices.
- I did not retrieve the original RPS, Eurogamer or Kickstarter pages (Kickstarter returned 403), so the reward tiers and full stretch-goal list are unverified.
- I found no Godus revenue, download or player-count figures.

## 9. Notable for Godling (short list; facts above, not a design proposal)

### Takeaway
Populous is the canonical proof that people watching an autonomous population will accept *indirect* influence: shaping terrain, one attractor, global conditions. Its mana bar is a clean model for tiered, refill-paced escalation of disasters. Godus shows what happens when paid currency starts buying outcomes and time-skips instead of conditions.

### Cited Findings
- The god never commands individuals. Influence is via terrain (about 80% of play), one Papal Magnet attractor and four global modes — see Sections 3 and 6 ([Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/bullfrog-time-machine-populous/); [GOG forum](https://gog.com/forum/populous_series/trying_to_understand_this_game_populous_1)).
- Powers sit on one bar in escalating order (terrain, then magnet, quake, swamp, knight, volcano, flood, Armageddon), gated by mana earned from population — [GOG forum](https://gog.com/forum/populous_series/trying_to_understand_this_game_populous_1); [Wikipedia: Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_(video_game)).
- End-forcing escalators were added late to break stalemates: knights, Armageddon, and Populous II's monsters at 75% map control — [Maher 2016](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/); [Wikipedia: Populous II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_II:_Trials_of_the_Olympian_Gods).
- Godus gems bought belief, resources and timer skips, and reviewers said it ruined the core verb — [Digitally Downloaded](https://www.digitallydownloaded.net/?p=47874); [Tobold](https://tobolds.blogspot.com/2014/08/godus-comparative-review.html).

### Inferences
- **Indirect levers translate directly**:
  - Viewers' credit-bought "conditions" (objects, weather, meteors) are Godling's equivalent of terrain and disasters.
  - Godling's own decisions are the walkers' "seek flat land, build the biggest thing that fits" rule.
  - Populous shows the audience will read emergent behaviour as the payoff, as long as the agent's response to changed conditions is legible.
- **Terrain quality to building ambition is a ready-made decision rule**. Populous's flat-land rule (small and fast vs large and slow, a 5x5 flat area for a castle) gives an agent a natural, explainable trade-off when viewers alter the site.
- **Tiered, refill-paced escalation**:
  - One bar, where position equals unlock threshold and price, and big events drain it.
  - This keeps disasters rare and earned.
  - Populous II's shared intervention caps and per-map caps on trees, rocks and walls are a precedent for limiting how many times viewers can spam the same event.
- **Stalemate breakers matter more in a 24/7 setting**. Populous needed knights and Armageddon because games stalled for hours. A never-ending stream instead needs periodic climaxes and recovery tools, such as Populous II's "plant flowers" repair and basalt as a counter to tidal waves, rather than a terminal end.
- **Hazard memory is where scripted agents look dumb**. Populous's AI did not anticipate floods and kept walking into a known swamp. An LLM agent that visibly remembers and adapts to recurring viewer-caused hazards would address the most-cited weakness of the original's AI.
- **Godus lessons**:
  1. Never let paid currency buy *outcomes or time-skips*. Godus's gems for instant belief and skipped timers were what critics condemned, while Godling's "conditions, not decisions" rule is the opposite.
  2. Do not let pacing differ by monetisation surface. Godus's iPad timers were 4x the PC ones.
  3. Never promise rewards or status that depend on unbuilt systems or on future revenue. The "god of gods" role needed multiplayer that was never built, and the 1% share depended on a profit that never came.
  4. Communicate continuously with high-stakes participants. Henderson emailed monthly for updates and was left without contact.

### Gaps
- None beyond those listed in the sections above. Exact Populous mana numbers would help calibrate an analogous credit or tier curve, but were not found.
