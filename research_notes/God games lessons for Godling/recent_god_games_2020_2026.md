# Recent god games and "watch autonomous inhabitants" sandboxes, 2020–2026 (state as of 2026-11-01)

Scope note: WorldBox, The Universim, Deisim, Reus 2, Populous, Godus, Godville, Black & White, RimWorld, Majesty, Dungeon Keeper, From Dust, The Sims and Dwarf Fortress are excluded (researched separately). Sales numbers from Raijin, Vaporlens, VG Insights, Gamalytic, SteamData.ai, GameRevenueData and similar trackers are **third-party estimates** (mostly derived from Steam review counts) and often disagree by 2–3x; they are labelled as such. Developer- or publisher-reported numbers are labelled "official". Review scores from Steam store fetches are as of 2026-11-01 unless dated otherwise.

## 1. Which god games launched or entered early access 2020–2026, and how did they do?

### Takeaway
The "pure" god game revival on Steam in this window is small and commercially modest: the three most visible new entries (Godsworn 2024, Fata Deum 2025, Masters of Albion 2026) are early-access titles in the low tens of thousands to low hundreds of thousands of copies, and two of the three sit at "Mixed" Steam ratings. The breakout hits that use god-game DNA (Cult of the Lamb, 7M copies) wrap follower management inside another, tighter genre.

### Cited Findings

**Godsworn (Thunderoak Interactive, self-published; PC; Early Access 28 Mar 2024)**
- Player-god: you pick a "divine hero" (Baltic pagan gods such as Saule, Meness, Ausrine vs. a crusader "Order" with Archangel Michael); an RTS with heroes, units and god powers — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1328990/Godsworn)
- God economy: "worshippers who pray generate faith", which pays for mythical creatures and god powers; workers respond automatically to global tasks and go back to praying/generating faith when idle — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1328990/Godsworn)
- Price €28.99 (about $30 in a US listing); Very Positive, 93% of 814 reviews (2026-11-01 fetch) — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1328990/Godsworn); EA launch date confirmed in the dev's announcement repost — [EVGA forum repost](https://forums.evga.com/Godsworn-a-Mythological-RTS-Out-Now-via-Early-Access-m3620729.aspx)
- Early-access length creep: the dev first estimated 1 year, then about 2 years — [Steam store, earlier snapshot via search](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1328990); the current store page says it now expects to leave EA in **Q2 2027 (~3 years)**, more than doubling content to 16 campaign missions (EA has 10 missions, 20 skirmish maps, 7 challenge maps) — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1328990/Godsworn)
- Players complained about limited content for the price (5-mission campaign at an earlier point) — [GamePressure review summary](https://www.gamepressure.com/games/godsworn/z668dd)
- Estimated gross revenue ~$574k (SteamData.ai, third-party estimate, undated) — [SteamData.ai](https://steamdata.ai/game/1328990)

### Inferences
- New dedicated god games in 2024–2026 have all chosen early access at €25–30 and struggle to hit "Very Positive" unless they are tightly scoped (Godsworn is an RTS with a clear mission structure; Fata Deum and Masters of Albion are open sandboxes and sit at Mixed).
- The two currently-most-discussed god games (Fata Deum, Masters of Albion) both use a **day/night split** (day = influence/economy, night = conflict/direction), and both name **mana** as the god's spend resource; Godsworn uses **faith generated by praying worshippers**. Faith-from-followers remains the standard god economy.
- Commercially, the god fantasy sells best when it is a layer inside a tight loop with a clear goal (Cult of the Lamb's roguelite + cult management; Wandering Village's survival on a creature), not as an open-ended "be god" sandbox.

### Takeaway
Hits share a clear loop, broad platforms, fast and steady free updates, and a fair one-time price (Cult of the Lamb, Wandering Village, Terra Nil, Norland's launch). Weak performers share long or slipping early access, thin content for a €25–30 price, technical problems, and open-ended sandboxes without clear goals (Creo, Fata Deum, Masters of Albion so far).

## 3. Cozy/toy sandboxes with a watchable, shareable quality: what made them break out?

### Takeaway
The breakouts (Townscaper, Tiny Glade, Islanders, Rusty's Retirement, Bongo Cat) are tiny teams making no-fail, instantly legible toys whose output looks good in a GIF or on a stream; virality came before launch (tweets, demos, wishlists). The newest wave is "ambient" software that runs at the bottom of the screen, and its huge concurrent-user numbers do not guarantee profit.

### Cited Findings
- **Tiny Glade** (Pounce Light, a 2-person Swedish studio; Steam 23/24 Sep 2024): 616,528 units and 34,496 DAU one month after launch (official, via GameDiscoverCo); 1.3M wishlists before launch per one report, 1.93M per another — [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20241120-tiny-glade-600k-sold-reason), [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/investment/item-45983); passed 1M wishlists by June 2024 — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/simulation/im-starting-to-think-people-like-cozy-games-a-little-steam-next-fest-darling-about-doodling-castles-whose-devs-thought-was-very-niche-hits-1-million-wishlists); 10,600 CCU at launch — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-45586); reasons given: viral early tweets, a demo, a Future Games Show trailer, streamer/influencer recommendations, a calm space with **no failure states**, "getting more with less effort"; buyers 32% US — [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20241120-tiny-glade-600k-sold-reason); devs "don't really want to have a roadmap" — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/city-builder/as-steams-cozy-new-city-builder-tiny-glade-celebrates-500-000-players-devs-explain-why-they-dont-really-want-to-have-a-roadmap); early VG Insights (26.9K) and Gamalytic (133.4K) estimates badly undercounted real sales — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/investment/item-45983)

### Inferences
- Shareability comes from small, legible, pretty outcomes per input (one click → a house appears), which clip well into short video; all of these are solo or 2-person teams.
- "Second-screen" or ambient presence (Rusty's Retirement, Bongo Cat, Fantasy Map Simulator wallpaper) is a proven 2024–2026 behaviour: people leave a gentle simulation running all day. Free-to-play ambient toys can reach huge CCU but monetize poorly (Bongo Cat).
- Pre-launch virality (wishlists in the millions for Tiny Glade) predicted success better than tracker estimates did.

### Gaps
- Launch prices for Tiny Glade, Townscaper and Rusty's Retirement were not captured from a cited source in this pass.
- No lifetime Tiny Glade total after Oct 2024 was found (no source confirms 1M+).

## 4. Games and experiments built around LLM/AI-driven autonomous characters, 2023–2026

### Takeaway
The research demos (Smallville 2023, Project Sid 2024, AIvilization 2025) showed believable emergent social behaviour but at high compute cost and without a lasting product; the startup behind Project Sid pivoted to spreadsheets. Commercial LLM-NPC games are mostly small (tens of thousands of copies at best). The largest "watch an AI" audiences are Twitch personalities (Neuro-sama) and AI-plays streams, and the main 24/7-stream failure is unmoderated output (Nothing, Forever's ban). Costs push developers toward token caps, subscriptions, bring-your-own-key or local models.

### Cited Findings

**Research and open-source**
- **Generative Agents / "Smallville"** (Park et al., April 2023, arXiv 2304.03442): 25 agents on gpt-3.5-turbo; simulating 25 agents for two game days was "costing thousands of dollars in token credits and taking multiple days to complete"; knowledge of Sam's mayoral candidacy spread from 1 agent (4%) to 8 (32%) and of Isabella's Valentine's party from 1 (4%) to 13 (52%); 5 of 12 invitees showed up; relationship network density rose 0.167 → 0.74; the full architecture was rated more believable (TrueSkill μ=29.89) than human crowdworkers role-playing the agents (μ=22.95) — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)
- A community fork reports a full in-game day for 25 agents on gpt-3.5-turbo-0125 costing ~$18.5 (2024 pricing), showing cost falls quickly with cheaper models — [GitHub fork](https://github.com/drudilorenzo/generative_agents)
- **AI Town** (a16z-infra + Convex, Aug 2023): MIT-licensed starter kit inspired by Smallville; 5K+ GitHub stars soon after launch — [Convex Stack](https://stack.convex.dev/ai-town-v2); ~8.6K stars in an undated snapshot — [SourcePulse](https://www.sourcepulse.org/projects/1323585); [repo](https://github.com/a16z-infra/ai-town/)

### Inferences
- Emergent-society results (party diffusion, religion, taxes) are strong headlines, but no LLM colony sim has yet become a commercial hit; the projects with the most reach are free/web (Infinite Craft, AIvilization) or streams (Neuro-sama).
- LLM cost is the defining business constraint. Every commercial model seen here passes it to the player or caps it: token packs (Suck Up!), subscription (AI People $10/month), BYOK (AI Cultivation World Simulator), on-device small models (inZOI, AI People local mode). VoxelMind ending its hosted service shows the cost of running agents for others.
- Audiences reward an AI *personality* (Neuro-sama) and legible thinking (Claude Plays Pokémon's visible reasoning, VoxelMind's readable thoughts, inZOI's thought bubbles) more than raw agent count.
- One unmoderated output can take down a 24/7 AI stream (Nothing, Forever).

### Gaps
- No reliable engagement or retention data was found for AI People, AIvilization (final numbers), Project Sid follow-ups, or VoxelMind beyond its own site.
- No dollar figures for Infinite Craft's inference costs.
- Inworld-powered shipped titles and their sales were not enumerated (search returned corporate news only).
- No verified LLM-driven *god game* (player is a god, villagers are LLM agents) has shipped commercially on Steam as of 2026-11-01; AI Cultivation World Simulator and VoxelMind are the closest, both unreleased or unavailable.

## 5. Common success factors and failure modes

### Takeaway
Success: clear core loop or no-fail toy design, a legible and attractive moment-to-moment show, pre-launch virality, fair one-time pricing, frequent free updates and multi-platform reach. Failure: long or slipping early access, thin content at a premium price, performance problems, open sandboxes with unclear goals, nostalgia overpromise, and for AI games, uncontrolled inference cost and unmoderated output.

### Cited Findings
- Long and slipping early access: Godsworn 1 → 2 → ~3 years — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1328990/Godsworn); Creo 2019 → Jan 2025 ending "Mixed" — [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/all/creo-god-simulator-review-ambitious-ideas-prosaic-divinity/); Fata Deum planned ~1 year from Sep 2025 and still listed as EA at 2026-11-01 — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1330360/Fata_Deum__The_God_Sim/)
- Thin content for the price — Godsworn players — [GamePressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/games/godsworn/z668dd); inZOI's thin careers and no pets at launch — [The Games Wiki](https://thegameswiki.com/inzoi/wiki/reception-and-reviews)
- Performance and rough interaction — Masters of Albion — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/masters-of-albion-early-access-mixed-reviews/), [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/masters-of-albion/early-access-review)
- Too little emergent life or environmental drama — Creo ("weak environmental events", "locusts eat your crops while you can only watch") — [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/all/creo-god-simulator-review-ambitious-ideas-prosaic-divinity/)
- Steady updates and new platforms keep sales growing years after launch — Cult of the Lamb 1M → 7M — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_of_the_Lamb)
- No-fail, low-effort creation plus pre-launch virality — Tiny Glade — [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20241120-tiny-glade-600k-sold-reason)
- Ambient "leave it running" play — Rusty's Retirement, Bongo Cat, Fantasy Map Simulator — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/simulation/after-selling-100000-copies-of-its-idle-stardew-valley-like-rustys-retirement-dev-celebrates-spawning-a-genre-of-games-that-run-at-the-bottom-of-your-screen), [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/life-sim/bongo-cat-still-one-of-the-biggest-games-on-steam-gets-even-bigger-with-multiplayer-you-can-now-have-up-to-100-cats-on-your-screen-at-once-all-of-them-happily-bapping-away-as-their-owners-type); popularity ≠ profit for free ambient toys — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-48117)
- Monetization friction for AI games: Suck Up!'s token hard cap — [The Magic Rain](https://themagicrain.com/?p=42121); AI People's subscription — [GoodAI](https://goodai.com/ai-people-alpha-officially-released); BYOK spending warnings — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4443180/AI__AI_Cultivation_World_Simulator/); player divide over AI use in games — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Steam-launch-New-interactive-fiction-game-debuts-to-Very-Positive-reviews-may-divide-gamers-over-heavy-AI-integration.1087821.0.html)
- Moderation failure on 24/7 AI streams — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/twitch-temporary-ban-seinfeld-parody-ai-transphobic-remarks-rcna69389)
- Agent weaknesses in embodied tasks (spatial awareness, physical coordination) even in the best-funded experiment — [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/computing/artificial-intelligence/ai-characters-find-religion-in-minecraft)

### Inferences
- A "watch it live" product competes with Bongo Cat and Rusty's Retirement for idle attention, and on Twitch with Neuro-sama for affection. Legibility (seeing what the character thinks and why) is a recurring hook across successful AI showcases.
- The god-economy pattern (faith from followers → mana → powers, often split by day/night) is well understood by players; novelty in this window has come from wrapping it in other genres or making the inhabitants' minds visible, not from new god-power economies.

### Notable for Godling
- **Viewer-as-god, not puppeteer, has precedent**: AI Cultivation World Simulator's "tribulations / alter a heart / change history" interventions on autonomous LLM agents and VoxelMind's weather/resource/winter sliders are the closest analogues to "change conditions, never buy decisions" — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4443180/AI__AI_Cultivation_World_Simulator/), [VoxelMind](https://voxel-mind.com/)
- **Show the mind**: readable thoughts (VoxelMind, inZOI Smart Zoi, Claude Plays Pokémon reasoning) are the main draw in AI-agent showcases.
- **Memory artefacts**: VoxelMind villagers writing books to get around short memory is a visible, story-making mechanic for an LLM builder — [VoxelMind](https://voxel-mind.com/)
- **Day/night rhythm and a mana-like spend meter** are what players expect from current god games (Fata Deum, Masters of Albion); Godsworn's "idle followers generate faith" maps onto passive credit accrual.
- **Moderation is existential for 24/7 AI streams** (Nothing, Forever's 14-day ban).
- **Cost discipline**: Smallville's 2-day run cost "thousands of dollars" in 2023, while a later fork ran a day for ~$18.5 on cheaper models; every commercial LLM game caps or passes on inference cost; VoxelMind shut its hosted service.
- **Ambient, phone-friendly watching works** (Rusty's Retirement, Bongo Cat, Fantasy Map Simulator wallpaper), but free ambient toys can fail to profit.
- **Emergent-event headlines travel** (Smallville's Valentine's party, Project Sid's religion and taxes); a single builder on an island needs equivalent, clip-able "moments".
- **Avoid slipping roadmaps and nostalgia overpromise**: Godsworn's 1→3-year EA and Masters of Albion's Mixed launch.

### Gaps
- No systematic data (e.g. Gamalytic tag-level medians) to quantify how early-access length correlates with outcome for god games specifically; the patterns above are drawn from individual cases.
